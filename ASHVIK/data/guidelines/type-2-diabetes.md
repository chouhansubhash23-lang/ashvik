# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

> **Source:** ICMR Standard Treatment Workflows Vol. 3 (2022); NPCDCS
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- India has a very high diabetes burden; many cases are diagnosed late.
- Diagnostic criteria: fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL, 2-h OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dL, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, or random ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms.
- Target HbA1c: < 7% for most adults (individualise in elderly, comorbidities).
- Screen for complications: eyes, feet, kidneys, heart.

## 2. Clinical features

Often asymptomatic. Look for:
- Polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss
- Fatigue, recurrent infections
- Blurred vision
- Delayed wound healing
- Neuropathy (burning feet, numbness)
- Acanthosis nigricans (insulin resistance)

## 3. Diagnosis and monitoring

| Test | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Fasting glucose / HbA1c | At diagnosis and every 3 months |
| BP | Every visit |
| Weight/BMI | Every visit |
| Urine albumin/creatinine | Yearly |
| Lipid profile | Yearly |
| Fundus exam | Yearly |
| Foot exam | Every visit |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Lifestyle (all patients)

- Medical nutrition therapy; reduce refined carbs/sugar
- 150 min/week moderate exercise
- Weight loss 5–10% if overweight
- Stop tobacco; limit alcohol
- Diabetes education and self-monitoring

### 4.2 Pharmacotherapy

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metformin | Glycomet, Glucophage | 500 mg OD/BD, titrate to 1 g BD | First-line; take after food |
| Glimepiride | Amaryl | 1–2 mg OD | Hypoglycaemia risk; before breakfast |
| Sitagliptin | Januvia | 100 mg OD | Weight neutral; renal dose adjustment |
| Dapagliflozin | Forxiga | 10 mg OD | Genital infections, dehydration; renal/heart benefit |
| Insulin (basal) | Human Actrapid, Mixtard, Lantus | Individualised | If uncontrolled or symptomatic |

### 4.3 When to start insulin

- HbA1c > 9–10% with symptoms
- Catabolic features (weight loss)
- Acute illness, surgery, pregnancy
- Failure of oral therapy

## 5. Complications screening

| Complication | Screen |
|---|---|
| Retinopathy | Annual fundus |
| Nephropathy | Urine albumin, creatinine |
| Neuropathy | Foot exam, monofilament |
| Cardiovascular | BP, lipids, ECG |
| Diabetic foot | Inspect feet every visit |

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Metformin:** hold before contrast imaging and in acute illness (lactic acidosis risk); avoid in eGFR < 30; GI upset common; B12 deficiency with long use.
- **Glimepiride:** hypoglycaemia with skipped meals, alcohol, other antidiabetics; caution in elderly/renal impairment.
- **Sitagliptin:** pancreatitis (rare), renal dose adjustment; may interact with digoxin.
- **Dapagliflozin:** genital mycotic infections, volume depletion, euglycaemic DKA; avoid in recurrent UTIs; not with loop diuretics in frail elderly.
- **Insulin:** hypoglycaemia; rotate injection sites; storage (2–8 °C).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hypoglycaemia (sweating, confusion, seizures)
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (vomiting, abdominal pain, deep breathing, fruity breath)
- Hyperosmolar state (severe dehydration, confusion)
- Foot ulcer with infection/gangrene
- Sudden vision loss
- Chest pain / stroke symptoms
- Pregnancy with uncontrolled diabetes

## 8. Follow-up

- Every 3 months until controlled, then 3–6 monthly.
- HbA1c every 3 months.
- Annual eye, kidney, foot, and lipid screening.
- Reinforce adherence and lifestyle at every visit.
