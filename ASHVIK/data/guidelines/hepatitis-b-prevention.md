# Hepatitis B Prevention

> **Source:** WHO; NHP; National Viral Hepatitis Programme
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Hepatitis B is vaccine-preventable.
- Birth dose within 24 hours.
- Screen pregnant women (HBsAg).
- Post-exposure prophylaxis for non-immune.

## 2. Clinical features

- Often asymptomatic
- Jaundice, fatigue
- Chronic liver disease

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| HBsAg | Screening |
| Anti-HBs | Immunity |
| HBV DNA | Viral load |
| LFT | Liver injury |

## 4. Treatment / prevention

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Vaccine | 0, 1, 6 months |
| Birth dose | Within 24 h |
| HBIG | Newborns of HBsAg+ mothers |
| Screening | Pregnant women |
| Paracetamol 500 mg | For pain/fever |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Engerix-B; HBIG.

## 5. Supportive care

- Safe injections
- Condoms
- Avoid sharing needles
- Counselling

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Vaccine:** local pain/fever.
- **HBIG:** allergy.
- **Paracetamol:** hepatotoxicity in liver disease.
- **Antivirals:** specialist.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Acute liver failure
- Jaundice with coagulopathy
- Pregnancy with high viral load

## 8. Follow-up

- Anti-HBs titers.
- Liver function.
- Vaccination of contacts.
