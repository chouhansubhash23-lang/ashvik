# Depression and Anxiety

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); National Mental Health Programme; WHO mhGAP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Depression and anxiety are common, treatable, and often present with physical symptoms in rural settings.
- Screen with PHQ-9 (depression) and GAD-7 (anxiety) where available.
- Always assess **suicide risk**.
- Rule out medical causes: anaemia, hypothyroidism, diabetes, substance use, medications.

## 2. Clinical features

### Depression
- Low mood most of the day, nearly every day
- Loss of interest/pleasure
- Fatigue, sleep and appetite change
- Worthlessness, guilt, poor concentration
- Suicidal thoughts

### Anxiety
- Excessive worry, restlessness
- Palpitations, sweating, tremor
- Breathlessness, chest tightness
- Avoidance behaviour, sleep disturbance

## 3. Diagnosis

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| PHQ-9 | Depression severity |
| GAD-7 | Anxiety severity |
| Clinical interview | Diagnosis and risk assessment |
| CBC, TSH, glucose | Rule out medical causes |
| Substance use history | Alcohol, tobacco, drugs |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Depression

| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild | Psychoeducation, counselling, exercise, sleep hygiene; review in 2 weeks |
| Moderate | SSRI + counselling |
| Severe / suicidal | Urgent referral; consider psychiatric admission |

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sertraline | Daxid, Zosert | 50 mg once daily | Start low; review at 2–4 weeks |
| Escitalopram | Nexito | 10 mg once daily | QT caution |
| Fluoxetine | Fludac, Prozac | 20 mg once daily | Long half-life |

Continue treatment for at least 6–12 months after remission.

### 4.2 Anxiety

| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild | Counselling, breathing exercises, sleep hygiene |
| Moderate-severe | SSRI (sertraline/escitalopram) + CBT |
| Severe/panic | Short-term benzodiazepine only if needed; refer |

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sertraline | Daxid | 50 mg once daily | First-line |
| Escitalopram | Nexito | 10 mg once daily | First-line |
| Lorazepam | Ativan, Lorazep | 1–2 mg as needed | Short-term only; dependence risk |

**Avoid long-term benzodiazepines.** Taper slowly if already dependent.

## 5. Supportive care

- Psychoeducation: condition is treatable, not a weakness
- Sleep hygiene, regular exercise, structured daily routine
- Family involvement and stigma reduction
- Alcohol/tobacco cessation
- Follow-up and safety planning

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **SSRIs:** serotonin syndrome with triptans, MAOIs, linezolid, tramadol; bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants; hyponatraemia in elderly; sexual dysfunction; discontinuation syndrome.
- **Escitalopram:** QT prolongation — caution with other QT drugs.
- **Benzodiazepines:** sedation, falls, dependence; avoid with alcohol/opioids; respiratory depression.
- **General:** do not stop antidepressants abruptly; taper.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Suicidal ideation or plan
- Self-harm
- Psychosis (hallucinations, delusions)
- Severe agitation or aggression
- Mania (bipolar disorder)
- Catatonia, refusal to eat/drink
- Postpartum psychosis
- Substance withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines)
- No improvement after 4–6 weeks

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 1–2 weeks initially, then monthly.
- Assess suicide risk at every visit.
- Monitor adherence and side effects.
- Continue treatment for at least 6–12 months after remission.
- Involve family and community support.
