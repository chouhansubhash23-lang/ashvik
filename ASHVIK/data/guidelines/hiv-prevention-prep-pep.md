# HIV Prevention: PrEP and PEP

> **Source:** NACO; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- PrEP prevents acquisition in high-risk HIV-negative people.
- PEP prevents infection after exposure.
- **PEP within 72 hours** (best < 2 h).
- Adherence is critical.

## 2. Indications

| PrEP | PEP |
|---|---|
| High-risk sexual exposure | Needlestick |
| Serodiscordant couple | Sexual assault |
| Injecting drug use | Occupational exposure |
| — | Condom failure |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| HIV test | Negative before PrEP |
| Renal function | TDF safety |
| Pregnancy test | Drug choice |
| Source HIV status | PEP decision |

## 4. Treatment

| Regimen | Detail |
|---|---|
| PrEP | TDF/FTC daily |
| PEP | TDF/FTC + DTG x 28 days |
| Adherence | Essential |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Tenofovir; Lamivudine; Dolutegravir.

## 5. Supportive care

- Counselling
- Paracetamol 500 mg for associated pain/fever
- Condoms
- Regular HIV testing
- STI screening

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **TDF:** kidney toxicity; monitor.
- **DTG:** insomnia; interactions with antacids.
- **PEP:** start within 72 h.
- **Adherence:** resistance risk.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- HIV-positive test
- Renal impairment
- Pregnancy
- High-risk exposure

## 8. Follow-up

- HIV testing every 3 months (PrEP).
- PEP follow-up at 6 weeks/3 months.
- Adherence support.
