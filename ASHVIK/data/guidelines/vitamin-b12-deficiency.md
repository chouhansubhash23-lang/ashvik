# Vitamin B12 Deficiency

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common in vegetarians, elderly, and metformin users.
- Causes anaemia and neuropathy.
- Treat with intramuscular or high-dose oral B12.
- Find the cause (pernicious anaemia, malabsorption).

## 2. Clinical features

- Fatigue, pallor
- Tingling/numbness
- Glossitis
- Memory problems
- Gait imbalance

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Serum B12 | Deficiency |
| CBC (MCV) | Macrocytosis |
| MMA/homocysteine | Sensitive (if available) |
| Intrinsic factor antibody | Pernicious anaemia |
| Endoscopy | Malabsorption |

## 4. Treatment

| Regimen | Dose |
|---|---|
| IM | Cyanocobalamin 1000 mcg daily x 1 week, then weekly x 4, then monthly |
| Oral | 1000–2000 mcg daily |
| Maintenance | 1000 mcg monthly or oral daily |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Nurokind; Methylcobalamin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Diet (animal products/fortified)
- Paracetamol 500 mg for associated pain
- Treat cause
- Monitor neuro symptoms

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Metformin/PPIs:** reduce B12.
- **Folate:** may mask B12 deficiency.
- **Iron:** separate.
- **Neurological symptoms:** treat promptly.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Neurological deficit
- Severe anaemia
- Heart failure
- Suspected pernicious anaemia

## 8. Follow-up

- Recheck B12.
- Neurological assessment.
- Lifelong therapy if pernicious anaemia.
