# Disaster Triage

> **Source:** WHO; NDMA; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Triage sorts casualties by urgency when resources are limited.
- Use a simple colour system (Red/Yellow/Green/Black).
- Reassess regularly.
- Document and communicate.

## 2. Triage categories

| Colour | Priority | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Immediate | Life-threatening, treat now |
| Yellow | Delayed | Serious, can wait |
| Green | Minor | Walking wounded |
| Black | Expectant | Dead/unsurvivable |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Airway/breathing/circulation
- Consciousness
- Haemorrhage
- Burns/trauma
- Time-critical conditions

## 4. Treatment

| Priority | Example |
|---|---|
| Red | Tension pneumothorax, severe bleeding, airway obstruction |
| Yellow | Fractures, moderate burns |
| Green | Minor wounds |
| Black | Cardiac arrest (in mass casualty) |

- **Paracetamol 500 mg** for minor pain.
- **Morphine 5–10 mg** for severe pain (if available).

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650; Morphine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Keep warm
- Hydration
- Psychological first aid
- Family reunification

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Opioids:** respiratory depression.
- **Sedatives:** avoid.
- **Antibiotics:** for open wounds.
- **Resources:** allocate to salvageable patients.

## 7. Red flags — escalate

- Mass casualty incident
- Resource shortage
- Contaminated scene
- Security threat

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess triage.
- Document.
- Debrief.
