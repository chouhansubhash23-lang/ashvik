# Pressure Ulcers (Bedsores)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NHP; NPUAP/EPUAP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Pressure ulcers occur over bony prominences in immobile patients.
- **Prevention is easier than treatment.**
- Reposition every 2 hours; use pressure-relieving surfaces.
- Infection and sepsis are major complications.

## 2. Clinical features and staging

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| 1 | Non-blanchable erythema, intact skin |
| 2 | Partial-thickness loss (blister/superficial ulcer) |
| 3 | Full-thickness loss (subcutaneous fat visible) |
| 4 | Full-thickness with bone/tendon/muscle exposure |
| Unstageable | Covered by slough/eschar |
| Deep tissue injury | Purple/maroon area |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical exam | Stage and location |
| Wound culture | Infection |
| X-ray/MRI | Osteomyelitis (deep ulcers) |
| Albumin | Nutrition |
| CBC/CRP | Infection |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Prevention

- Reposition every 2 h; 30° tilt.
- Pressure-relieving mattress/cushions.
- Keep skin clean and dry.
- Nutrition (protein, calories).
- Avoid friction/shearing.

### 4.2 Wound care

| Stage | Management |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Protective dressing, relieve pressure |
| 3–4 | Debridement, moist dressing, specialist referral |
| Infected | Clean, drain, antibiotics |
| Necrotic | Debridement (sharp/enzymatic) |

### 4.3 Infection

- Superficial: topical antiseptic (cautious) + dressing.
- Deep/spreading: systemic antibiotics (amoxicillin-clavulanate or as per culture).
- Osteomyelitis: prolonged antibiotics + surgical referral.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Amoxicillin-clavulanate — Augmentin; Silver sulfadiazine — Silvadene.

## 5. Supportive care

- Nutrition: protein 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day if tolerated.
- Hydration.
- Pain control (paracetamol 500 mg three times daily).
- Treat incontinence.
- Caregiver training.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Antibiotics:** renal dose adjustment; *C. difficile*.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in frail elderly.
- **Opioids:** sedation; constipation.
- **Topical antiseptics:** may impair healing; use judiciously.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Stage 3/4 ulcer
- Signs of sepsis
- Osteomyelitis
- Rapidly spreading infection
- Necrotising fasciitis
- Uncontrolled pain
- Malnutrition

## 8. Follow-up

- Review dressing every 1–3 days.
- Reassess stage and nutrition.
- Pressure-relief audit.
- Refer to wound care/plastic surgery if needed.
