# Skin and Soft Tissue Infections

> **Source:** ICMR antimicrobial use guidelines; NCDC; National Health Portal
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Includes cellulitis, erysipelas, abscess, folliculitis, impetigo, and infected wounds.
- *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Streptococcus pyogenes* are the commonest organisms.
- Diabetes, poor hygiene, trauma, and injection drug use increase risk.
- Abscess needs **drainage**, not just antibiotics.

## 2. Clinical features

| Condition | Features |
|---|---|
| Cellulitis | Spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pain, fever |
| Erysipelas | Raised, well-demarcated red plaque (face/leg) |
| Abscess | Tender fluctuant swelling, pus, surrounding cellulitis |
| Folliculitis | Pus-filled bumps around hair follicles |
| Impetigo | Honey-coloured crusts (children) |
| Infected wound | Pus, pain, redness, delayed healing |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical exam | Usually sufficient |
| Pus culture | Recurrent/severe infection, diabetes |
| Blood culture | Systemic signs |
| Blood sugar | All patients with severe skin infection |
| Ultrasound | Deep abscess |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Cellulitis / erysipelas (adult)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cephalexin | Sporidex | 500 mg four times daily | 5–7 days |
| Amoxicillin-clavulanate | Augmentin, Clavam | 625 mg three times daily | 5–7 days |
| Clindamycin (penicillin allergy) | Dalacin | 300–450 mg three times daily | 5–7 days |

Mark the border of redness and reassess in 24–48 h. Elevate the limb.

### 4.2 Abscess

- **Incision and drainage** is the primary treatment.
- Antibiotics if surrounding cellulitis, systemic signs, diabetes, or immunocompromise.
- Pack and review in 48 h.

### 4.3 Impetigo / folliculitis

| Condition | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Impetigo (limited) | Mupirocin 2% ointment three times daily x 5–7 days |
| Impetigo (extensive) | Oral cephalexin or amoxicillin-clavulanate |
| Folliculitis | Topical clindamycin/benzoyl peroxide; hygiene |

**Brand examples:** Mupirocin — T-Bact; Clindamycin — Dalacin.

### 4.4 Severe / necrotising infection

- IV antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem + clindamycin) and **urgent surgical referral**.
- Necrotising fasciitis is a surgical emergency.

## 5. Supportive care

- Elevate and rest the affected limb.
- Wound cleaning and dressing.
- Pain relief (paracetamol).
- Control diabetes.
- Tetanus prophylaxis for wounds.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Cephalexin:** rash, GI upset; caution in penicillin allergy (cross-reactivity low).
- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate:** diarrhoea, cholestatic jaundice; take with food.
- **Clindamycin:** *C. difficile* colitis; stop if severe diarrhoea.
- **Mupirocin:** local irritation; avoid eyes.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in cellulitis with kidney injury.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Rapidly spreading redness, pain out of proportion, crepitus, skin necrosis (necrotising fasciitis)
- Fever with hypotension
- Abscess in face/neck/hand
- Diabetic foot with deep infection/gangrene
- Orbital/periorbital cellulitis
- Infection in an immunocompromised patient
- No improvement after 48–72 h

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24–48 h for cellulitis and abscess.
- Complete antibiotics; do not stop early.
- Diabetes control and foot care.
- Hygiene and wound care education.
