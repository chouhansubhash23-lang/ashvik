# Hand Hygiene and Infection Control

> **Source:** WHO hand hygiene; NCDC; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Hand hygiene is the single most effective infection control measure.
- Follow the WHO **5 moments** for hand hygiene.
- Use alcohol-based hand rub (ABHR) or soap and water.
- Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Observe hand hygiene compliance.
- Check availability of ABHR/soap.
- Review infection rates.
- Assess isolation practices.

## 3. Treatment: hand hygiene practice

| Moment | When |
|---|---|
| 1 | Before touching patient |
| 2 | Before clean/aseptic procedure |
| 3 | After body fluid exposure |
| 4 | After touching patient |
| 5 | After touching patient surroundings |

- ABHR: apply **3 mL**, rub 20–30 seconds.
- Soap and water: 40–60 seconds.
- Use **70% alcohol** ABHR.

## 4. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dose:** not applicable (non-drug topic).
- **Alcohol-based rub:** flammable; avoid near cautery.
- **Chlorhexidine:** avoid eyes/ears; allergy.
- **Soap:** may dry skin; use moisturiser.

## 5. Supportive care

- Provide ABHR at point of care.
- Train and audit.
- Display posters.
- Feedback to staff.

## 6. Red flags

- Outbreaks of healthcare-associated infection
- Poor compliance
- No ABHR available
- Glove misuse

## 7. Follow-up

- Monthly audits.
- Hand hygiene training.
- Track infection rates.
