# Joint Aspiration and Injection

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); ACR; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Joint aspiration diagnoses septic arthritis, gout, and hemarthrosis.
- Injection delivers steroid/local anaesthetic for inflammatory arthritis.
- **Aseptic technique is mandatory.**
- Avoid injecting through infected skin.

## 2. Indications

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| Aspiration | Suspected septic arthritis, gout, effusion |
| Injection | Inflammatory arthritis, bursitis, OA flare |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Joint swelling, warmth, range of motion
- Fever/systemic signs
- Coagulation status
- Skin infection over site
- Consent

## 4. Treatment: technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Aseptic technique, gloves |
| 2 | Mark landmarks |
| 3 | Local anaesthesia (lidocaine 1%) |
| 4 | Aspirate/inject |
| 5 | Send fluid for cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals |
| 6 | Dressing and rest |

### Common sites

| Joint | Approach |
|---|---|
| Knee | Medial/lateral parap patellar |
| Shoulder | Posterior |
| Elbow | Lateral |
| Ankle | Anteromedial |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Lidocaine — Xylocaine; Triamcinolone — Kenacort; Methylprednisolone — Depo-Medrol.

## 5. Supportive care

- Rest and ice
- Analgesia (paracetamol 500 mg)
- Monitor for infection

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Steroid injection:** infection risk, hyperglycaemia, skin atrophy; avoid repeated frequent injections.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk.
- **Lidocaine:** max dose.
- **Septic arthritis:** do not inject steroid.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Suspected septic arthritis
- Fever with hot joint
- Coagulopathy
- Prosthetic joint
- No improvement after aspiration

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24–48 h.
- Fluid results.
- Orthopedic referral if septic.
