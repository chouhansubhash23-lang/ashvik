# Cervical Cancer Screening

> **Source:** National Cancer Control Programme; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Cervical cancer is preventable through screening and HPV vaccination.
- Screen women 30–65 years (or as per national policy).
- VIA (visual inspection with acetic acid) is feasible at PHC.
- Refer abnormal results for colposcopy.

## 2. Clinical features

- Often asymptomatic
- Postcoital bleeding
- Intermenstrual bleeding
- Foul discharge
- Pelvic pain

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| VIA | Screening at PHC |
| Pap smear | Cytology |
| HPV test | High-risk HPV |
| Colposcopy/biopsy | Abnormal screen |

## 4. Treatment

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Normal | Repeat screening |
| Abnormal VIA/Pap | Refer colposcopy |
| Precancer | Cryotherapy/LEEP |
| Cancer | Refer oncology |

- **Paracetamol 500 mg** for procedure pain.
- HPV vaccination for eligible girls.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650; HPV vaccine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Counselling
- Hygiene
- HPV vaccination
- Follow-up

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Acetic acid:** avoid eyes.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding with biopsy.
- **Pregnancy:** screen postpartum.
- **Infection:** treat before screening.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Postcoital bleeding
- Abnormal VIA
- Suspicious cervix
- Pelvic mass
- Weight loss

## 8. Follow-up

- Repeat screening as per policy.
- Colposcopy follow-up.
- HPV vaccination.
