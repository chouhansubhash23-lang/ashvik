# Burns

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Burns are a major cause of trauma in rural India (flame, scald, electrical, chemical).
- **Cool running water for 20 minutes** is the first aid.
- Estimate burn surface area (Wallace rule of nines).
- Refer major burns, inhalation injury, and special sites.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Area | % TBSA (adult) |
|---|---|
| Head/neck | 9 |
| Each arm | 9 |
| Each leg | 18 |
| Anterior trunk | 18 |
| Posterior trunk | 18 |
| Perineum | 1 |

- **Major burn:** > 20% TBSA adult, > 10% child, or any inhalation/special site.
- Depth: superficial (erythema), partial (blisters), full (white/charred, painless).

## 3. Clinical features

- Pain, redness, blisters
- Charred/leathery skin
- Singed nasal hair, hoarseness, stridor (inhalation)
- Cough, carbonaceous sputum
- Shock (tachycardia, hypotension)
- Circumferential burns (compartment syndrome)

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 First aid

- Remove clothing/jewellery.
- Cool with running water 20 min (avoid ice).
- Cover with clean dry sheet.
- Do not apply toothpaste/oil.
- IV fluids for major burns.

### 4.2 Fluid resuscitation (Parkland)

- 4 mL × body weight (kg) × % TBSA in 24 h (Ringer's lactate).
- Half in first 8 h from injury; rest over 16 h.
- Monitor urine output (0.5–1 mL/kg/h).

### 4.3 Wound care

- Clean, debride, dressing.
- Silver sulfadiazine or appropriate dressing.
- Tetanus prophylaxis.
- Analgesia.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Silver sulfadiazine — Silvadene; Paracetamol — Dolo 650.

### 4.4 Referral

- Major burns, inhalation injury, face/hands/genitals, circumferential burns, chemical/electrical burns, children/elderly.

## 5. Supportive care

- Oxygen
- Warm environment
- Nutrition (high protein/calories)
- Infection control
- Pain management

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **NSAIDs:** avoid in kidney injury/hypovolaemia.
- **Opioids:** monitor breathing.
- **Silver sulfadiazine:** avoid in G6PD deficiency, pregnancy, near eyes.
- **Antibiotics:** not routine; use for infection.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- > 20% TBSA (adult) or > 10% (child)
- Inhalation injury (stridor, hoarseness)
- Face/hands/genitals/perineum
- Circumferential burns
- Electrical/chemical burns
- Shock
- Children/elderly/pregnancy

## 8. Follow-up

- Wound review every 2–3 days.
- Physiotherapy/scar care.
- Nutrition and rehabilitation.
