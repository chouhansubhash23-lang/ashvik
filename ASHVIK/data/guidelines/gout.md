# Gout and Hyperuricaemia

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); ACR/EULAR guidance; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Gout = monosodium urate crystal arthritis; sudden severe monoarthritis.
- Risk: male, obesity, alcohol, high-purine diet, kidney disease, diuretics.
- Treat the acute attack first; urate-lowering therapy is long-term.
- Do not start/stop allopurinol abruptly during an attack without cover.

## 2. Clinical features

- Sudden severe pain, swelling, redness of one joint (big toe, ankle, knee)
- Peak in 24 hours; exquisite tenderness
- Fever may occur
- Tophi (chronic), kidney stones
- Asymptomatic hyperuricaemia is common — not always treated.

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Joint aspiration + polarised microscopy | Gold standard (needle-shaped crystals) |
| Serum uric acid | Supportive; may be normal during attack |
| CBC, CRP, creatinine | Inflammation, kidney function |
| X-ray | Erosions in chronic gout |
| Ultrasound | Tophi, stones |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Acute attack

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSAID (e.g., indomethacin) | Indocap | 50 mg three times daily | 3–5 days |
| Naproxen | Naprosyn | 500 mg twice daily | 3–5 days |
| Colchicine | Colchicine | 0.5 mg twice daily | 3–5 days |
| Prednisolone | Wysolone | 30–35 mg daily | 5 days (if NSAID contraindicated) |

Ice, rest, elevate the joint. Avoid aspirin (raises urate).

### 4.2 Urate-lowering therapy (ULT)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allopurinol | Zyloric | 100 mg daily, titrate to 300 mg | Start low; target urate < 6 mg/dL |
| Febuxostat | Feburic | 40 mg daily | Alternative; caution in CVD |

Start ULT after the acute attack settles; cover with colchicine/NSAID for 3–6 months.

### 4.3 Lifestyle

- Weight loss
- Reduce alcohol (especially beer)
- Reduce purine-rich food (red meat, seafood, organ meat)
- Hydration
- Avoid thiazides/loop diuretics if possible

## 5. Supportive care

- Analgesia
- Joint rest
- Treat hypertension, diabetes, kidney disease
- Screen for stones

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury; avoid in CKD.
- **Colchicine:** diarrhoea, myopathy; reduce dose in renal/hepatic impairment; interacts with clarithromycin, statins.
- **Allopurinol:** rash (SJS), hypersensitivity; reduce dose in CKD; interacts with azathioprine/mercaptopurine (serious).
- **Febuxostat:** cardiovascular risk; avoid in ischemic heart disease.
- **Diuretics:** raise urate.
- **Aspirin:** low-dose may raise urate; avoid high-dose.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Septic arthritis (fever, hot joint) — aspirate
- Suspected SJS (rash, mucosal involvement) after allopurinol
- Severe kidney injury
- Tophaceous gout with infection
- Uncontrolled pain

## 8. Follow-up

- Recheck urate in 4–6 weeks; target < 6 mg/dL.
- Continue ULT long-term; do not stop.
- Monitor renal function and LFT.
- Lifestyle counselling.
