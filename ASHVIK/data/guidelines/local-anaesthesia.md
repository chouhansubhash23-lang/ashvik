# Local Anaesthesia

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Lidocaine is the most commonly used local anaesthetic.
- **Always aspirate** before injecting to avoid intravascular injection.
- Respect maximum dose: 3 mg/kg plain, 7 mg/kg with adrenaline.
- Keep resuscitation equipment ready.

## 2. Indications

- Wound suturing
- Abscess drainage
- Minor procedures
- Foreign body removal

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Allergy history
- Weight (dose calculation)
- Site and vascularity
- Infection at injection site
- Patient anxiety

## 4. Treatment: technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Explain and consent |
| 2 | Use 1–2% lidocaine |
| 3 | 25–27 G needle |
| 4 | Aspirate before injecting |
| 5 | Inject slowly; wait 3–5 min |
| 6 | Test sensation before procedure |

### Maximum dose

- Lidocaine plain: **3 mg/kg** (max 200 mg)
- Lidocaine with adrenaline: **7 mg/kg** (max 500 mg)
- Example: 70 kg adult → 210 mg plain = 21 mL of 1% or 10.5 mL of 2%

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Lidocaine — Xylocaine; Bupivacaine — Sensorcaine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor for toxicity
- Reassure patient
- Have oxygen, suction, adrenaline ready

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Adrenaline:** avoid in fingers/toes/nose/ears (risk of ischemia) and in severe hypertension/CAD.
- **Lidocaine toxicity:** perioral numbness, tinnitus, confusion, seizures, arrhythmia — stop and resuscitate.
- **Allergy:** rare; use alternative (e.g., chloroprocaine) after specialist advice.
- **Anticoagulants:** caution with deep injections.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Seizures/arrhythmia
- Anaphylaxis
- Persistent sensory/motor deficit
- Infection at site
- Large/Complex procedure

## 8. Follow-up

- Review wound after procedure.
- Document agent, dose, time.
