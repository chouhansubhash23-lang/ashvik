# Medical Documentation

> **Source:** MCI/NMC guidelines; NHP; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- If it is not documented, it is not done.
- Records are legal documents and support continuity of care.
- Write legibly, date/time, sign, and avoid abbreviations.
- Protect confidentiality.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Review completeness of records.
- Check consent, diagnosis, treatment, follow-up.
- Ensure allergies and comorbidities documented.
- Verify signatures.

## 3. Treatment: documentation standards

| Element | Include |
|---|---|
| Identification | Name, age, sex, ID |
| History | Complaints, duration, comorbidities |
| Examination | Vitals, findings |
| Diagnosis | Working/final diagnosis |
| Treatment | Drug, dose, route, duration (e.g., paracetamol 500 mg TDS) |
| Advice | Follow-up, red flags |
| Signature | Name, registration number |

- Use structured formats (SOAP).
- Correct errors with single strike, date, sign.
- Store securely.

## 4. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dose:** not applicable (non-drug topic).
- **Paracetamol 500 mg** example of complete prescribing.
- **Abbreviations:** avoid ambiguous ones.
- **Confidentiality:** share only with consent/legal requirement.

## 5. Supportive care

- Train staff.
- Use standard templates.
- Backup electronic records.
- Audit records.

## 6. Red flags

- Missing consent
- Illegible/unsigned notes
- Missing allergy documentation
- Altered records
- Breach of confidentiality

## 7. Follow-up

- Regular record audit.
- Feedback and training.
- Retention as per law.
