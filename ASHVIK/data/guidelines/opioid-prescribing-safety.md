# Opioid Prescribing Safety

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; National Palliative Care Programme
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Opioids are essential for severe pain but carry risks.
- Assess indication, dose, and monitoring.
- Co-prescribe laxatives.
- Follow narcotics regulations.

## 2. Clinical features

- Indications: severe cancer pain, palliative care, acute severe pain
- Risk factors: elderly, renal/hepatic disease, respiratory disease, substance use
- Signs of toxicity: sedation, respiratory depression, pinpoint pupils

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Assessment | Detail |
|---|---|
| Pain severity | 0–10 |
| Function | ADL |
| Comorbidities | Renal/hepatic/respiratory |
| Substance use | Alcohol/drugs |
| Constipation risk | Laxative plan |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Opioid |
|---|---|
| Moderate pain | Tramadol 50–100 mg TDS |
| Severe pain | Morphine 5–10 mg q4h (IR) or 15–30 mg BD (SR) |
| Breakthrough | 10–15% of daily dose |
| Overdose | Naloxone 0.4 mg IV (repeat) |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Tramadol — Ultracet; Morphine; Naloxone.

- Always add laxative (senna/lactulose).
- Do not combine with sedatives unless necessary.

## 5. Supportive care

- Written instructions
- Safe storage
- Pill counts
- Caregiver education

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Benzodiazepines:** respiratory depression.
- **Alcohol:** sedation.
- **Renal failure:** accumulate — reduce dose.
- **SSRIs + tramadol:** serotonin syndrome.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Respiratory depression
- Sedation/confusion
- Suspected overdose
- Substance misuse
- Uncontrolled pain

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess dose/effect.
- Monitor bowel function.
- Review indication.
