# Lumbar Puncture (LP)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- LP diagnoses meningitis, encephalitis, and subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- **Contraindicated in raised intracranial pressure** or coagulopathy.
- Use aseptic technique.
- Send CSF for cells, protein, glucose, Gram stain, culture, PCR.

## 2. Indications

- Suspected meningitis/encephalitis
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage (if CT negative)
- Demyelinating disease
- Therapeutic (rare)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Check | Reason |
|---|---|
| GCS/neuro exam | Raised ICP |
| Fundus | Papilloedema |
| Coagulation | Bleeding risk |
| Skin infection | Avoid site |
| CT head | Before LP if focal deficit/raised ICP |

## 4. Treatment: technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Consent, position (lateral decubitus) |
| 2 | Aseptic technique |
| 3 | Local anaesthesia (lidocaine 1%) |
| 4 | Insert at L3–L4 or L4–L5 |
| 5 | Measure opening pressure |
| 6 | Collect 3–4 tubes |
| 7 | Send for analysis |
| 8 | Bed rest, hydrate |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Lidocaine — Xylocaine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hydration
- Analgesia (paracetamol 500 mg)
- Monitor for headache
- Neurological observation

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Anticoagulants:** withhold as advised.
- **Lidocaine:** max dose.
- **Mannitol:** only for raised ICP (specialist).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Papilloedema/focal deficit
- Coagulopathy
- Skin infection at site
- Respiratory distress
- Herniation signs
- Post-LP deterioration

## 8. Follow-up

- CSF results.
- Treat meningitis.
- Bed rest for headache.
