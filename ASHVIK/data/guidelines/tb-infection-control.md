# TB Infection Control

> **Source:** NTEP; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- TB spreads by airborne droplets.
- Use administrative, environmental, and personal protection.
- Screen and isolate suspected cases.
- Health workers need protection.

## 2. Clinical features (screening)

- Cough > 2 weeks
- Fever/night sweats
- Weight loss
- Haemoptysis

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Triage | Separate suspected TB |
| Sputum/CBNAAT | Diagnosis |
| HIV test | Co-infection |
| Ventilation | Air changes |

## 4. Treatment / prevention

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Administrative | Fast-track diagnosis, cough etiquette |
| Environmental | Open windows, UVGI, ventilation |
| Personal | N95 masks for staff |
| Preventive therapy | Contacts |
| Paracetamol 500 mg | For fever |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** N95 mask; Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Patient education
- Nutrition
- Adherence

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **N95:** fit testing.
- **UVGI:** avoid direct exposure.
- **Isolation:** until non-infectious.
- **Vaccine:** BCG at birth.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Haemoptysis
- Severe breathlessness
- MDR/XDR TB
- HIV co-infection

## 8. Follow-up

- Contact screening.
- Adherence.
- Infection control audit.
