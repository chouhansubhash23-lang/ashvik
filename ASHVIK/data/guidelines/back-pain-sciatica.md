# Low Back Pain and Sciatica

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NICE/WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Most low back pain is mechanical and self-limiting.
- Imaging is not needed for uncomplicated back pain in the first 6 weeks.
- **Red flags** suggest serious pathology — refer urgently.
- Stay active; bed rest is not recommended.

## 2. Clinical features

- Lower back pain, stiffness, muscle spasm
- Pain radiating to buttock/leg (sciatica)
- Numbness/weakness in dermatomal pattern
- Reduced reflexes
- Morning stiffness (inflammatory) vs mechanical pain

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical exam | First-line |
| Straight leg raise | Sciatica |
| Neuro exam | Power, sensation, reflexes |
| X-ray/MRI | Only if red flags or persistent symptoms |
| ESR/CRP | Infection/inflammation |
| Urine | Renal cause |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Acute mechanical back pain

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Activity | Stay active; avoid prolonged bed rest |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol 500–650 mg TDS; NSAID short course if no contraindication |
| Muscle relaxant | Short-term (e.g., thiocolchicoside) if severe spasm |
| Heat | Local heat packs |
| Physiotherapy | Early mobilisation, core exercises |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Dolo 650; Ibuprofen — Brufen.

### 4.2 Sciatica

- Analgesia, neuropathic agents (amitriptyline/gabapentin) if persistent.
- Physiotherapy.
- Refer if severe/progressive deficit.

### 4.3 Chronic back pain

- Exercise, weight loss, ergonomics.
- Multidisciplinary pain management.
- Avoid long-term opioids.

## 5. Supportive care

- Reassurance and education (good prognosis).
- Ergonomic advice.
- Weight management.
- Smoking cessation (disc degeneration).

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury, hypertension; avoid in elderly/CKD.
- **Muscle relaxants:** sedation, dependence.
- **Gabapentin/pregabalin:** sedation, renal dose adjustment.
- **Opioids:** avoid long-term; constipation, dependence.
- **Steroids:** not routine for back pain.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Cauda equina: saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel dysfunction, bilateral leg weakness
- Progressive neurological deficit
- Fever, night pain, weight loss (infection/malignancy)
- Recent significant trauma
- History of cancer
- Age > 50 with new severe pain
- Intravenous drug use/immunosuppression

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 2–4 weeks.
- Refer if no improvement after 6 weeks.
- Encourage activity and exercise.
