# Breast Cancer Screening

> **Source:** National Cancer Control Programme; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Breast cancer is the commonest cancer in Indian women.
- Screen with clinical breast exam; mammography where available.
- Teach breast self-awareness.
- Refer any suspicious lump.

## 2. Clinical features

- Painless breast lump
- Nipple discharge (bloody)
- Skin dimpling/peau d'orange
- Nipple retraction
- Axillary lymph node
- Breast pain (usually benign)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical breast exam | Screening |
| Mammography | 40+ (where available) |
| Ultrasound | Young women/lumps |
| FNAC/biopsy | Confirmation |
| Receptor status | Treatment planning |

## 4. Treatment

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Benign | Reassure/follow-up |
| Suspicious lump | Refer for biopsy |
| Cancer | Oncology referral |
| Pain | Paracetamol 500 mg |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Counselling
- Breast self-awareness
- Psychological support
- Palliative care if advanced

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Hormone therapy:** risk with prolonged use.
- **NSAIDs:** minimal role.
- **Biopsy:** anticoagulants.
- **Pregnancy:** imaging choice.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hard fixed lump
- Nipple discharge with blood
- Skin changes
- Axillary node
- Rapid growth

## 8. Follow-up

- Biopsy result.
- Annual screening.
- Survivorship care.
