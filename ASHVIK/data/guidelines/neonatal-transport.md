# Neonatal Transport

> **Source:** WHO; RMNCH+A; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Transport is a high-risk period for sick neonates.
- Stabilise before transfer: warmth, breathing, glucose, IV access.
- Use trained transport/ambulance.
- Communicate with receiving facility.

## 2. Clinical features

- Respiratory distress
- Apnoea
- Hypothermia
- Hypoglycaemia
- Seizures
- Jaundice/sepsis

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Assessment | Action |
|---|---|
| Airway/breathing | Oxygen/ventilation |
| Temperature | Warm |
| Glucose | Treat |
| Circulation | IV access |
| Weight | Drug doses |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Stabilise ABC |
| 2 | Warm (KMC/incubator) |
| 3 | Glucose 10% 2 mL/kg if low |
| 4 | IV access |
| 5 | Refer with note |

**Paracetamol:** not routine.
**Ampicillin/gentamicin** if sepsis suspected (dose by weight).

**Brand examples (illustrative):** 10% dextrose; Ampicillin; Gentamicin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor vitals
- Keep warm
- Oxygen
- Document

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dextrose:** avoid hyperglycaemia.
- **Gentamicin:** weight-based.
- **Oxygen:** avoid hyperoxia.
- **Sedatives:** avoid.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Apnoea
- Shock
- Seizures
- Severe respiratory distress
- Hypothermia

## 8. Follow-up

- Receiving facility feedback.
- Growth/neuro follow-up.
