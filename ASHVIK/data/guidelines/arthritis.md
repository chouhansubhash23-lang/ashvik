# Osteoarthritis and Rheumatoid Arthritis

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NHP; ACR/EULAR guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **Osteoarthritis (OA):** degenerative joint disease; knee, hip, spine; pain worse with activity.
- **Rheumatoid arthritis (RA):** autoimmune, symmetrical small-joint arthritis; needs early DMARDs.
- Distinguish inflammatory (RA) from mechanical (OA) pain.
- Early referral in RA prevents joint destruction.

## 2. Clinical features

| Feature | OA | RA |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Gradual, older age | Gradual, 30–60 years |
| Joints | Knee, hip, spine, DIP | MCP, PIP, wrist, symmetrical |
| Morning stiffness | < 30 min | > 1 hour |
| Swelling | Bony, hard | Soft, synovial |
| Systemic | None | Fatigue, fever, weight loss |
| Deformity | Late | Early if untreated |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical exam | OA vs RA pattern |
| X-ray | Joint space narrowing, osteophytes (OA); erosions (RA) |
| ESR/CRP | Inflammation (RA) |
| Rheumatoid factor / anti-CCP | RA (anti-CCP more specific) |
| CBC | Anaemia of chronic disease |
| Uric acid | Exclude gout |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Osteoarthritis

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Exercise | Quadriceps strengthening, walking, swimming |
| Weight loss | Reduces knee load |
| Paracetamol | 500–650 mg TDS (max 4 g/day) |
| Topical NSAID | Diclofenac gel 1% TDS |
| Oral NSAID (short-term) | Ibuprofen 400 mg TDS with food; avoid in renal/GI risk |
| Intra-articular steroid | For flare; specialist |
| Referral | Severe disability for joint replacement |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Dolo 650; Ibuprofen — Brufen; Diclofenac gel — Voveran.

### 4.2 Rheumatoid arthritis

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methotrexate | Folitrax | 7.5–15 mg once weekly | + folic acid 5 mg weekly (not same day) |
| Hydroxychloroquine | HCQS | 200–400 mg daily | Eye screening |
| Sulfasalazine | Saaz | 500 mg BD, titrate | GI upset, rash |
| Prednisolone | Wysolone | 5–10 mg daily | Bridge only; taper |
| NSAID | Ibuprofen | 400 mg TDS | Symptom relief only |

Refer to rheumatology for DMARD initiation/monitoring; do not rely on steroids alone.

## 5. Supportive care

- Physiotherapy and occupational therapy
- Hot/cold packs
- Assistive devices (walking stick, knee brace)
- Weight management
- Patient education and self-management

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Paracetamol:** hepatotoxicity; max 4 g/day.
- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury, hypertension; avoid with warfarin, ACEi/ARB, diuretics; topical preferred in elderly.
- **Methotrexate:** hepatotoxicity, bone marrow suppression, pneumonitis; monitor CBC/LFT/renal; avoid in pregnancy; interacts with NSAIDs, trimethoprim, proton pump inhibitors (caution).
- **Hydroxychloroquine:** retinopathy (annual eye exam), QT prolongation.
- **Sulfasalazine:** rash, GI upset, blood dyscrasias; monitor CBC.
- **Steroids:** osteoporosis, hyperglycaemia, infection; taper.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hot, swollen joint with fever (septic arthritis)
- Sudden severe monoarthritis (gout/septic)
- Neurological deficit with back pain (cord compression)
- RA with systemic vasculitis, breathlessness, chest pain
- Cervical spine instability
- Pregnancy with active RA
- Rapidly progressive deformity

## 8. Follow-up

- OA: review 4–6 weeks; reinforce exercise/weight loss.
- RA: monthly until controlled, then 3-monthly; monitor DMARD toxicity.
- Annual eye exam for hydroxychloroquine.
- Assess function and quality of life.
