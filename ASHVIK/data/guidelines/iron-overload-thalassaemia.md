# Iron Overload and Thalassaemia

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Thalassaemia trait is common in India; do not treat with iron unless deficient.
- Thalassaemia major needs transfusions and chelation.
- Iron overload damages liver, heart, and endocrine organs.
- Screen family members.

## 2. Clinical features

- Microcytic anaemia
- Pallor, fatigue
- Hepatosplenomegaly
- Frontal bossing (major)
- Diabetes/hypogonadism (overload)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| CBC/MCV | Microcytosis |
| Ferritin | Iron stores |
| Hb electrophoresis | Diagnosis |
| Serum iron/transferrin | Overload |
| Cardiac/liver MRI | Organ iron |

## 4. Treatment

| Condition | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Thalassaemia trait | No iron; counselling |
| Thalassaemia major | Transfusion + chelation |
| Iron overload | Deferasirox 20 mg/kg/day |
| Iron deficiency with trait | Iron if ferritin low |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Deferasirox — Asunra; Folic acid 5 mg.

## 5. Supportive care

- Family screening
- Genetic counselling
- Vaccination
- Avoid iron/vitamin C excess

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Deferasirox:** renal/hepatic toxicity; monitor.
- **Vitamin C:** increases iron absorption — avoid high doses.
- **Iron:** harmful in overload.
- **NSAIDs:** kidney injury.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Heart failure
- Severe anaemia
- Ferritin > 1000 ng/mL
- Suspected organ damage

## 8. Follow-up

- Ferritin monitoring.
- Transfusion/chelation adherence.
- Genetic counselling.
