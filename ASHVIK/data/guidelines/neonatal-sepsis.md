# Neonatal Sepsis

> **Source:** WHO; IMNCI/F-IMNCI; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Sepsis is a leading cause of neonatal death.
- Risk factors: prematurity, prolonged rupture of membranes, maternal fever.
- **Start antibiotics early.**
- Refer sick neonates.

## 2. Clinical features

- Poor feeding, lethargy
- Hypothermia/fever
- Fast breathing, grunting
- Abdominal distension
- Jaundice, seizures
- Skin pustules/umbilical infection

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| CBC, CRP | Infection |
| Blood culture | Confirm |
| Glucose | Hypoglycaemia |
| Urine/stool | Source |
| Lumbar puncture | Meningitis (referral) |

## 4. Treatment

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Ampicillin | 50 mg/kg IV 12-hourly |
| Gentamicin | 5 mg/kg IV once daily |
| Ceftriaxone | 50 mg/kg IV once daily (alternative) |
| Glucose | 10% dextrose if hypoglycaemic |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ampicillin; Gentamicin; Monocef.

## 5. Supportive care

- Warmth (KMC)
- Feeding support
- Oxygen if hypoxic
- Monitor vitals

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Gentamicin:** nephro/ototoxic; dose by weight.
- **Ampicillin:** allergy.
- **Ceftriaxone:** avoid in neonates with calcium.
- **Fluid overload:** caution.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Apnoea
- Shock
- Seizures
- Meningitis signs
- Severe jaundice
- Weight < 1.5 kg

## 8. Follow-up

- Culture results.
- Feeding/growth.
- Immunisation.
