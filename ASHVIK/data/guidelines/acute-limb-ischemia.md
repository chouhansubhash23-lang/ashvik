# Acute Limb Ischemia

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Acute limb ischemia = sudden decrease in limb perfusion threatening viability.
- Causes: embolism (AF), thrombosis, trauma.
- **Time-critical** — refer immediately.
- Do not elevate the limb; keep it dependent and warm.

## 2. Clinical features — 6 Ps

- **P**ain (severe, out of proportion)
- **P**allor
- **P**ulselessness
- **P**aresthesia
- **P**aralysis (late)
- **P**oikilothermia (cold limb)

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Pulse/Doppler | Perfusion |
| ABPI | < 0.4 suggests severe ischemia |
| ECG | Atrial fibrillation |
| CBC, coagulation | Baseline |
| Creatinine, CK | Rhabdomyolysis |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Analgesia | Paracetamol 500 mg/or opioid |
| Anticoagulation | IV heparin bolus 5000 IU then infusion (specialist) |
| Positioning | Keep limb dependent; avoid compression |
| Fluids | IV 0.9% saline |
| Referral | **Emergency vascular referral** |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Heparin — Heparin; Paracetamol — Dolo 650.

**Do not** use a tourniquet or elevate the limb.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor pulse, colour, sensation
- Treat AF/heart failure
- Hydration
- Prepare for embolectomy/thrombolysis

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Heparin:** bleeding; monitor APTT/platelets (HIT).
- **Opioids:** monitor breathing.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid if anticoagulated.
- **Warfarin/DOACs:** bleeding risk.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Paralysis/anaesthesia
- Absent Doppler signals
- Rapidly progressive pain
- Rhabdomyolysis/dark urine
- AF with embolism
- Trauma with vascular injury

## 8. Follow-up

- Vascular review.
- Anticoagulation plan.
- Rehabilitation.
