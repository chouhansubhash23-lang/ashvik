# Snake Bite, Scorpion Sting and Poisoning

> **Source:** ICMR/NCDC snake bite and poisoning guidance; National Snakebite Protocol; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- India has high snakebite mortality; most bites are from cobra, krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper.
- **Do not** cut, suck, apply tourniquet, or use traditional remedies.
- Poisoning (organophosphate, kerosene, rat poison) is common in rural India.
- Stabilise and refer early; ASV is available at CHC/district hospital.

## 2. Clinical features

### Snake bite
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Neurotoxic (cobra, krait) | Ptosis, diplopia, dysphagia, weakness, respiratory failure |
| Hemotoxic (viper) | Local swelling, bleeding, coagulopathy, AKI |
| Local | Fang marks, pain, swelling, necrosis |

### Scorpion sting
- Severe local pain, sweating, vomiting, hypertension, tachycardia
- Children: pulmonary oedema, shock

### Organophosphate poisoning
- Salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhoea, vomiting, bronchorrhoea, bradycardia, miosis
- Muscle fasciculations, weakness, respiratory failure

### Other poisoning
- Kerosene: cough, breathlessness (aspiration)
- Rat poison (warfarin): bleeding
- Aluminium phosphide: shock, arrhythmia (very high mortality)

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical assessment | Type of bite/poison |
| 20-minute whole blood clotting test | Viper bite coagulopathy |
| CBC, creatinine, electrolytes | Systemic effects |
| ECG | Arrhythmia (scorpion/OP) |
| Cholinesterase | OP poisoning (if available) |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Snake bite — first aid

- Reassure; immobilise the limb; remove rings/bands.
- Pressure immobilisation bandage for neurotoxic bites (not tight tourniquet).
- Transport to hospital with ASV.
- **Do not** incise, suck, apply heat, or give alcohol.

### 4.2 Snake bite — hospital

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| ASV | Polyvalent ASV as per protocol; observe for anaphylaxis |
| Tetanus | Prophylaxis |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol; avoid NSAIDs if coagulopathy |
| Support | Airway, breathing, fluids, dialysis if AKI |

### 4.3 Scorpion sting

- Local anaesthetic/analgesia
- Monitor vitals; ECG
- Prazosin for severe autonomic storm (specialist)
- Refer if severe

### 4.4 Organophosphate poisoning

| Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Atropine | 2 mg IV, repeat every 5–10 min until secretions dry | Endpoint: dry mouth, clear chest |
| Pralidoxime | 1–2 g IV over 30 min, then infusion | Early use |
| Decontamination | Remove clothes, wash skin | Protect staff |

### 4.5 Other poisons

- Activated charcoal 1 g/kg if within 1 hour and airway protected (not for corrosives/hydrocarbons).
- Kerosene: no gastric lavage; supportive; refer.
- Warfarin: vitamin K; FFP if bleeding.
- Aluminium phosphide: supportive; refer immediately.

## 5. Supportive care

- ABCDE approach; oxygen; IV access.
- Monitor urine output, BP, breathing.
- Keep patient warm; treat pain.
- Counsel on prevention (footwear, lights, storing food safely).

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **ASV:** anaphylaxis — keep adrenaline ready; skin test is unreliable.
- **Atropine:** tachycardia, urinary retention, confusion with overdose; titrate to secretions.
- **Pralidoxime:** caution in renal impairment.
- **Activated charcoal:** not with corrosives, hydrocarbons, or unprotected airway.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in coagulopathy/bleeding.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Any neurotoxic snake bite (ptosis, breathing difficulty)
- Bleeding/coagulopathy
- Shock, altered consciousness
- Scorpion sting in a child
- OP poisoning with respiratory distress
- Aluminium phosphide ingestion
- Burns/corrosive ingestion
- Pregnancy with envenomation

## 8. Follow-up

- Observe 24 hours after snake bite.
- Repeat clotting test if viper bite.
- Physiotherapy for limb weakness.
- Mental health support after severe envenomation.
