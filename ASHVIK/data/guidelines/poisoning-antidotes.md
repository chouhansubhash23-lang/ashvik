# Poisoning Antidotes

> **Source:** NCDC; WHO; National Poison Information Centre
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Antidotes are few; supportive care is most important.
- Identify agent and time of exposure.
- Protect airway before decontamination.
- Refer early for severe poisoning.

## 2. Common antidotes

| Poison | Antidote |
|---|---|
| Organophosphate | Atropine + pralidoxime |
| Paracetamol | N-acetylcysteine |
| Iron | Desferrioxamine |
| Opioid | Naloxone |
| Benzodiazepine | Flumazenil (caution) |
| Warfarin | Vitamin K |
| Methanol/ethylene glycol | Fomepizole/ethanol (specialist) |
| Cyanide | Hydroxocobalamin (specialist) |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Agent, amount, time
- Vitals/SpO2
- Glucose
- ECG
- Poison levels if available

## 4. Treatment

| Antidote | Dose |
|---|---|
| Atropine | 2 mg IV, repeat until secretions dry |
| Pralidoxime | 1–2 g IV then infusion |
| NAC | 150 mg/kg IV over 1 h |
| Naloxone | 0.4 mg IV/IM, repeat |
| Vitamin K | 10 mg IV/IM |
| Calcium gluconate | 10 mL 10% IV |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Atropine; Naloxone; Vitamin K.

## 5. Supportive care

- ABCDE
- Decontamination (when appropriate)
- IV fluids
- Monitor

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Flumazenil:** seizures in mixed overdose.
- **Naloxone:** precipitates withdrawal.
- **Activated charcoal:** not for corrosives/hydrocarbons.
- **Never induce vomiting.**

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Respiratory depression
- Seizures
- Shock
- Arrhythmia
- Altered consciousness
- Intentional overdose

## 8. Follow-up

- Psychiatric assessment.
- LFT/renal monitoring.
- Counselling.
