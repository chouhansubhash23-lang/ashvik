# Intravenous Fluid Therapy

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Crystalloids (0.9% saline, Ringer's lactate) are first-line.
- Assess volume status before prescribing fluids.
- Avoid overload — especially in heart/kidney failure.
- Monitor urine output, BP, and signs of pulmonary oedema.

## 2. Indications

- Dehydration/shock
- Vomiting/diarrhoea with poor intake
- Sepsis
- Pre/post-operative
- Unable to take oral fluids

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Assessment | Finding |
|---|---|
| Volume status | Thirst, skin turgor, BP, urine output |
| Overload | Breathlessness, crackles, raised JVP, oedema |
| Electrolytes | Na+, K+, creatinine |
| Urine output | Target 0.5–1 mL/kg/h |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Resuscitation (shock)

- 0.9% saline or Ringer's lactate 500–1000 mL bolus (adult)
- Reassess after each bolus
- Children: 10–20 mL/kg bolus

### 4.2 Maintenance (adult)

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Water | 25–30 mL/kg/day |
| Sodium | 1–2 mmol/kg/day |
| Potassium | 0.5–1 mmol/kg/day |
| Glucose | 50–100 g/day (if needed) |

Example: 0.9% saline 1 L + 5% dextrose 1 L + KCl 20 mmol over 24 h (adjust to labs).

### 4.3 Special situations

| Condition | Fluid |
|---|---|
| DKA | 0.9% saline, then dextrose when glucose falls |
| Burns | Parkland formula |
| Heart failure | Restrict fluids; monitor |
| Kidney injury | Adjust volume; avoid K+ |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** 0.9% saline; Ringer's lactate; 5% dextrose; KCl.

## 5. Supportive care

- Record input/output
- Daily weight
- Monitor electrolytes
- Check cannula site

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Potassium:** never IV bolus; dilute and monitor.
- **Saline:** hyperchloraemic acidosis with large volumes.
- **Dextrose:** hyperglycaemia.
- **Fluid overload:** pulmonary oedema.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Shock not responding
- Pulmonary oedema
- Severe electrolyte abnormality
- Anuria
- Sepsis
- DKA

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess fluids daily.
- Stop IV when oral intake adequate.
- Monitor labs.
