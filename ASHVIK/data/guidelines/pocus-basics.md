# Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS) Basics

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; international POCUS guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- POCUS answers focused clinical questions at the bedside.
- Common: eFAST (trauma), lung, cardiac, IVC, obstetric, urinary.
- It complements, not replaces, clinical assessment.
- Training and supervision are essential.

## 2. Common applications

| Application | Question |
|---|---|
| eFAST | Free fluid/blood in trauma |
| Lung | Effusion, pneumothorax, B-lines |
| Cardiac | Pericardial effusion, ejection fraction |
| IVC | Volume status |
| Obstetric | Pregnancy, fetal heart, placenta |
| Urinary | Hydronephrosis, bladder volume |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Use a low-frequency probe for deep structures; high-frequency for superficial.
- Obtain standard views.
- Document findings.
- Correlate with clinical context.

## 4. Treatment implications

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Free fluid in trauma | Refer for surgery |
| Pericardial effusion with tamponade | Emergency pericardiocentesis/refer |
| Pleural effusion | Thoracentesis if indicated |
| Hydronephrosis | Urinary obstruction workup |
| Fetal heart absent | Confirm and refer |

## 5. Supportive care

- Clean probe between patients.
- Paracetamol 500 mg for procedure-related pain.
- Use gel and appropriate settings.
- Avoid prolonged scanning in unstable patients.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Not a substitute** for definitive imaging.
- **Operator dependence** — interpret cautiously.
- **Infection control** — clean probe.
- **Pregnancy** — follow safety guidance.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Free intraperitoneal fluid in trauma
- Tamponade
- Ruptured ectopic pregnancy
- Massive pleural effusion
- Obstructed kidney with sepsis

## 8. Follow-up

- Repeat scan as needed.
- Formal imaging for uncertain findings.
- Document images.
