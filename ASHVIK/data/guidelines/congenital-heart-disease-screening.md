# Congenital Heart Disease (CHD) Screening

> **Source:** RBSK; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- CHD is a common birth defect; early detection improves outcomes.
- Screen with pulse oximetry and cardiac exam.
- Cyanotic CHD needs urgent referral.
- Critical CHD may present in the first week.

## 2. Clinical features

- Cyanosis (central)
- Murmur
- Poor feeding, failure to thrive
- Tachypnoea, sweating with feeds
- Recurrent pneumonia
- Clubbing (late)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Pulse oximetry (pre/post ductal) | Critical CHD screening |
| Cardiac auscultation | Murmur |
| BP in all limbs | Coarctation |
| Chest X-ray | Heart size/shape |
| Echocardiography | Confirm (referral) |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Cyanotic spell | Knee-chest position, oxygen, morphine, refer |
| Heart failure | Diuretics, refer |
| Critical CHD | Urgent referral |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Furosemide 1 mg/kg/dose; Morphine 0.1 mg/kg.

## 5. Supportive care

- Oxygen if hypoxic (careful in duct-dependent lesions)
- Nutrition
- Immunisation
- Parent counselling

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Oxygen:** may close ductus in duct-dependent lesions — specialist advice.
- **Diuretics:** electrolyte imbalance.
- **Prostaglandin E1:** specialist use only.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in heart failure.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Central cyanosis
- Severe respiratory distress
- Poor feeding/failure to thrive
- Murmur with symptoms
- Absent femoral pulses
- Cyanotic spell

## 8. Follow-up

- Cardiology follow-up.
- Growth monitoring.
- Immunisation.
