# Scorpion Sting

> **Source:** NCDC; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Most stings cause local pain only.
- Children and red scorpion stings can be severe.
- Watch for autonomic storm and pulmonary oedema.
- Refer severe cases.

## 2. Clinical features

- Severe local pain, redness
- Sweating, vomiting
- Hypertension, tachycardia
- Priapism
- Breathlessness (pulmonary oedema)
- Shock in children

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical | Pain + autonomic symptoms |
| ECG | Arrhythmia |
| SpO2 | Pulmonary oedema |
| BP | Autonomic storm |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Local anaesthesia | Lidocaine infiltration |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol 500 mg/or opioid |
| Prazosin | 0.5 mg (specialist) for autonomic storm |
| Oxygen | If hypoxic |
| Referral | Severe/paediatric |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Xylocaine; Dolo 650; Minipress.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor vitals
- ECG if severe
- IV fluids cautiously

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Prazosin:** hypotension; specialist.
- **Opioids:** monitor breathing.
- **NSAIDs:** kidney injury.
- **Antivenom:** not routinely available/effective in India.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Breathlessness
- Shock
- Altered consciousness
- Severe pain in child
- Arrhythmia

## 8. Follow-up

- Observe 24 h.
- Analgesia.
- Review complications.
