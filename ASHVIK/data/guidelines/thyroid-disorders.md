# Thyroid Disorders (Hypothyroidism, Hyperthyroidism)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NHP; ATA guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Hypothyroidism is common in India, especially in women and iodine-deficient regions.
- Congenital hypothyroidism needs newborn screening and early treatment.
- Hyperthyroidism requires specialist input for definitive therapy.
- Thyroid function tests: TSH, free T4, (T3).

## 2. Clinical features

| Hypothyroidism | Hyperthyroidism |
|---|---|
| Weight gain, fatigue | Weight loss, heat intolerance |
| Cold intolerance | Sweating, palpitations |
| Constipation | Diarrhoea |
| Dry skin, hair loss | Tremor, anxiety |
| Bradycardia | Tachycardia, AF |
| Menstrual irregularity | Menstrual irregularity |
| Goitre (sometimes) | Goitre, eye signs |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| TSH | High in primary hypothyroidism; low in hyperthyroidism |
| Free T4 | Low in overt hypothyroidism; high in hyperthyroidism |
| Anti-TPO | Autoimmune thyroiditis |
| Thyroid ultrasound | Nodules, goitre |
| Radioiodine uptake | Specialist |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Hypothyroidism

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Levothyroxine | Thyronorm, Eltroxin | 1.6 mcg/kg/day (adult); start 25–50 mcg in elderly/CAD | Empty stomach, 30–60 min before food |

- Recheck TSH in 6–8 weeks; adjust by 25–50 mcg.
- Pregnancy: increase dose, target TSH < 2.5 mIU/L (trimester-specific).
- Lifelong therapy usually.

### 4.2 Hyperthyroidism

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbimazole | Neo-Mercazole | 10–20 mg daily | Monitor CBC/LFT |
| Propylthiouracil | PTU | 100–200 mg three times daily | Preferred in first trimester/pregnancy |
| Propranolol | Ciplar | 20–40 mg twice daily | Symptom control |

- Refer for radioiodine/surgery.
- **Agranulocytosis:** stop and check CBC if fever/sore throat.

### 4.3 Thyroid nodule / goitre

- TSH, ultrasound, FNAC if suspicious.
- Refer if compressive symptoms, rapid growth, or malignancy suspicion.

## 5. Supportive care

- Iodised salt
- Counselling on adherence
- Pregnancy planning and monitoring
- Newborn screening for congenital hypothyroidism

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Levothyroxine:** reduced absorption with calcium, iron, antacids, PPIs, soy — separate by 4 hours; increases warfarin effect; may worsen angina.
- **Carbimazole/PTU:** agranulocytosis, hepatotoxicity; PTU in first trimester; avoid breastfeeding at high doses.
- **Propranolol:** bradycardia, bronchospasm; avoid in asthma.
- **Amiodarone:** alters thyroid function.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Thyroid storm (fever, tachycardia, agitation, heart failure)
- Myxoedema coma (hypothermia, bradycardia, altered consciousness)
- Agranulocytosis (fever, sore throat on antithyroid drugs)
- Severe heart failure/AF
- Pregnancy with uncontrolled thyroid disease
- Rapidly growing thyroid nodule / hoarseness

## 8. Follow-up

- Hypothyroidism: TSH every 6–8 weeks until stable, then yearly.
- Hyperthyroidism: CBC/LFT during antithyroid therapy; TSH/T4 every 4–6 weeks.
- Pregnancy: monthly TSH.
- Long-term adherence counselling.
