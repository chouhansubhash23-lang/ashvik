# CPR and Basic Life Support (BLS)

> **Source:** Indian Resuscitation Council (IRC); AHA/ILCOR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- High-quality chest compressions save lives.
- Start CPR immediately for unresponsive, non-breathing patients.
- Rate 100–120/min, depth 5–6 cm, full recoil.
- Minimise interruptions.

## 2. Recognition

- Unresponsive
- Not breathing or only gasping
- No pulse (check ≤ 10 s)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Scene safety
- Check response and breathing
- Call for help/ambulance
- Start compressions

## 4. Treatment: BLS sequence

| Step | Adult |
|---|---|
| Compressions | 30 compressions : 2 breaths |
| Rate | 100–120/min |
| Depth | 5–6 cm |
| Recoil | Full |
| AED | Use as soon as available |
| Adrenaline (ALS) | 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 min |
| Amiodarone (ALS) | 300 mg IV after 3rd shock |

**Children:** 15:2 (two rescuers), depth 1/3 chest.
**Infants:** 2 fingers/thumb technique.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Adrenaline — Adrenaline; Amiodarone — Cordarone.

## 5. Supportive care

- Oxygen
- IV/IO access
- Monitor rhythm
- Post-ROSC care: oxygen, BP, glucose, temperature

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Adrenaline:** arrhythmia, hypertension.
- **Amiodarone:** hypotension, bradycardia.
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** only for specific indications.
- **Atropine:** bradycardia (not routine in cardiac arrest).

## 7. Red flags / stop CPR

- Return of spontaneous circulation
- Exhaustion of rescuers (rotate)
- DNR order (if valid)
- Unsafe scene

## 8. Follow-up

- Post-arrest care
- Neurological assessment
- Family support
- Debrief team
