# Congenital Anomalies

> **Source:** WHO; RBSK; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Birth defects are a leading cause of infant mortality.
- Screen at birth and during RBSK visits.
- Refer for surgical/cardiac/developmental evaluation.
- Counsel families.

## 2. Clinical features

- Neural tube defects
- Cleft lip/palate
- Congenital heart disease
- Limb anomalies
- Down syndrome
- Ambiguous genitalia

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Physical exam | Detection |
| Ultrasound | Prenatal/postnatal |
| Echocardiography | Cardiac |
| Genetic testing | Selected cases |
| Hearing/vision screen | Early intervention |

## 4. Treatment

| Anomaly | Action |
|---|---|
| Neural tube defect | Surgical referral, folate |
| Cleft lip/palate | Feeding support, surgery |
| CHD | Cardiology referral |
| Down syndrome | Developmental support |
| Paracetamol 500 mg | For pain (age-adjusted) |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Folic acid 5 mg; Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Feeding support
- Family counselling
- Early intervention
- Immunisation

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Folic acid:** preconception prevention.
- **Antiepileptics:** teratogenic risk.
- **Sedatives:** avoid.
- **Paracetamol:** weight-based.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Cyanosis
- Respiratory distress
- Feeding difficulty
- Meningomyelocele
- Ambiguous genitalia

## 8. Follow-up

- Surgical/developmental follow-up.
- Growth monitoring.
- Family support.
