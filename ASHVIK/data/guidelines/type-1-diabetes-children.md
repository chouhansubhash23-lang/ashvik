# Type 1 Diabetes in Children

> **Source:** ICMR; ISPAD; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Type 1 diabetes is insulin-dependent; presents with polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss.
- DKA is a life-threatening emergency.
- **Never delay insulin.**
- Refer to paediatric diabetes centre.

## 2. Clinical features

- Polyuria, polydipsia
- Weight loss
- Fatigue
- Abdominal pain, vomiting (DKA)
- Kussmaul breathing
- Dehydration

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Blood glucose | > 200 mg/dL with symptoms |
| HbA1c | Diagnosis/monitoring |
| Ketones | DKA |
| Electrolytes, pH | DKA severity |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| New diagnosis | Start insulin; refer |
| DKA | IV fluids, insulin infusion, potassium; emergency |
| Hypoglycaemia | 15 g glucose; recheck |
| Sick day | Never stop insulin; monitor ketones |

**Insulin:** basal-bolus regimen; 0.5–1 unit/kg/day (specialist).
**Hypoglycaemia:** glucose 0.3 g/kg oral or IV dextrose.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Human Actrapid; Mixtard; Lantus.

## 5. Supportive care

- Education on insulin, diet, monitoring
- Hypoglycaemia management
- Sick-day rules
- Psychosocial support

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Insulin:** hypoglycaemia; rotate sites.
- **Steroids:** raise glucose.
- **Beta-blockers:** mask hypoglycaemia.
- **Never stop insulin** in DKA.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- DKA (vomiting, Kussmaul, ketones)
- Severe hypoglycaemia
- Altered consciousness
- Dehydration
- Suspected infection

## 8. Follow-up

- Frequent glucose monitoring.
- HbA1c every 3 months.
- Growth and puberty review.
