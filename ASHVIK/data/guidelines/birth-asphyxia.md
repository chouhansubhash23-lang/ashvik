# Birth Asphyxia

> **Source:** WHO; NRP; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Asphyxia = impaired gas exchange at birth.
- Assess with Apgar and need for resuscitation.
- Hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (HIE) may follow.
- Cooling within 6 h improves outcomes (referral).

## 2. Clinical features

- Poor cry/tone
- Bradycardia
- Cyanosis
- Seizures
- Multiorgan dysfunction

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Apgar | Severity |
| Blood gas | Acidosis |
| Glucose/electrolytes | Complications |
| EEG/imaging | HIE (referral) |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Newborn resuscitation |
| 2 | Oxygen/ventilation |
| 3 | Correct glucose/electrolytes |
| 4 | Treat seizures |
| 5 | Refer for cooling |

**Phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg** for seizures (specialist).
**Paracetamol:** not routine.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Phenobarbitone; 10% dextrose.

## 5. Supportive care

- Warmth
- Feeding support
- Monitor urine output
- Neuro follow-up

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Phenobarbitone:** respiratory depression.
- **Oxygen:** avoid hyperoxia.
- **Fluids:** avoid overload.
- **Sedatives:** monitor.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Seizures
- Apnoea
- Shock
- Oliguria
- Severe acidosis

## 8. Follow-up

- Neurodevelopment.
- Hearing/vision.
- Immunisation.
