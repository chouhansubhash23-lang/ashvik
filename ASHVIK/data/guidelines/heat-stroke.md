# Heat Stroke

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Heat stroke = core temp ≥ 40 °C + altered mental state.
- **Cool immediately** — every minute matters.
- Cool first, transport second.
- Avoid antipyretics (not effective).

## 2. Clinical features

- High body temperature
- Confusion, seizures, coma
- Hot dry skin (classic) or sweating (exertional)
- Tachycardia, hypotension
- Vomiting

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Core temperature | ≥ 40 °C |
| Consciousness | Altered |
| BP/pulse | Shock |
| Electrolytes/renal | Complications |
| Glucose | Hypoglycaemia |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Move to shade; remove clothes |
| 2 | Cool with water/fan/ice packs |
| 3 | IV fluids 0.9% saline |
| 4 | Oxygen if needed |
| 5 | Refer |
| 6 | Paracetamol 500 mg not effective for heat stroke |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650 (only for associated pain).

**Do not** use alcohol rubs or ice immersion in elderly.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor temperature
- Treat seizures (lorazepam)
- Monitor urine output

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Antipyretics:** ineffective.
- **Sedatives:** may worsen.
- **Diuretics:** dehydration.
- **Vasopressors:** if shock.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Altered consciousness
- Temp > 40 °C
- Shock
- Seizures
- Renal failure
- Rhabdomyolysis

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess temperature/neuro.
- Hydration.
- Prevention advice.
