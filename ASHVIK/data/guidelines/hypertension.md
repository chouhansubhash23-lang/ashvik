# Hypertension

> **Source:** ICMR Standard Treatment Workflows Vol. 3 (2022); NPCDCS
> **Evidence:** `../evidence/raw/icmr_stw_vol3_2022.pdf`, `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- India: ~1 in 4 adults has hypertension; most are undiagnosed or uncontrolled.
- BP ≥ 140/90 mmHg (clinic, repeated) = hypertension.
- Target: < 140/90 generally; < 130/80 in diabetes/CKD if tolerated; elderly < 150/90.
- Rule out secondary causes in young patients, resistant hypertension, or sudden onset.

## 2. Clinical features

Usually asymptomatic. Look for:
- Headache, dizziness, blurred vision
- Chest pain, breathlessness
- Palpitations
- Features of target-organ damage: stroke, heart failure, kidney disease, retinopathy

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| BP measurement (both arms, repeated) | Confirm diagnosis |
| Home/ambulatory BP | White-coat/masked hypertension |
| Urine dipstick, creatinine | Kidney involvement |
| Fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids | Metabolic risk |
| ECG | Left ventricular hypertrophy, arrhythmia |
| Fundus | Hypertensive retinopathy |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Lifestyle (all patients)

- Salt < 5 g/day
- DASH-style diet: fruit, vegetables, low-fat dairy
- Exercise 150 min/week
- Weight loss if overweight
- Stop tobacco; limit alcohol
- Sleep 7–8 h; manage stress

### 4.2 First-line drugs (adult)

| Drug class | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amlodipine (CCB) | Amlopres, Stamlo | 5 mg once daily (2.5–10 mg) | Preferred in elderly, isolated systolic HTN |
| Telmisartan (ARB) | Telma | 40 mg once daily (20–80 mg) | Preferred in diabetes/CKD |
| Chlorthalidone (thiazide-like) | Generic | 12.5 mg once daily | Monitor potassium/sodium |

**Combination therapy** (e.g., amlodipine + telmisartan) is often needed. Use a single-pill combination where available to improve adherence.

### 4.3 Special situations

| Situation | Preferred |
|---|---|
| Pregnancy | Labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa. **Never ACEi/ARB.** |
| Diabetes/CKD | ACEi or ARB (monitor creatinine/K+) |
| Heart failure | ACEi/ARB + beta-blocker + diuretic |
| Post-stroke | ACEi/ARB + thiazide |
| Elderly | Amlodipine or thiazide; start low |

## 5. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Amlodipine:** ankle oedema; CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, ketoconazole) raise levels; grapefruit juice; simvastatin dose limit.
- **Telmisartan:** hyperkalaemia with spironolactone, ACEi, potassium supplements; NSAIDs reduce effect and worsen kidney function; contraindicated in pregnancy; lithium toxicity.
- **Chlorthalidone:** hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia; digoxin toxicity with low K+; lithium toxicity.
- **General:** avoid sudden withdrawal of beta-blockers; check adherence at every visit.

## 6. Red flags — refer immediately

- BP ≥ 180/120 with chest pain, breathlessness, neuro deficit, visual loss, or altered consciousness
- Suspected stroke or TIA
- Acute pulmonary oedema
- Aortic dissection (tearing chest pain)
- Pregnancy with severe hypertension/pre-eclampsia
- New hypertension in a young patient (secondary cause workup)

## 7. Follow-up

- Review every 2–4 weeks until BP controlled, then every 3 months.
- Check renal function and electrolytes 2–4 weeks after starting ACEi/ARB or diuretic.
- Reinforce lifestyle at every visit.
- Screen family members.
