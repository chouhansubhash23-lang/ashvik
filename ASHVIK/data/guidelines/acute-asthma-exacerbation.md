# Acute Asthma Exacerbation

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); GINA; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Assess severity immediately: speak in sentences? SpO2? PEF?
- **Severe/life-threatening exacerbation is an emergency.**
- Oxygen + inhaled bronchodilators + systemic steroid are the core.
- Avoid sedatives and beta-blockers.

## 2. Clinical features

| Severity | Features |
|---|---|
| Mild-moderate | Speaks in sentences, SpO2 ≥ 92%, PEF > 50% |
| Severe | Speaks in words, SpO2 < 92%, PEF 33–50%, accessory muscles |
| Life-threatening | Drowsy, silent chest, cyanosis, PEF < 33% |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| SpO2 | Severity |
| Peak flow | If available |
| Chest X-ray | Suspected pneumothorax/pneumonia |
| ABG | Life-threatening (if available) |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Mild-moderate

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Salbutamol inhaler | 2–4 puffs via spacer, repeat every 20 min x 3 |
| Prednisolone | 40–50 mg daily x 5 days |

### 4.2 Severe

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Oxygen | Target SpO2 94–98% |
| Salbutamol nebulisation | 2.5–5 mg every 20 min x 3, then as needed |
| Ipratropium nebulisation | 0.5 mg every 20 min x 3 |
| Prednisolone | 40–50 mg daily x 5–7 days (or IV hydrocortisone 100 mg 6-hourly) |
| Magnesium sulfate | 2 g IV over 20 min (severe, specialist) |

### 4.3 Life-threatening

- Oxygen, continuous nebulisation, IV steroids.
- Magnesium sulfate, consider IV salbutamol (specialist).
- **Refer immediately**; prepare airway support.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Salbutamol — Asthalin; Ipratropium — Ipravent; Prednisolone — Wysolone; Hydrocortisone — Efcorlin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Sit upright; reassure.
- Hydration.
- Monitor SpO2, pulse, PEF.
- Identify and remove trigger.
- Review inhaler technique before discharge.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Beta-blockers:** contraindicated (bronchospasm).
- **Salbutamol:** tachycardia, tremor, hypokalaemia.
- **Steroids:** hyperglycaemia, infection; short course preferred.
- **Magnesium:** hypotension, bradycardia — monitor.
- **Sedatives:** avoid (respiratory depression).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- SpO2 < 92% despite treatment
- Silent chest, cyanosis
- Drowsiness/confusion
- PEF < 33%
- Unable to speak
- Suspected pneumothorax
- No response to bronchodilators

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 2–7 days.
- Start/optimise controller inhaler (ICS).
- Written action plan.
- Avoid triggers; vaccination.
