# Dyslipidaemia

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NPCDCS
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Dyslipidaemia is a major cardiovascular risk factor.
- Statins are first-line for most patients.
- Lifestyle change is foundational.
- Target depends on cardiovascular risk.

## 2. Clinical features

- Often asymptomatic
- Xanthelasma
- Arcus corneae
- Obesity/central adiposity
- Family history

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Lipid profile | Diagnosis |
| TSH | Hypothyroidism |
| HbA1c | Diabetes |
| Liver/renal function | Before statin |
| Cardiovascular risk | Treatment target |

## 4. Treatment

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Atorvastatin | 10–40 mg HS (high risk 40–80 mg) |
| Rosuvastatin | 5–20 mg HS |
| Fenofibrate | 160 mg OD (hypertriglyceridaemia) |
| Ezetimibe | 10 mg OD (add-on) |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Atorva; Rosuvas; Tricor; Ezetrol.

- Lifestyle: diet, exercise, weight loss, stop smoking.

## 5. Supportive care

- Dietitian
- Exercise
- Smoking cessation
- Diabetes/BP control

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Statins + fibrates:** myopathy risk.
- **Statins + macrolides/azoles:** rhabdomyolysis.
- **Grapefruit:** raises statin levels.
- **Pregnancy:** avoid statins.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Chest pain
- Severe hypertriglyceridaemia (pancreatitis)
- Muscle pain/weakness (rhabdomyolysis)
- Familial hypercholesterolaemia

## 8. Follow-up

- Lipid profile in 8–12 weeks.
- LFT if symptoms.
- Adherence counselling.
