# Paediatric Skin Infections

> **Source:** ICMR; NCDC; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common: impetigo, scabies, fungal infections, abscess.
- Hygiene and early treatment prevent spread.
- Treat household contacts for scabies.
- Refer severe/invasive infections.

## 2. Clinical features

| Condition | Features |
|---|---|
| Impetigo | Honey-coloured crusts |
| Scabies | Itching, burrows, family itching |
| Tinea | Ring-shaped scaly patches |
| Abscess | Tender fluctuant swelling |
| Cellulitis | Spreading redness, fever |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Clinical exam
- Skin scraping/KOH for fungal/scabies
- Pus culture for abscess
- Blood sugar if recurrent

## 4. Treatment

| Condition | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Impetigo (limited) | Mupirocin 2% ointment TDS x 5–7 days |
| Impetigo (extensive) | Cephalexin 25–50 mg/kg/day |
| Scabies | Permethrin 5% cream (age-specific) |
| Tinea | Clotrimazole 1% cream BD x 2–4 weeks |
| Abscess | Incision and drainage |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** T-Bact; Sporidex; Permite; Candid.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hygiene
- Avoid sharing towels
- Treat contacts
- Trim nails

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Permethrin:** avoid eyes; age-specific use.
- **Cephalexin:** allergy; renal adjustment.
- **Clotrimazole:** local irritation.
- **Steroids:** avoid in infection.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Cellulitis with fever
- Abscess in face/neck
- Extensive infection
- Immunocompromised
- Suspected sepsis

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 48–72 h.
- Complete treatment.
- Household treatment for scabies.
