# Urinary Incontinence in Older Adults

> **Source:** ICMR; NPHCE; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common but not a normal part of ageing.
- Types: stress, urge, overflow, functional.
- Treat reversible causes first (UTI, constipation, drugs).
- Pelvic floor exercises help stress incontinence.

## 2. Clinical features

- Leakage with cough/sneeze (stress)
- Sudden urgency (urge)
- Constant dribbling (overflow)
- Nocturia
- Functional (mobility/cognitive)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Urine dipstick | UTI |
| Post-void residual | Overflow |
| Bladder diary | Pattern |
| Pelvic exam | Prolapse |
| Medication review | Diuretics/sedatives |

## 4. Treatment

| Type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Stress | Pelvic floor exercises; pessary |
| Urge | Bladder training; oxybutynin 2.5–5 mg |
| Overflow | Relieve obstruction; catheter |
| Functional | Mobility aids; scheduled toileting |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Oxybutynin — Ditropan; Solifenacin — Vesicare.

## 5. Supportive care

- Fluid management
- Constipation treatment
- Skin care
- Absorbent products

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Anticholinergics:** confusion, dry mouth, retention.
- **Diuretics:** urgency; time doses.
- **Sedatives:** functional incontinence.
- **Caffeine/alcohol:** worsen urgency.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hematuria
- Retention
- Neurological deficit
- Suspected fistula
- Recurrent UTI

## 8. Follow-up

- Bladder diary review.
- Pelvic floor training.
- Urology referral if refractory.
