# Geriatric Care (Falls, Polypharmacy, Frailty)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly (NPHCE); WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Older adults often present with atypical symptoms (confusion, falls, poor appetite).
- **Polypharmacy** and drug interactions are major preventable harms.
- Falls, delirium, incontinence, and pressure ulcers are geriatric syndromes.
- Assess function, cognition, mood, nutrition, and social support.

## 2. Common geriatric syndromes

| Syndrome | Features |
|---|---|
| Falls | Unsteadiness, syncope, fracture |
| Delirium | Acute confusion, fluctuating attention |
| Dementia | Progressive memory/cognitive decline |
| Incontinence | Urinary/fecal |
| Pressure ulcers | Bedbound, immobile |
| Malnutrition | Weight loss, poor intake |
| Polypharmacy | ≥ 5 medicines, adverse effects |
| Depression | Low mood, isolation |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Domain | Tool |
|---|---|
| Function | ADL/IADL, gait speed |
| Cognition | MMSE/MOCA (if available) |
| Mood | PHQ-9/GDS |
| Nutrition | Weight, BMI, appetite |
| Falls risk | History, vision, BP, medications |
| Medication review | List all drugs, doses, indications |
| Social | Caregiver, finances, abuse |

## 4. Treatment / Management

### 4.1 Falls prevention

- Review medications (sedatives, antihypertensives)
- Vision and hearing correction
- Home hazards: lighting, rails, loose rugs
- Physiotherapy and balance training
- Treat osteoporosis (calcium 500 mg twice daily + vitamin D 800 IU daily; bisphosphonate if indicated)

### 4.2 Polypharmacy / deprescribing

- Stop drugs without a clear indication.
- Avoid Beers-listed drugs in elderly (benzodiazepines, long-acting sulfonylureas, anticholinergics).
- Simplify dosing; use pill boxes.
- Review after every hospitalization.

### 4.3 Delirium

- Treat the cause: infection, dehydration, drugs, constipation, pain, hypoxia.
- Reorient; involve family; avoid restraints.
- Avoid antipsychotics unless severe agitation/psychosis.

### 4.4 Pressure ulcers

- Reposition every 2 h; pressure-relieving mattress.
- Keep skin clean and dry.
- Nutrition (protein, calories).
- Debridement and dressing; treat infection.

## 5. Supportive care

- Encourage mobility and exercise.
- Adequate hydration and protein.
- Vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19).
- Caregiver education and respite.
- Advance care planning and goals of care.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Benzodiazepines:** falls, confusion, dependence — avoid long-term.
- **Anticholinergics:** confusion, urinary retention, constipation.
- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury — avoid.
- **Sulfonylureas:** hypoglycaemia — prefer safer agents.
- **Multiple antihypertensives:** orthostatic hypotension and falls.
- **Warfarin/DOACs:** bleeding risk; review indication and dose.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Acute confusion/delirium
- Fall with injury/fracture
- Suspected stroke or sepsis
- Severe dehydration
- Chest pain/breathlessness
- GI bleeding
- Neglect/abuse
- Suicidal ideation

## 8. Follow-up

- Regular review every 3–6 months.
- Medication review at each visit.
- Falls risk reassessment.
- Caregiver support and community services (NPHCE).
