# Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

> **Source:** ICMR; NPCDCS; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Leading cause of preventable blindness in adults.
- Screen annually; more often if retinopathy present.
- Control sugar, BP, and lipids.
- Refer for laser/anti-VEGF if sight-threatening.

## 2. Clinical features

- Often asymptomatic
- Blurred vision
- Floaters
- Sudden vision loss (vitreous hemorrhage)
- Difficulty reading

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Visual acuity | Baseline |
| Fundus exam | Retinopathy staging |
| Fundus photography | Screening |
| OCT/angiography | Specialist |
| HbA1c, BP | Risk control |

## 4. Treatment

| Stage | Action |
|---|---|
| No retinopathy | Annual screening |
| Mild NPDR | 6–12 months |
| Sight-threatening | Refer urgently |
| Macular edema | Anti-VEGF/laser |

- **Control HbA1c < 7%**, BP < 130/80.
- **Paracetamol 500 mg** for pain (not specific).

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Telma; Amlopres; Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Glycaemic control
- BP/lipid control
- Smoking cessation
- Eye protection

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk with injections.
- **Steroids:** cataract/glaucoma.
- **Anti-VEGF:** specialist.
- **Tight control:** may transiently worsen retinopathy.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Sudden vision loss
- Vitreous hemorrhage
- Retinal detachment
- Neovascular glaucoma
- Macular edema

## 8. Follow-up

- Annual fundus exam.
- Control risk factors.
- Refer if progression.
