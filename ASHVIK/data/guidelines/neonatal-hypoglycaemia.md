# Neonatal Hypoglycaemia

> **Source:** WHO; IAP; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Blood glucose < 45 mg/dL (symptomatic or high risk).
- Common in preterm, LBW, diabetic mothers, sepsis.
- Treat with feeding or IV dextrose.
- Monitor and prevent recurrence.

## 2. Clinical features

- Jitteriness, tremors
- Lethargy, poor feeding
- Apnoea, cyanosis
- Seizures
- Hypothermia

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Blood glucose | Diagnosis |
| Sepsis screen | Cause |
| Glucose after treatment | Response |
| Insulin/other | Persistent cases |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic, feeding | Breastfeed/expressed milk |
| Symptomatic/< 25 mg/dL | 10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV |
| Persistent | IV dextrose infusion + refer |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** 10% dextrose; Dolo 650 (maternal).

## 5. Supportive care

- Keep warm
- Early feeding
- Monitor glucose
- Treat cause

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dextrose:** avoid hyperglycaemia.
- **Steroids:** not routine.
- **Glucagon:** specialist.
- **Parenteral nutrition:** specialist.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Seizures
- Apnoea
- Persistent hypoglycaemia
- Sepsis
- Preterm/LBW

## 8. Follow-up

- Glucose monitoring.
- Feeding/growth.
- Neuro assessment.
