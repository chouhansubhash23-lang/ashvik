# Stroke (Cerebrovascular Accident) — Referral Focus

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NPCDCS; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Stroke = sudden focal neurological deficit due to infarction (80%) or haemorrhage (20%).
- **Time is brain** — thrombolysis window is 4.5 hours for ischemic stroke.
- Rural PHC role: recognise with FAST, check glucose, avoid harmful BP lowering, refer immediately.
- Do not give aspirin until haemorrhage is excluded by CT.

## 2. Clinical features

- **F**ace droop
- **A**rm weakness
- **S**peech difficulty
- **T**ime to call emergency

Other features:
- Sudden weakness/numbness of one side
- Vision loss, double vision
- Severe headache, vomiting (haemorrhage)
- Vertigo, imbalance
- Altered consciousness, seizures

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| FAST + neuro exam | Recognition |
| Blood glucose | Exclude hypoglycaemia |
| BP | Avoid aggressive lowering |
| CT head | Differentiate ischemic vs haemorrhagic |
| ECG | Atrial fibrillation |
| CBC, INR, creatinine | Before thrombolysis |

## 4. Treatment (immediate)

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Airway/breathing | Oxygen if SpO2 < 94% |
| Glucose | Treat hypoglycaemia (< 70 mg/dL) |
| BP | Lower only if > 220/120 mmHg (or > 185/110 before thrombolysis) — specialist advice |
| Aspirin | 300 mg only after CT excludes haemorrhage |
| Antiemetic | Ondansetron 4–8 mg IV for vomiting |
| Referral | Immediate transfer to CT-capable hospital |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Aspirin — Ecosprin; Ondansetron — Emeset.

### Thrombolysis

- IV alteplase/tenecteplase within 4.5 h in eligible patients — only at a centre with protocol and CT.
- Do not delay transfer for thrombolysis.

## 5. Supportive care

- Nil by mouth until swallow assessed
- Position 30° head-up
- Avoid urinary catheter if possible
- Prevent pressure sores
- Physiotherapy/speech therapy after stabilisation
- Control fever, sugar, hydration

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Aspirin:** avoid in haemorrhagic stroke; bleeding risk; GI protection.
- **Antihypertensives:** avoid rapid BP drop — may worsen ischemia.
- **Ondansetron:** QT prolongation.
- **Thrombolytics:** bleeding, angioedema; strict contraindications.
- **Anticoagulants:** hold until haemorrhage excluded; reverse if on warfarin.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Any sudden focal deficit
- Altered consciousness, seizures
- Severe headache with vomiting (haemorrhage)
- BP > 220/120
- Signs of raised intracranial pressure
- Pregnancy with stroke
- Stroke in a young patient (workup needed)

## 8. Follow-up

- Secondary prevention: antiplatelet, statin, BP control, sugar control, smoking cessation.
- Carotid evaluation if indicated.
- Rehabilitation: physiotherapy, speech, occupational therapy.
- Screen for atrial fibrillation.
