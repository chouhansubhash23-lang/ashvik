# Nutritional Anaemia in Children

> **Source:** National Iron Plus Initiative; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Iron deficiency is the commonest cause of childhood anaemia.
- Treat the cause (diet, worms, malaria, blood loss).
- **Iron dose is weight-based.**
- Severe anaemia needs referral.

## 2. Clinical features

- Pallor (palms, conjunctiva)
- Fatigue, poor appetite
- Irritability, poor concentration
- Pica
- Breathlessness in severe cases

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Hb | Anaemia severity |
| CBC/MCV | Microcytic anaemia |
| Ferritin | Iron deficiency |
| Stool | Worms |
| Malaria | Endemic areas |

## 4. Treatment

| Age | Iron dose |
|---|---|
| 6–59 months | 3 mg/kg/day elemental iron (syrup) |
| 5–12 years | 30–60 mg elemental iron daily |
| Adolescents | 60 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg folic acid daily |

**Duration:** 3 months after Hb normalises.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Autrin; Orofer; IFA syrup.

- Deworming: albendazole 400 mg (≥ 2 years).
- Vitamin C improves absorption.

## 5. Supportive care

- Iron-rich diet
- Avoid tea/coffee with meals
- Deworming
- Malaria prevention

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Iron:** constipation, black stools; separate from calcium/antacids by 2 h.
- **Albendazole:** avoid in first trimester.
- **Paracetamol:** max 4 doses/day.
- **Thalassaemia:** check before iron in severe anaemia.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hb < 7 g/dL with symptoms
- Heart failure
- Severe dehydration
- Suspected thalassaemia
- No response after 4 weeks

## 8. Follow-up

- Recheck Hb in 4 weeks.
- Continue iron 3 months after normalisation.
- Nutrition counselling.
