# Iron-Deficiency Anaemia (IDA)

> **Source:** ICMR/NHP anaemia guidance; National Iron Plus Initiative; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Most common anaemia in India, especially in women and children.
- Causes: low iron intake, hookworm, malaria, blood loss (menstrual, GI), pregnancy, malabsorption.
- WHO cut-offs (adults): Hb < 12 g/dL women, < 13 g/dL men; children < 11 g/dL.
- Always look for the **cause** of iron deficiency, especially in men and postmenopausal women (GI blood loss).

## 2. Clinical features

- Fatigue, weakness, breathlessness on exertion
- Pallor (conjunctiva, palms, tongue)
- Dizziness, palpitations
- Pica, brittle nails, angular stomatitis
- Severe: tachycardia, heart failure, syncope

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| CBC with Hb, MCV, MCH | Microcytic hypochromic anaemia |
| Peripheral smear | Confirm morphology |
| Serum ferritin | Low (< 15–30 ng/mL) in IDA |
| Iron studies | Transferrin saturation < 20% |
| Stool exam | Hookworm/occult blood |
| Pregnancy test | In women of reproductive age |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Oral iron (first-line)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ferrous sulphate + folic acid | Autrin, Orofer | 1 tablet once daily (or alternate day) | 3 months after Hb normalises |
| Ferrous ascorbate | Ferium | 100 mg elemental iron daily | 3 months after Hb normalises |
| Vitamin C | Generic | 500 mg with iron | Improves absorption |

**Take on an empty stomach or with vitamin C.** Avoid tea/coffee/milk/calcium within 1–2 hours.

### 4.2 Parenteral iron

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Severe anaemia, intolerance, malabsorption | IV ferric carboxymaltose (Ferinject, Encicarb) — calculated dose |
| Late pregnancy with severe anaemia | IV iron under supervision |

### 4.3 Severe anaemia

- Hb < 7 g/dL with symptoms, or heart failure → refer for transfusion.
- Treat the underlying cause (hookworm, malaria, menorrhagia, GI bleed).

## 5. Supportive care

- Iron-rich diet: green leafy vegetables, legumes, jaggery, meat
- Deworming (albendazole) as per national programme
- Malaria prevention and treatment
- Menstrual hygiene and contraception counselling
- Adolescent and pregnancy iron-folic acid supplementation

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Oral iron:** nausea, constipation, black stools; reduces absorption of levothyroxine, tetracyclines, quinolones, bisphosphonates — separate by 2–4 hours.
- **Tea/coffee/calcium/antacids** reduce iron absorption.
- **Vitamin C** enhances absorption.
- **IV iron:** anaphylactoid reactions (monitor), avoid in active infection, caution in pregnancy (2nd/3rd trimester).
- **Thalassaemia trait:** iron may be harmful if overloaded — check ferritin.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hb < 7 g/dL with breathlessness, chest pain, syncope
- Heart failure
- Active bleeding (GI, menstrual)
- Suspected malignancy (weight loss, GI symptoms)
- Pregnancy with severe anaemia
- No response after 4 weeks of oral iron (check adherence, diagnosis)

## 8. Follow-up

- Recheck Hb in 4 weeks; expect 1–2 g/dL rise.
- Continue iron for 3 months after Hb normalises to replenish stores.
- Recheck ferritin if available.
- Treat and follow up the underlying cause.
