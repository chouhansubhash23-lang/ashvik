# Dog Bite Wound Care

> **Source:** National Rabies Control Programme; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **Wash immediately with soap and water for 15 minutes.**
- Rabies PEP depends on wound category.
- Do not suture immediately unless necessary.
- Tetanus prophylaxis.

## 2. Clinical features

- Bite/scratch wound
- Bleeding
- Nerve/tendon injury
- Infection signs

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Category | Exposure |
|---|---|
| I | Touching, intact skin |
| II | Nibbling, superficial scratch |
| III | Bite with bleeding, mucosal contact |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wash 15 min with soap/water |
| 2 | Antiseptic (povidone-iodine) |
| 3 | Do not suture immediately |
| 4 | Rabies vaccine 0,3,7,14,28 |
| 5 | RIG for category III |
| 6 | Tetanus prophylaxis |
| 7 | Antibiotics for infected/bite wounds |

**Antibiotics:** amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg TDS x 5–7 days.
**Paracetamol 500 mg** for pain.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Augmentin; Verorab; Equirab.

## 5. Supportive care

- Wound review
- Analgesia
- Observe animal if possible

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **RIG:** infiltrate wound; not into healed wound.
- **Vaccine:** complete schedule.
- **Antibiotics:** allergy.
- **Suturing:** delayed if contaminated.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Category III bite
- Face/hand/genital bite
- Nerve/tendon injury
- Wild animal/bat bite
- Infection/systemic signs

## 8. Follow-up

- Complete vaccine.
- Wound review.
- Tetanus booster.
