# Dengue Triage (Warning-Sign Assessment)

> **Source:** NVBDCP dengue guidelines; WHO Dengue Guidelines (S12)
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Triage decides home care vs admission vs emergency referral.
- The critical phase is around defervescence (day 4–7).
- **Warning signs** = admit and monitor.
- **Severe dengue** = emergency resuscitation and referral.

## 2. Triage groups (WHO/NVBDCP)

| Group | Criteria | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| A | No warning signs, tolerating oral fluids, good urine output | Home care + daily review |
| B | Warning signs or co-morbidity (pregnancy, diabetes, elderly, infancy) | Admit for observation |
| C | Severe dengue (shock, bleeding, organ failure, respiratory distress) | Emergency resuscitation + refer |

## 3. Warning signs

- Abdominal pain/tenderness
- Persistent vomiting
- Mucosal bleeding
- Lethargy/restlessness
- Liver enlargement > 2 cm
- Rising haematocrit with falling platelets
- Fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion)
- Oliguria

## 4. Severe dengue

- Shock (cold extremities, weak pulse, narrow pulse pressure, hypotension)
- Respiratory distress/pulmonary oedema
- Severe bleeding
- Altered consciousness/seizures
- Organ failure (liver, kidney, heart)
- Severe metabolic acidosis

## 5. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Vital signs | 4–6 hourly (group B) |
| Haematocrit/platelets | Daily or more often |
| Urine output | Hourly if admitted |
| SpO2 | If respiratory symptoms |
| BP | Postural drop |

## 6. Treatment: management by group

### Group A (home)
- Paracetamol 500–650 mg TDS (max 4 g/day)
- ORS/fluids
- Daily review; return if warning signs
- Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin

### Group B (admit)
- IV fluids (0.9% saline/Ringer's lactate) titrated
- Monitor haematocrit, urine output, vitals
- Avoid overhydration
- Treat warning signs

### Group C (emergency)
- Oxygen, IV access, rapid fluids
- Blood transfusion if severe bleeding
- Refer to higher centre
- Notify

## 7. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Paracetamol:** max 4 g/day; hepatotoxicity.
- **NSAIDs/aspirin:** contraindicated.
- **IV fluids:** overhydration → pulmonary oedema.
- **Steroids:** not routine.
- **IM injections:** avoid if thrombocytopenia.

## 8. Red flags — refer immediately

- Shock
- Severe bleeding
- Respiratory distress
- Altered consciousness
- Organ failure
- Pregnancy with warning signs
- Infant < 1 year with warning signs

## 9. Follow-up

- Daily review until afebrile 24–48 h and improving.
- Repeat CBC as advised.
- Patient education on warning signs.
