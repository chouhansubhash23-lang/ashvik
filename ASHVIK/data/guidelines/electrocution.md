# Electrocution

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **Switch off power before touching the victim.**
- Cardiac arrhythmia is the main cause of death.
- Burns may hide deep tissue injury.
- Monitor ECG.

## 2. Clinical features

- Entry/exit burns
- Cardiac arrest/arrhythmia
- Muscle contraction, fractures
- Altered consciousness
- Rhabdomyolysis

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| ECG | Arrhythmia |
| CK, renal function | Rhabdomyolysis |
| Burns assessment | Severity |
| Neuro exam | Injury |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Switch off power |
| 2 | ABCDE/CPR |
| 3 | ECG monitoring |
| 4 | Treat burns |
| 5 | IV fluids |
| 6 | Refer |

**Paracetamol 500 mg** for pain; morphine if severe.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650; Morphine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Tetanus prophylaxis
- Wound care
- Monitor urine output
- Psychological support

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Adrenaline:** for arrest.
- **Fluids:** avoid overload.
- **NSAIDs:** kidney injury.
- **Sedatives:** monitor.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Cardiac arrest/arrhythmia
- High-voltage injury
- Deep burns
- Rhabdomyolysis
- Altered consciousness
- Pregnancy

## 8. Follow-up

- ECG monitoring.
- Wound care.
- Rehabilitation.
