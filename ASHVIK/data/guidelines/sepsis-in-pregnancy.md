# Sepsis in Pregnancy and Postpartum

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Pregnancy makes sepsis more dangerous.
- Common sources: uterus, urinary tract, breast, wound.
- **Antibiotics within 1 hour.**
- Refer for obstetric and critical care.

## 2. Clinical features

- Fever/hypothermia
- Tachycardia, tachypnoea
- Hypotension
- Foul lochia
- Abdominal pain
- Confusion

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Vitals | Severity |
| Blood/urine culture | Source |
| CBC, CRP | Infection |
| Ultrasound | Retained products/abscess |
| Lactate | Perfusion |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Oxygen |
| 2 | IV fluids |
| 3 | Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 h |
| 4 | Source control (uterus) |
| 5 | Refer |

**Antibiotics:** piperacillin-tazobactam or ceftriaxone + metronidazole.
**Paracetamol 500 mg** for fever.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Monocef; Flagyl; Augmentin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor urine output
- Uterine evacuation if retained products
- Breast care
- DVT prophylaxis

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Antibiotics:** renal adjustment; allergy.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in kidney injury.
- **Fluids:** overload risk.
- **Vasopressors:** if shock.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Shock
- Altered consciousness
- Oliguria
- Respiratory distress
- Retained products
- Necrotising infection

## 8. Follow-up

- Culture results.
- De-escalation.
- Postpartum care.
