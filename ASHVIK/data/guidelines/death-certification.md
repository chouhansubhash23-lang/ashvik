# Death Certification

> **Source:** Registration of Births and Deaths Act; WHO ICD; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Death certificate is a legal document.
- Record immediate cause, antecedent causes, and underlying cause.
- Do not certify if you did not examine/treat the patient (unless permitted).
- Report unnatural deaths to police.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Confirm death (no pulse, breathing, reflexes).
- Review history and records.
- Determine cause sequence.
- Identify unnatural/medico-legal cases.

## 3. Treatment: certification

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Immediate cause | Final disease/condition |
| Antecedent cause | Conditions leading to it |
| Underlying cause | Root cause |
| Duration | Approximate |
| Manner | Natural/unnatural |

- Use **ICD codes** where required.
- Example: immediate cause "acute myocardial infarction"; underlying "hypertension".
- Use **paracetamol 500 mg** only as an example of documenting treatment, not cause.

## 4. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dose:** not applicable (non-drug topic).
- **Medico-legal cases:** do not issue certificate; inform police.
- **Accuracy:** false certification is an offence.
- **Confidentiality:** share as per law.

## 5. Supportive care

- Inform family with sensitivity.
- Explain certificate process.
- Support bereavement.

## 6. Red flags

- Unnatural death
- Suspected poisoning
- Death within 24 h of admission
- Unknown identity
- Maternal death

## 7. Follow-up

- Register death.
- Maintain records.
- Audit certificates.
