# Snakebite First Aid

> **Source:** National Snakebite Protocol; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- First aid aims to limit venom spread and get the patient to hospital.
- **Do not** cut, suck, burn, or apply a tourniquet.
- Immobilise the limb and remove constricting items.
- Transport urgently.

## 2. Clinical features

- Fang marks, pain, swelling
- Bleeding/coagulopathy
- Ptosis, dysphagia, weakness
- Shock

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Time and site of bite
- Snake description (if safe)
- Symptoms/signs
- 20-minute whole blood clotting test (hospital)

## 4. Treatment: first aid

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Reassure; keep calm |
| 2 | Immobilise limb; splint |
| 3 | Remove rings/bands |
| 4 | Pressure bandage for neurotoxic bites |
| 5 | Transport to ASV centre |
| 6 | Tetanus prophylaxis at hospital |

- **Paracetamol 500 mg** for pain.
- **Do not** give aspirin/NSAIDs if coagulopathy.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Keep warm
- Monitor breathing
- IV access if trained

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Tourniquet:** causes ischemia/necrosis — avoid.
- **Incision/suction:** harmful.
- **Alcohol:** avoid.
- **NSAIDs:** bleeding risk.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Ptosis/breathing difficulty
- Bleeding
- Shock
- Rapidly spreading swelling
- Children/elderly

## 8. Follow-up

- Hospital observation 24 h.
- Repeat clotting test.
- Physiotherapy if weakness.
