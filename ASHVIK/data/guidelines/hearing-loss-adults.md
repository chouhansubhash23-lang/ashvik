# Hearing Loss in Adults

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Hearing loss is common with age and noise exposure.
- Screen with whisper test/audiometry.
- Exclude wax, infection, and drug causes.
- Refer for hearing aids.

## 2. Clinical features

- Difficulty hearing speech
- Tinnitus
- Social withdrawal
- Need for loud TV
- Ear discharge/pain

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Otoscopy | Wax/infection |
| Whisper test | Screening |
| Audiometry | Confirm |
| Tympanometry | Middle ear |
| MRI | Retrocochlear (referral) |

## 4. Treatment

| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Wax | Syringing (if trained) |
| Otitis media | Antibiotics |
| Presbycusis | Hearing aid |
| Noise-induced | Protection |
| Sudden SNHL | Urgent referral |

- **Paracetamol 500 mg** for ear pain.
- **Amoxicillin 500 mg TDS** for infection.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Mox; Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hearing protection
- Communication strategies
- Hearing aid fitting
- Social support

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Aminoglycosides:** ototoxic.
- **Loop diuretics:** ototoxic.
- **Aspirin high dose:** tinnitus.
- **Ear drops:** avoid if perforation.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Sudden hearing loss
- Unilateral hearing loss
- Associated neurology
- Ear bleeding
- Chronic discharge

## 8. Follow-up

- Audiometry follow-up.
- Hearing aid review.
- Treat cause.
