# Pneumonia (Community-Acquired)

> **Source:** ICMR Treatment Guidelines for Antimicrobial Use in Common Syndromes; WHO/IMNCI
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Leading cause of childhood mortality in India.
- Common pathogens: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, atypical bacteria, viruses.
- Risk: age < 5 or > 65, malnutrition, smoking, COPD, diabetes, indoor biomass smoke.

## 2. Clinical features

- Fever, cough with sputum, chest pain
- Breathlessness, fast breathing
- Wheeze, crepitations on auscultation
- Children: chest indrawing, nasal flaring, grunting, unable to feed
- Elderly: confusion, falls, poor appetite may be the only signs

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Respiratory rate + SpO2 | Severity triage |
| Chest X-ray | Confirms consolidation |
| CBC | Leucocytosis |
| Sputum gram stain/culture | If available |
| Blood culture | Severe cases |

**Severity (adult):** SpO2 < 92%, RR > 30, hypotension, confusion, multilobar involvement → refer.

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Adults — outpatient

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | Mox, Novamox | 500 mg three times daily (or 1 g TDS) | 5–7 days |
| Doxycycline (atypical cover) | Doxt, Doxy 100 | 100 mg twice daily | 5–7 days |
| Azithromycin (alternative) | Azee, Azithral | 500 mg once daily | 3 days |

### 4.2 Adults — severe / inpatient

| Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ceftriaxone | 1–2 g IV once daily | Add azithromycin for atypical cover |
| Amoxicillin-clavulanate | 1.2 g IV 8-hourly | Alternative |
| Oxygen | Keep SpO2 ≥ 92% | Essential |

### 4.3 Children (IMNCI)

| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Fast breathing / pneumonia | Oral amoxicillin 40–45 mg/kg/day divided BD for 5 days |
| Severe pneumonia | Refer; IM/IV ampicillin + gentamicin or ceftriaxone |
| Wheeze | Salbutamol nebulisation; assess for asthma |

**Brand examples:** Amoxicillin — Mox; Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Azithromycin — Azee; Salbutamol — Asthalin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hydration, antipyretics (paracetamol)
- Oxygen if hypoxic
- Chest physiotherapy in selected cases
- Treat underlying asthma/COPD
- Vaccination: pneumococcal, influenza, COVID-19 as eligible

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Amoxicillin:** rash with infectious mononucleosis; may reduce OCP efficacy (uncertain); probenecid raises levels.
- **Doxycycline:** avoid in pregnancy and children < 8 years; photosensitivity; antacids/iron reduce absorption (separate 2 h); may increase INR with warfarin.
- **Azithromycin:** QT prolongation with amiodarone/fluoroquinolones/ondansetron; antacids reduce absorption; warfarin interaction.
- **Ceftriaxone:** avoid calcium-containing IV in neonates; not with Ringer's lactate; may potentiate warfarin.
- **Salbutamol:** tachycardia, tremor, hypokalaemia with high doses; caution in arrhythmia.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- SpO2 < 92% or severe respiratory distress
- Chest indrawing, grunting, unable to feed (children)
- Altered consciousness, seizures
- Hypotension / shock
- Confusion in elderly
- Multilobar pneumonia, pleural effusion, empyema
- No improvement after 48–72 h of antibiotics
- Pregnancy with severe pneumonia

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 48–72 hours.
- Repeat X-ray only if not improving or suspected complication.
- Complete the full antibiotic course.
- Counsel on vaccination and smoking cessation.
