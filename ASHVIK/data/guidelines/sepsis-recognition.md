# Sepsis Recognition and Early Management

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; Surviving Sepsis Campaign
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Sepsis = life-threatening organ dysfunction from infection.
- Recognise early with qSOFA/NEWS.
- **Antibiotics within 1 hour** of recognition.
- Fluids and source control save lives.

## 2. Clinical features

- Fever/hypothermia
- Tachycardia, tachypnoea
- Hypotension
- Altered mental state
- Reduced urine output
- Mottled skin

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Vitals | qSOFA |
| Lactate | Perfusion |
| CBC, CRP | Infection |
| Blood culture | Before antibiotics |
| Urine | Source |
| Imaging | Source |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Oxygen if hypoxic |
| 2 | IV fluids 30 mL/kg crystalloid |
| 3 | Antibiotics within 1 h (broad-spectrum) |
| 4 | Vasopressors if hypotension persists |
| 5 | Source control |
| 6 | Refer to higher centre |

**Antibiotics:** ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV (adjust).
**Paracetamol 500 mg** for fever.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Monocef; Flagyl; Noradrenaline.

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor urine output
- Glucose control
- DVT prophylaxis
- Nutrition

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Antibiotics:** renal adjustment, allergy.
- **Vasopressors:** extravasation, arrhythmia.
- **Fluids:** overload in heart failure.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in kidney injury.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hypotension despite fluids
- Lactate > 4 mmol/L
- Altered consciousness
- Oliguria
- Respiratory distress
- Necrotising infection

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess perfusion.
- De-escalate antibiotics after culture.
- Source control.
