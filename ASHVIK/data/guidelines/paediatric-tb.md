# Paediatric Tuberculosis

> **Source:** NTEP; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Children often have paucibacillary TB; diagnosis is clinical.
- Use NTEP paediatric weight bands.
- Screen household contacts.
- **Do not use single drugs.**

## 2. Clinical features

- Persistent cough > 2 weeks
- Fever, weight loss, night sweats
- Poor appetite, failure to thrive
- Lymphadenopathy
- Meningitis (headache, vomiting, neck stiffness)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Suggestive |
| Sputum/gastric aspirate CBNAAT | Confirm |
| Mantoux | Supportive |
| Contact history | Important |
| HIV test | All children |

## 4. Treatment (NTEP)

| Phase | Regimen | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Intensive | HRZE | 2 months |
| Continuation | HRE | 4 months |

**Paediatric FDC weight bands** as per NTEP. Isoniazid 10 mg/kg/day, rifampicin 15 mg/kg/day. Do not crush adult FDC.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paediatric AKT; Pyridoxine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Nutrition support
- Pyridoxine
- Adherence support
- Contact screening
- Notify

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Rifampicin:** enzyme inducer; hepatotoxicity.
- **Isoniazid:** neuropathy (pyridoxine); hepatotoxicity.
- **Pyrazinamide:** hepatotoxicity, hyperuricaemia.
- **Ethambutol:** optic neuritis (vision monitoring).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- TB meningitis
- Severe respiratory distress
- Miliary TB
- Drug-resistant TB
- HIV co-infection
- Severe malnutrition

## 8. Follow-up

- Monthly review.
- Sputum/CBNAAT at 2 months and end.
- Contact screening.
