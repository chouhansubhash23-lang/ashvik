# Antibiotic Stewardship

> **Source:** ICMR Treatment Guidelines for Antimicrobial Use in Common Syndromes; WHO; NCDC AMR
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Antimicrobial resistance is a major threat in India.
- Most viral fevers, colds, and acute diarrhoea **do not need antibiotics**.
- Choose the narrowest effective antibiotic, at the right dose and duration.
- Culture before antibiotics when possible; de-escalate based on results.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment: when antibiotics are indicated

| Condition | Antibiotics? |
|---|---|
| Viral fever/URI | No |
| Acute watery diarrhoea | No (unless cholera) |
| Uncomplicated cystitis | Yes (nitrofurantoin) |
| Pneumonia | Yes (amoxicillin) |
| Typhoid | Yes (azithromycin/ceftriaxone) |
| Cellulitis | Yes (cephalexin) |
| Abscess | Drainage first; antibiotics if cellulitis |
| TB | Yes (NTEP regimen) |

## 3. Principles

1. **Confirm infection** — avoid treating colonisation.
2. **Send cultures** before starting antibiotics when feasible.
3. **Start empirical therapy** based on local resistance if severe.
4. **Review at 48–72 h** — de-escalate or stop.
5. **Use the shortest effective course.**
6. **IV to oral switch** when improving.
7. **Document** indication, drug, dose, duration.
8. **Educate patients** — complete the course, no sharing.

## 4. Treatment: common empirical choices (adult)

| Condition | First-line | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Pneumonia | Amoxicillin 500 mg TDS | Doxycycline/azithromycin |
| Typhoid | Azithromycin 500 mg OD | Ceftriaxone |
| UTI | Nitrofurantoin 100 mg BD | Fosfomycin |
| Cellulitis | Cephalexin 500 mg QID | Clindamycin |
| Cholera | Doxycycline 300 mg single | Azithromycin |
| Dysentery | Ciprofloxacin 500 mg BD | Azithromycin |
| TB | NTEP 4-FDC | — |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Amoxicillin — Mox; Azithromycin — Azee; Nitrofurantoin — Martifur; Cephalexin — Sporidex.

## 5. De-escalation and duration

- Review culture at 48–72 h.
- Narrow spectrum when possible.
- Stop antibiotics if no bacterial infection.
- Typical durations: pneumonia 5–7 days; UTI 5 days; cellulitis 5–7 days; typhoid 7–14 days.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Fluoroquinolones:** QT prolongation, tendon injury, avoid in children/pregnancy.
- **Macrolides:** QT prolongation, CYP3A4 inhibition.
- **Cephalosporins:** allergy; *C. difficile*.
- **Metronidazole:** alcohol reaction.
- **Doxycycline:** pregnancy/children, photosensitivity.
- **Renal/hepatic adjustment** for many antibiotics.

## 7. Red flags — refer/escalate

- Sepsis/septic shock
- No improvement after 48–72 h
- Multidrug-resistant organism suspected
- Deep-seated infection (abscess, osteomyelitis, endocarditis)
- Immunocompromised patient
- Pregnancy with severe infection

## 8. Follow-up

- Review at 48–72 h.
- Culture results and de-escalation.
- Patient education on adherence.
- Audit antibiotic use periodically.
