# Wound Management and Suturing

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Clean wounds can be sutured after thorough irrigation.
- Bites, contaminated wounds, and puncture wounds need special care.
- Tetanus prophylaxis for all wounds.
- **Do not suture infected wounds** — drain and dress.

## 2. Assessment

| Feature | Action |
|---|---|
| Clean, < 6–12 h | Primary closure |
| Contaminated/bite | Irrigation, delayed closure, antibiotics |
| Infected | Drainage, dressing, antibiotics |
| Deep/neurovascular | Refer |
| Foreign body | Remove/exclude |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Wound size, depth, contamination
- Neurovascular status distal to wound
- Tendon/muscle involvement
- Foreign body
- Tetanus status

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Wound care

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Anaesthesia (lidocaine 1–2%) |
| 2 | Irrigation with saline (high pressure) |
| 3 | Debridement of devitalised tissue |
| 4 | Haemostasis |
| 5 | Closure (suture/staples) or dressing |
| 6 | Tetanus prophylaxis |
| 7 | Antibiotics if indicated |

### 4.2 Suturing

- Use non-absorbable suture for skin (e.g., 3-0 nylon).
- Approximate edges without tension.
- Suture removal: face 5 days, scalp 7–10 days, limbs 10–14 days.

### 4.3 Antibiotics

| Wound | Antibiotic |
|---|---|
| Clean | Not routine |
| Contaminated | Amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg TDS x 5–7 days |
| Bite | Amoxicillin-clavulanate + rabies prophylaxis |
| Infected | Culture-directed |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Lidocaine — Xylocaine; Amoxicillin-clavulanate — Augmentin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Analgesia (paracetamol 500 mg)
- Elevation
- Wound review in 48 h
- Keep dry

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Lidocaine:** maximum dose 3 mg/kg (7 mg/kg with adrenaline); avoid intravascular injection.
- **Antibiotics:** allergy, renal adjustment.
- **Tetanus toxoid:** local reaction.
- **NSAIDs:** bleeding, kidney injury.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Neurovascular deficit
- Tendon/joint involvement
- Deep penetrating wound
- Bite with severe tissue loss
- Infected wound with systemic signs
- Compartment syndrome

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 48 h.
- Suture removal as per site.
- Physiotherapy if needed.
