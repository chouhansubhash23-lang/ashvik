# Epilepsy and Seizures

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); National Epilepsy Programme; ILAE
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Seizure = transient abnormal electrical activity; epilepsy = ≥ 2 unprovoked seizures > 24 h apart.
- First seizure needs evaluation for cause (hypoglycaemia, infection, head injury, stroke, toxins).
- **Status epilepticus** is a medical emergency.
- Most epilepsy is controllable with one appropriately chosen drug.

## 2. Clinical features

| Seizure type | Features |
|---|---|
| Generalised tonic-clonic | Loss of consciousness, tonic stiffening, clonic jerking, post-ictal confusion |
| Focal | Motor/sensory/autonomic symptoms with or without awareness |
| Absence | Brief staring, blinking, no post-ictal confusion (children) |
| Myoclonic | Brief jerks |
| Status epilepticus | Seizure > 5 min or repeated without recovery |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Witness history | Most important |
| Blood glucose | Exclude hypoglycaemia |
| Electrolytes, calcium, magnesium | Metabolic cause |
| CBC, LFT, renal function | Baseline before AED |
| EEG | Support diagnosis |
| MRI/CT brain | Structural cause |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 First seizure

- Stabilise; check glucose; exclude infection/toxin.
- Refer for EEG/imaging and driving/occupational advice.
- Do not start long-term AED after a single unprovoked seizure without specialist input.

### 4.2 Generalised tonic-clonic epilepsy

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium valproate | Valparin | 500–1000 mg twice daily | Avoid in women of childbearing age |
| Levetiracetam | Levipil | 500–1000 mg twice daily | Preferred in women/pregnancy |
| Phenytoin | Eptoin | 100 mg three times daily | Narrow index; gum hypertrophy |
| Carbamazepine | Tegretol | 200–400 mg twice daily | Focal seizures |

### 4.3 Focal epilepsy

- Carbamazepine, levetiracetam, phenytoin, or oxcarbazepine.

### 4.4 Status epilepticus

| Step | Drug | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | IV Lorazepam | 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg), repeat once after 5–10 min |
| 2 | IV Phenytoin | 20 mg/kg over 20 min (monitor ECG/BP) |
| 3 | IV Levetiracetam | 60 mg/kg (max 4.5 g) |
| 4 | Refractory | Anaesthesia/ICU |

Refer immediately; protect airway; check glucose.

## 5. Supportive care

- Safety advice: no driving, swimming alone, heights, machinery.
- Sleep hygiene, avoid alcohol, treat fever promptly.
- Pregnancy: folic acid 5 mg daily; plan pregnancy with specialist.
- Epilepsy surgery evaluation for drug-resistant cases.
- School/workplace counselling; reduce stigma.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Valproate:** hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, weight gain, tremor; teratogenic — avoid in women of childbearing age unless essential; interacts with lamotrigine (rash), aspirin.
- **Carbamazepine:** enzyme inducer — reduces OCP efficacy, warfarin, other AEDs; hyponatraemia, rash (SJS).
- **Phenytoin:** zero-order kinetics, narrow index; gum hypertrophy, ataxia, hirsutism; interacts with many drugs.
- **Levetiracetam:** irritability, behavioural change; renal dose adjustment; relatively safe in pregnancy.
- **Lorazepam:** respiratory depression; monitor.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Status epilepticus
- First seizure
- Seizure with fever/neck stiffness (meningitis)
- Seizure after head injury
- Focal deficit / papilloedema
- Pregnancy with seizures
- Seizure in a child < 6 months
- Drug-resistant epilepsy

## 8. Follow-up

- Review every 1–3 months until seizure-free.
- Check adherence and side effects.
- AED levels if available (phenytoin, valproate).
- Do not stop AED abruptly.
- Annual review; consider withdrawal after 2–5 seizure-free years (specialist).
