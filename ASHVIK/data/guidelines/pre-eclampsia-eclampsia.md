# Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia

> **Source:** RMNCH+A / NHM; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Pre-eclampsia = BP ≥ 140/90 + proteinuria after 20 weeks.
- Severe features: BP ≥ 160/110, headache, visual changes, epigastric pain.
- Eclampsia = seizures.
- **Magnesium sulfate prevents/treats seizures.**

## 2. Clinical features

- Hypertension
- Proteinuria
- Headache, blurred vision
- Epigastric pain, vomiting
- Facial/hand swelling
- Seizures (eclampsia)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| BP | Diagnosis |
| Urine protein | Pre-eclampsia |
| Platelets, LFT, creatinine | Severity |
| Fetal monitoring | Wellbeing |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Severe pre-eclampsia | BP control + MgSO4 + refer |
| Eclampsia | MgSO4 + airway + refer |
| BP control | Labetalol 100 mg BD or nifedipine 10 mg |
| MgSO4 | 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM q4h |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Labetalol; Nifedipine; Magnesium sulfate.

## 5. Supportive care

- Left lateral position
- Oxygen
- Monitor reflexes/respiration/urine
- Calcium gluconate ready

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **MgSO4:** respiratory depression, loss of reflexes; monitor.
- **Nifedipine:** hypotension; avoid sublingual.
- **Labetalol:** bronchospasm.
- **ACEi/ARB:** contraindicated.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Seizures
- BP ≥ 160/110
- Pulmonary oedema
- Platelets < 100,000
- Liver/renal dysfunction
- Fetal distress

## 8. Follow-up

- Postpartum BP monitoring.
- Contraception counselling.
- Recurrence risk.
