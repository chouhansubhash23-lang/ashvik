# Geriatric Nutrition

> **Source:** NPHCE; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Malnutrition is common and often missed in older adults.
- Screen with weight loss, BMI, appetite, and MNA.
- Protein needs are higher (1.0–1.5 g/kg/day).
- Treat dental, swallowing, and social causes.

## 2. Clinical features

- Weight loss, loose clothing
- Weakness, fatigue
- Poor appetite
- Dry skin, hair loss
- Swallowing difficulty
- Cognitive decline

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Measure | Use |
|---|---|
| Weight/BMI | Malnutrition |
| MNA | Nutrition screen |
| Albumin | Chronic malnutrition (not specific) |
| Dental exam | Chewing problems |
| Swallow assessment | Dysphagia |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Protein | 1.0–1.5 g/kg/day |
| Calories | 25–30 kcal/kg/day |
| Oral supplements | If intake poor |
| Vitamin D | 800 IU daily |
| Calcium | 500 mg twice daily |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ensure/Protinex; Shelcal; Uprise D3.

## 5. Supportive care

- Dental care
- Swallowing therapy
- Social eating
- Food fortification

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Calcium:** constipation; separate from iron.
- **Vitamin D:** toxicity with excess.
- **Iron:** constipation.
- **Appetite stimulants:** avoid routine.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe weight loss
- Dysphagia
- Dehydration
- Suspected malignancy
- Aspiration pneumonia

## 8. Follow-up

- Monthly weight.
- Dietitian referral.
- Reassess intake.
