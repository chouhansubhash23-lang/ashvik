# Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) Basics

> **Source:** ICMR AMR surveillance; WHO; NCDC
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- AMR is driven by unnecessary and incorrect antibiotic use.
- Culture before antibiotics when possible.
- Use narrow-spectrum agents; complete the course.
- Infection control reduces spread.

## 2. Mechanisms

- Enzymatic inactivation (beta-lactamase, carbapenemase)
- Efflux pumps
- Target modification
- Reduced permeability

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Culture/sensitivity | Guide therapy |
| Antibiogram | Local resistance |
| Molecular tests | Resistance genes (referral) |

## 4. Treatment principles

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| Right drug | Based on culture/local antibiogram |
| Right dose | Weight/renal adjustment |
| Right duration | Shortest effective |
| De-escalation | Narrow after culture |
| Stop | If no bacterial infection |

**Example:** amoxicillin 500 mg TDS for pneumonia; nitrofurantoin 100 mg BD for cystitis.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Mox; Martifur; Azee.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hand hygiene
- Isolation of resistant organisms
- Vaccination
- Patient education

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Broad-spectrum:** resistance, *C. difficile*.
- **Combinations:** only when indicated.
- **Renal adjustment:** avoid toxicity.
- **Self-medication:** discourage.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Sepsis
- No response to antibiotics
- Suspected MDR/XDR
- Immunocompromised

## 8. Follow-up

- Culture results.
- De-escalate.
- Audit prescribing.
