# Newborn Resuscitation

> **Source:** NRP / IAP; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Most newborns breathe with drying and stimulation.
- Use the **golden minute**: warm, dry, stimulate, position.
- Positive pressure ventilation if not breathing.
- Chest compressions if HR < 60 after ventilation.

## 2. Clinical features

- Not crying/breathing
- Poor tone
- HR < 100
- Cyanosis
- Meconium-stained fluid

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Assessment | Action |
|---|---|
| Term? | Decide pathway |
| Breathing/crying? | Stimulate/ventilate |
| Tone? | Assess |
| HR < 100? | PPV |
| HR < 60? | Compressions |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Warm, dry, stimulate |
| 2 | Clear airway if needed |
| 3 | PPV 40–60/min |
| 4 | Chest compressions 3:1 |
| 5 | Adrenaline 0.01–0.03 mg/kg IV/IO if HR < 60 |
| 6 | Refer |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Adrenaline; Oxygen.

## 5. Supportive care

- Keep warm
- Monitor HR/breathing
- Glucose
- Breastfeeding when stable

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Adrenaline:** arrhythmia.
- **Oxygen:** avoid hyperoxia.
- **Naloxone:** not routine.
- **Paracetamol:** not routine in neonates.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- HR < 60 after resuscitation
- Apnoea
- Seizures
- Meconium aspiration
- Preterm

## 8. Follow-up

- Neuro assessment
- Feeding support
- Immunisation
