# Scabies and Fungal Skin Infections

> **Source:** NHP; ICMR/NCDC skin infection guidance; WHO scabies guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **Scabies:** *Sarcoptes scabiei* mite; spreads by prolonged skin-to-skin contact; and entire household must be treated.
- **Fungal (tinea):** dermatophytes; warm, humid skin; common in monsoon.
- **Candidiasis:** *Candida* overgrowth in moist areas.
- Both are common in rural India and often misdiagnosed as allergies.

## 2. Clinical features

| Condition | Features |
|---|---|
| Scabies | Intense itching (worse at night), burrows, papules in web spaces, wrists, axillae, groin; family members itch too |
| Tinea corporis | Ring-shaped scaly plaque with central clearing, itchy |
| Tinea cruris | Itchy rash in groin folds (jock itch) |
| Tinea pedis | Peeling, maceration between toes |
| Tinea capitis | Scalp scaling, hair loss, broken hairs (children) |
| Candidiasis | Red, moist patches with satellite lesions; vaginal discharge in women |
| Secondary bacterial infection | Pain, pus, crusting, fever |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical examination | Usually sufficient |
| Skin scraping + KOH microscopy | Scabies mite/eggs; fungal hyphae |
| Wood's lamp | Some tinea capitis |
| Fungal culture | Recurrent/unusual cases |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Scabies

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose/use | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permethrin 5% cream | Permite, Scabper | Apply neck to toe, wash after 8–12 h | Repeat after 7 days |
| Ivermectin | Ivermec | 200 mcg/kg single dose | Repeat after 1–2 weeks |
| Benzyl benzoate 25% | Generic | Apply neck to toe | 2–3 consecutive nights |
| Cetirizine | Cetzine, Alerid | 10 mg at night | 7 days (itching) |

**Treat all household contacts simultaneously.** Wash clothes/bedding in hot water and dry in sun.

**Ivermectin:** avoid in pregnancy, children < 5 years or < 15 kg (use permethrin).

### 4.2 Fungal infections

| Condition | Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose/use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tinea corporis/cruris/pedis | Clotrimazole 1% cream | Candid | Apply twice daily for 2–4 weeks |
| Extensive tinea | Terbinafine | Terbinafine 250 mg | 250 mg once daily for 2 weeks |
| Tinea capitis | Griseofulvin | Generic | 10–20 mg/kg/day for 6–8 weeks |
| Vaginal candidiasis | Fluconazole | Forcan | 150 mg single dose |
| Oral candidiasis | Nystatin suspension / clotrimazole troches | Mycostatin | 7–14 days |

**Do not use topical steroids alone** — they worsen tinea (tinea incognito).

### 4.3 Secondary bacterial infection

- Oral antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin-clavulanate or cephalexin) if cellulitis/pus.
- Drain abscess if present.

## 5. Supportive care

- Keep skin clean and dry.
- Loose cotton clothing.
- Do not share towels/combs.
- Treat family members and close contacts.
- Avoid scratching; trim nails.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Permethrin:** generally safe; avoid eyes/mucosa; safe in pregnancy (2nd/3rd trimester) and children > 2 months.
- **Ivermectin:** avoid in pregnancy, < 5 years/< 15 kg; caution in liver disease; may cause transient dizziness.
- **Terbinafine:** CYP2D6 inhibitor — increases levels of tricyclics, SSRIs, beta-blockers, codeine; hepatotoxicity (monitor LFT if prolonged); avoid in pregnancy/lactation.
- **Griseofulvin:** enzyme inducer — reduces OCP efficacy, warfarin; avoid in pregnancy; photosensitivity.
- **Fluconazole:** QT prolongation; interacts with warfarin, statins, sulfonylureas; avoid in pregnancy.
- **Clotrimazole:** minimal systemic absorption; local irritation.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Extensive/non-healing lesions in immunocompromised (HIV, diabetes)
- Suspected leprosy (hypopigmented patch with sensory loss)
- Cellulitis with fever, spreading redness
- Crusted/Norwegian scabies
- Tinea capitis or nail involvement
- Severe drug reaction (Stevens-Johnson)
- No improvement after 2–4 weeks

## 8. Follow-up

- Review after 2 weeks.
- Itching may persist 2–4 weeks after successful scabies treatment.
- Confirm household treatment.
- Advise on hygiene and preventing recurrence.
