# Tuberculosis (TB)

> **Source:** NTEP / Central TB Division technical and operational guidelines; WHO TB guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Cause: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (airborne).
- India has the highest TB burden globally.
- Types: pulmonary (most common), extrapulmonary (lymph node, pleural, bone, CNS, abdominal, genital).
- Risk: malnutrition, diabetes, HIV, smoking, alcohol, crowding, silicosis.

## 2. Clinical features

- Cough > 2 weeks
- Evening fever, night sweats
- Weight loss, loss of appetite
- Chest pain, breathlessness
- Haemoptysis (advanced disease)
- Extrapulmonary: lymph node swelling, ascites, back pain, headache, infertility

## 3. Diagnosis (NTEP)

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Sputum smear microscopy | First-line at PHC |
| CBNAAT / GeneXpert | Preferred; detects TB and rifampicin resistance |
| Chest X-ray | Supportive |
| HIV test | All TB patients |
| Blood sugar | All TB patients |
| Drug susceptibility testing | Rifampicin-resistant/retreatment cases |

**Presumptive TB:** cough > 2 weeks, fever > 2 weeks, weight loss, night sweats, haemoptysis, abnormal chest X-ray.

## 4. Treatment (NTEP daily regimen)

### 4.1 New drug-sensitive TB

| Phase | Regimen | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Intensive | Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E) | 2 months |
| Continuation | H + R + E | 4 months |

**Weight-band fixed-dose combinations (FDC)** are used under NTEP. Common FDC: 4-FDC (H 75 mg + R 150 mg + Z 400 mg + E 275 mg) and 3-FDC (H 75 + R 150 + E 275).

| Body weight | 4-FDC (intensive phase) |
|---|---|
| 25–39 kg | 2 tablets daily |
| 40–54 kg | 3 tablets daily |
| 55–69 kg | 4 tablets daily |
| ≥ 70 kg | 5 tablets daily |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** AKT-4, Forecox, Monto-4.

### 4.2 Retreatment / rifampicin-resistant TB

- Refer to NTEP DR-TB centre for bedaquiline-based regimens.
- Do not start an ad-hoc regimen.

### 4.3 Special situations

| Situation | Note |
|---|---|
| Pregnancy | HRZE is safe; add pyridoxine |
| HIV | Start ART within 2–8 weeks; watch for drug interactions |
| Diabetes | Control sugar; monitor response |
| Liver disease | Avoid pyrazinamide if severe; specialist input |
| Children | Weight-band FDC; avoid ethambutol only if vision monitoring impossible |

## 5. Supportive care

- Nutritional support (Nikshay Poshan Yojana)
- Pyridoxine 10–25 mg daily to prevent isoniazid neuropathy
- Adherence support (DOTS/99DOTS/MERM)
- Screen and test household contacts
- Notify every case (Nikshay portal)

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Rifampicin:** potent CYP450 inducer — reduces efficacy of oral contraceptives, warfarin, steroids, oral hypoglycaemics, many ART drugs, phenytoin, theophylline; orange-red urine/tears (harmless); hepatotoxicity.
- **Isoniazid:** peripheral neuropathy (prevent with pyridoxine), hepatotoxicity, may raise phenytoin levels.
- **Pyrazinamide:** hyperuricaemia/gout, hepatotoxicity.
- **Ethambutol:** optic neuritis (colour vision, visual acuity) — baseline and monthly eye check.
- **Combined:** avoid alcohol; monitor LFTs; stop drugs if jaundice/hepatitis develops (follow NTEP protocol).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Haemoptysis (massive)
- Severe breathlessness / SpO2 < 92%
- Meningeal TB (headache, neck stiffness, altered consciousness)
- Spinal TB with neurological deficit
- Suspected MDR/XDR TB
- TB with HIV and CD4 < 200
- Pregnancy with complicated TB
- Drug-induced hepatitis

## 8. Follow-up

- Monthly clinical review; weight and adherence check.
- Sputum/CBNAAT at 2 months and end of treatment.
- Screen contacts; offer preventive therapy as per NTEP.
- Notify and document in Nikshay.
