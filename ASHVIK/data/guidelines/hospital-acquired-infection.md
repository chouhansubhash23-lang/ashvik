# Hospital-Acquired Infection (HAI)

> **Source:** NCDC; WHO; ICMR
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common HAIs: VAP, CLABSI, CAUTI, SSI.
- Prevention bundles are effective.
- Hand hygiene is central.
- Remove devices early.

## 2. Clinical features

| HAI | Features |
|---|---|
| VAP | Fever, purulent secretions, new infiltrate |
| CLABSI | Fever, line site infection |
| CAUTI | Fever, dysuria, cloudy urine |
| SSI | Wound redness, discharge, fever |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Cultures | Blood/urine/sputum/wound |
| CBC/CRP | Infection |
| Imaging | Pneumonia/abscess |
| Device review | Need for continuation |

## 4. Treatment

| HAI | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VAP | Antibiotics per culture; suction |
| CLABSI | Remove line; antibiotics |
| CAUTI | Remove catheter; antibiotics |
| SSI | Drainage; antibiotics |

**Example:** ceftriaxone 1 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV for intra-abdominal SSI.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Monocef; Flagyl.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hand hygiene
- Device bundles
- Isolation
- Nutrition

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Antibiotics:** resistance, renal adjustment.
- **Devices:** remove early.
- **Sedation:** VAP risk.
- **PPIs:** *C. difficile* risk.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Sepsis
- Multidrug-resistant organism
- Necrotising infection
- Device-related endocarditis

## 8. Follow-up

- Culture results.
- Device audit.
- Infection control review.
