# Falls Prevention Programme

> **Source:** NPHCE; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Falls are preventable; screen all older adults yearly.
- Multifactorial intervention reduces falls.
- Review medications, vision, home hazards, gait.
- Vitamin D and exercise help.

## 2. Clinical features

- Previous fall
- Fear of falling
- Dizziness/orthostatic symptoms
- Unsteady gait
- Visual impairment

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Timed up-and-go | Gait/balance |
| BP lying/standing | Orthostatic hypotension |
| Vision/hearing | Impairment |
| cognition/mood | Dementia/depression |
| Homesafety checklist | Hazards |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Exercise | Balance/strength 3x/week |
| Medication review | Stop sedatives |
| Vitamin D | 800 IU daily |
| Calcium | 500 mg twice daily |
| Home modifications | Rails, lighting |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Uprise D3; Shelcal; Paracetamol 500 mg for pain.

## 5. Supportive care

- Physiotherapy
- Vision correction
- Footwear
- Emergency call device

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Sedatives/benzodiazepines:** falls.
- **Antihypertensives:** orthostatic hypotension.
- **Anticholinergics:** confusion.
- **Opioids:** falls/confusion.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Fall with injury/fracture
- Head injury
- Syncope
- Neurological deficit
- Recurrent falls

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess risk.
- Exercise programme.
- Home safety review.
