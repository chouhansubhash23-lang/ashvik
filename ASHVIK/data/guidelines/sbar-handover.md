# SBAR Handover

> **Source:** WHO patient safety; NHP; NHM
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- SBAR = Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation.
- Structured handover reduces errors.
- Use for referrals, shift changes, and escalations.
- Be concise and specific.

## 2. SBAR structure

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| Situation | Who, what, why now |
| Background | History, meds, allergies |
| Assessment | Vitals, findings, impression |
| Recommendation | What you need/next steps |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Identify urgency.
- Gather key data.
- Confirm receiver.

## 4. Treatment: example

- **S:** 45-year-old male with fever and hypotension.
- **B:** 3 days fever, no comorbidities.
- **A:** Suspected sepsis, BP 90/60, HR 120.
- **R:** IV fluids, antibiotics, transfer to CHC.

**Example drug:** ceftriaxone 1 g IV; paracetamol 500 mg.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Monocef; Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Write handover note.
- Confirm understanding (read-back).
- Document time.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Incomplete info:** errors.
- **Assumptions:** verify.
- **Noise/distraction:** use structured form.
- **Confidentiality:** protect data.

## 7. Red flags — escalate immediately

- Deteriorating patient
- Missing critical information
- No receiver available
- Medication discrepancy

## 8. Follow-up

- Confirm actions.
- Update record.
- Debrief.
