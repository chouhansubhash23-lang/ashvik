# Drowning and Electrocution

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; National Health Portal
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Drowning: respiratory impairment from submersion/immersion.
- Electrocution: injury from electric current; can cause arrhythmia and deep tissue damage.
- **Start CPR immediately** for unresponsive/non-breathing victims.
- Assume cervical spine injury in diving/water accidents.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

### Clinical features
- Cough, breathlessness
- Hypoxia, cyanosis
- Altered consciousness
- Hypothermia
- Aspiration/pneumonia

### Management
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Remove from water safely; protect rescuer |
| 2 | Check breathing/pulse; start CPR if needed |
| 3 | Give 5 rescue breaths first (drowning) |
| 4 | Oxygen, suction |
| 5 | Cervical spine immobilisation if trauma |
| 6 | Refer to hospital |

- Do not attempt to drain water from lungs.
- Monitor for secondary drowning (24–48 h).

## 3. Treatment: drowning and electrocution

### Electrocution

### Clinical features
- Burns at entry/exit
- Cardiac arrhythmia (VF/asystole)
- Muscle contraction, fractures
- Deep tissue injury
- Loss of consciousness

### Management
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Switch off power** before touching victim |
| 2 | ABCDE; CPR if needed |
| 3 | ECG monitoring |
| 4 | Treat burns |
| 5 | IV fluids; refer |

- High-voltage injury may cause rhabdomyolysis and kidney injury.
- Monitor urine output and CK.

## 4. Supportive care

- Oxygen
- Warmth (hypothermia)
- Analgesia
- Tetanus prophylaxis
- Psychological support

## 5. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Adrenaline:** for cardiac arrest; follow CPR protocol.
- **Analgesics:** paracetamol 500 mg or opioid as needed.
- **Antibiotics:** only for infected wounds.
- **IV fluids:** cautious in drowning (pulmonary oedema).

## 6. Red flags — refer immediately

- Cardiac arrest/arrhythmia
- Hypoxia/respiratory distress
- Altered consciousness
- High-voltage electrical injury
- Deep burns
- Suspected cervical spine injury
- Hypothermia
- Pregnancy

## 7. Follow-up

- Observe 24 h after near-drowning.
- ECG after electrocution.
- Wound care and rehabilitation.
- Counsel on water/electricity safety.
