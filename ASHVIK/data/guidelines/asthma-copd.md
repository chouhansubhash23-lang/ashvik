# Asthma and COPD

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); GINA/GOLD; NPCDCS
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **Asthma:** reversible airway obstruction; allergic/inflammatory; often starts in childhood.
- **COPD:** persistent airflow limitation, usually from smoking or biomass (chulha) smoke exposure.
- Both present with wheeze, cough, breathlessness.
- Spirometry is needed to confirm; where unavailable, treat empirically and refer.

## 2. Clinical features

| Feature | Asthma | COPD |
|---|---|---|
| Age of onset | Often childhood | Usually > 40 years |
| Smoking/biomass | May be absent | Usually present |
| Symptoms | Episodic wheeze, night cough, triggers | Progressive breathlessness, chronic cough, sputum |
| Reversibility | Significant | Limited |
| Allergies | Common | Less common |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Peak expiratory flow (PEF) | Before/after bronchodilator |
| Spirometry | Confirms obstruction and reversibility |
| SpO2 | Severity |
| Chest X-ray | Exclude pneumonia/TB |
| CBC, eosinophils | Asthma phenotype |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Asthma

| Step | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild intermittent | Salbutamol inhaler 2 puffs as needed |
| Persistent | Low-dose inhaled corticosteroid (budesonide 200 mcg BD) |
| Uncontrolled | ICS + LABA (budesonide-formoterol) |
| Severe exacerbation | Prednisolone 40–50 mg daily x 5 days + salbutamol nebulisation; oxygen; refer |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Salbutamol — Asthalin; Budesonide — Budecort; Budesonide-formoterol — Foracort, Symbicort.

### 4.2 COPD

| Step | Treatment |
|---|---|
| All patients | Stop smoking/biomass; vaccination; pulmonary rehab |
| Symptomatic | Short-acting bronchodilator (salbutamol/ipratropium) |
| Persistent | Long-acting bronchodilator (tiotropium 18 mcg OD or formoterol) |
| Exacerbation | Prednisolone 40 mg x 5 days + bronchodilators + antibiotics if infection; oxygen (target SpO2 88–92%) |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Tiotropium — Spiriva; Ipratropium — Ipravent; Deriphyllin (etophylline + theophylline).

### 4.3 Inhaler technique

- Use a spacer with MDI.
- Teach and check technique at every visit.
- Rinse mouth after inhaled steroid.

## 5. Supportive care

- Avoid triggers: dust, smoke, pollen, cold air.
- Annual influenza vaccine; pneumococcal vaccine in COPD.
- Smoking cessation counselling (brief advice at every visit).
- Written action plan for asthma.
- Home nebuliser if available.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Beta-blockers** (including eye drops) can precipitate bronchospasm — avoid in asthma/COPD.
- **Salbutamol:** tachycardia, tremor, hypokalaemia with high doses; caution in arrhythmia.
- **Inhaled steroids:** oral candidiasis, hoarseness; rinse mouth.
- **Oral steroids:** hyperglycaemia, hypertension, infection, osteoporosis with long use; short course preferred.
- **Theophylline:** narrow therapeutic index; interacts with ciprofloxacin, clarithromycin, rifampicin; monitor if available.
- **Tiotropium:** dry mouth, urinary retention, glaucoma caution.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe breathlessness, unable to speak full sentences
- SpO2 < 92% (asthma) or < 88% (COPD)
- Cyanosis, silent chest, exhaustion
- Altered consciousness, confusion
- Peak flow < 50% predicted
- Suspected pneumothorax
- No response to bronchodilators

## 8. Follow-up

- Review every 1–3 months until controlled.
- Check inhaler technique and adherence.
- Spirometry yearly if available.
- Vaccination and smoking cessation at every visit.
