# Incision and Drainage — Variations

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Standard I&D: incise, drain, irrigate, pack.
- Variations: loop drainage, marsupialisation, catheter drainage.
- Choose based on size, location, and contents.
- Aseptic technique and anaesthesia are essential.

## 2. Indications

- Cutaneous/subcutaneous abscess
- Breast abscess (refer if lactation)
- Pilonidal abscess
- Perianal abscess (refer)
- Bartholin abscess

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Size, location, fluctuance
- Overlying skin condition
- Systemic signs
- Diabetes/immunosuppression
- Consent

## 4. Treatment: techniques

| Technique | Use |
|---|---|
| Simple I&D | Most abscesses |
| Loop drainage | Small abscess, avoids packing |
| Catheter drainage | Large cavity |
| Marsupialisation | Bartholin/pilonidal |
| Packing | Large/deep cavity |

### Steps

1. Local anaesthesia (lidocaine 1–2%)
2. Incise at dependent point
3. Drain and break loculations
4. Irrigate
5. Pack/loop/catheter
6. Dress and review

**Antibiotics:** amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg TDS if cellulitis.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Augmentin; Lidocaine — Xylocaine.

## 5. Supportive care

- Analgesia (paracetamol 500 mg)
- Warm compress
- Diabetes control
- Wound review in 24–48 h

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Lidocaine:** max dose; aspirate.
- **Antibiotics:** allergy, renal adjustment.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk.
- **Perianal/breast abscess:** specialist referral.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Perianal/breast/face abscess
- Necrotising infection
- Sepsis
- Immunocompromised
- No improvement after drainage

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24–48 h.
- Remove pack/loop when healed.
- Wound care education.
