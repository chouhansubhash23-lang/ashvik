# Anaphylaxis

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; National Health Portal; allergy society guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Anaphylaxis = severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction.
- **Adrenaline IM is the first-line treatment — give it immediately.**
- Common triggers: drugs (penicillin, NSAIDs), foods, insect stings, contrast.
- Do not delay adrenaline for antihistamines or steroids.

## 2. Clinical features

- Sudden onset after exposure
- Urticaria, flushing, angioedema
- Breathlessness, wheeze, stridor
- Hypotension, syncope, tachycardia
- Abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea
- Feeling of impending doom

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical diagnosis | Rapid onset + airway/breathing/circulation involvement |
| BP, pulse, SpO2 | Severity |
| ECG | Arrhythmia |
| Tryptase | Confirm (later, if available) |

## 4. Treatment (immediate)

| Step | Drug | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Adrenaline IM** | 0.5 mg (0.5 mL of 1:1000) into anterolateral thigh; repeat every 5 min if needed |
| 2 | Oxygen | High-flow; airway support |
| 3 | IV fluids | 0.9% saline bolus 500–1000 mL (adult) |
| 4 | Antihistamine | Cetirizine 10 mg or chlorpheniramine 10 mg IM (adjunct, not first-line) |
| 5 | Steroid | Hydrocortisone 200 mg IV (adjunct) |
| 6 | Bronchodilator | Salbutamol nebulisation if wheeze |

**Children:** adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg IM (max 0.5 mg).

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Adrenaline — Adrenaline; Cetirizine — Cetzine; Hydrocortisone — Efcorlin; Salbutamol — Asthalin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Lie flat with legs elevated (unless breathing difficulty).
- Monitor BP, pulse, SpO2.
- Remove trigger (stop drug, remove stinger).
- Observe for biphasic reaction (4–12 h).
- Refer to hospital after stabilisation.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Adrenaline:** tachycardia, arrhythmia, hypertension; caution in CAD/elderly but do not withhold in anaphylaxis.
- **Antihistamines:** sedation; do not replace adrenaline.
- **Steroids:** hyperglycaemia; delayed onset.
- **Beta-blockers:** may blunt adrenaline response — consider glucagon in refractory cases (specialist).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Airway obstruction/stridor
- Hypotension/shock
- SpO2 < 92%
- Altered consciousness
- Refractory reaction
- Biphasic reaction
- Pregnancy with anaphylaxis

## 8. Follow-up

- Allergy referral for testing.
- Adrenaline auto-injector if available.
- Avoid trigger; medical alert card.
- Education on early adrenaline use.
