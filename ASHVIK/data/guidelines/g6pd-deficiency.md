# G6PD Deficiency

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- X-linked enzyme deficiency; triggers hemolysis.
- Common triggers: primaquine, sulfa drugs, nitrofurantoin, fava beans, naphthalene.
- Avoid triggers; treat hemolysis supportively.
- Screen before primaquine in malaria.

## 2. Clinical features

- Jaundice, dark urine
- Pallor, fatigue
- Abdominal/back pain
- Hemolytic crisis after trigger

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| G6PD assay | Diagnosis |
| CBC/reticulocytes | Hemolysis |
| Bilirubin | Jaundice |
| Urine | Hemoglobinuria |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Acute hemolysis | Stop trigger; hydration |
| Severe anemia | Transfusion |
| Malaria | Avoid primaquine; use alternative |
| Paracetamol 500 mg | For pain/fever (safe) |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Dolo 650; Folic acid 5 mg.

## 5. Supportive care

- Avoid triggers
- Hydration
- Folic acid
- Patient education card

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Primaquine:** hemolysis — contraindicated.
- **Sulfonamides:** hemolysis.
- **Nitrofurantoin:** hemolysis.
- **Naphthalene/fava beans:** avoid.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe anemia
- Hemoglobinuria
- Shock
- Renal failure

## 8. Follow-up

- Avoid triggers.
- Genetic counselling.
- Monitor Hb.
