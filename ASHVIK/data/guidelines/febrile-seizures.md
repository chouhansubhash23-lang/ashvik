# Febrile Seizures

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common in children 6 months–5 years with fever.
- Simple febrile seizure: generalised, < 15 min, once in 24 h.
- Complex: focal, > 15 min, recurrent.
- Reassure parents; most have no long-term harm.

## 2. Clinical features

- Generalised tonic-clonic movements
- Loss of consciousness
- Fever (often the first sign)
- Post-ictal drowsiness
- Complex features: focal, prolonged, recurrent

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Confirm fever and seizure type
- Exclude meningitis (neck stiffness, bulging fontanelle)
- Check glucose
- Consider malaria/dengue if endemic

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Ongoing seizure | IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg), repeat once |
| After seizure | Paracetamol 15 mg/kg/dose |
| Complex/recurrent | Refer |
| Meningitis suspected | Antibiotics + refer |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Lorazepam — Ativan; Paracetamol — Calpol.

**Do not** give aspirin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Recovery position
- Maintain airway
- Cool environment
- Parental reassurance

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Lorazepam:** respiratory depression; monitor.
- **Paracetamol:** max 4 doses/day.
- **Antipyretics:** do not prevent recurrence.
- **Sedatives:** avoid.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Seizure > 15 min
- Focal seizure
- Recurrent in 24 h
- Meningism
- Age < 6 months
- Post-ictal deficit
- Status epilepticus

## 8. Follow-up

- Parent education
- Fever management
- Neurology referral if complex/recurrent
