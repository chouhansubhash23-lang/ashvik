# Smoking Cessation

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; National Tobacco Control Programme
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Brief advice at every visit increases quit rates.
- Use the 5A approach: Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange.
- Combine counselling + pharmacotherapy.
- Relapse is common — re-engage.

## 2. Clinical features

- Tobacco use (smoked/smokeless)
- Cough, breathlessness
- Oral lesions
- Cardiovascular risk
- Dependence (Fagerström)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Assessment | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tobacco history | Type, amount, duration |
| Dependence | Time to first cigarette |
| Readiness | Stage of change |
| Comorbidities | COPD/CVD/cancer |
| Mental health | Depression |

## 4. Treatment

| Option | Dose |
|---|---|
| Nicotine replacement | Patch 21 mg/24 h or gum 2–4 mg |
| Varenicline | 0.5 mg OD → 1 mg BD (specialist) |
| Bupropion | 150 mg OD → BD (specialist) |
| Behavioural support | Counselling |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** NicoDerm; Nicorette; Champix; Zyban.

- Set a quit date.
- Follow up in 1 week and 1 month.

## 5. Supportive care

- Counselling (5A/5R)
- Quitline support
- Family support
- Avoid triggers

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Varenicline:** nausea, neuropsychiatric effects.
- **Bupropion:** seizures, insomnia.
- **NRT:** caution in CVD (but safer than smoking).
- **Counselling:** essential.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Chest pain
- Haemoptysis
- Suspected cancer
- Severe psychiatric symptoms

## 8. Follow-up

- Review quit date.
- Manage withdrawal.
- Relapse prevention.
