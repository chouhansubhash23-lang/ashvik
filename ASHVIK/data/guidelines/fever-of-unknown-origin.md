# Fever of Unknown Origin (FUO)

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- FUO = fever > 3 weeks without diagnosis after initial workup.
- Common causes in India: TB, malaria, enteric fever, brucellosis, HIV, lymphoma, connective tissue disease.
- Reassess history/exam repeatedly.
- Avoid empirical antibiotics without cultures.

## 2. Clinical features

- Prolonged fever
- Weight loss, night sweats
- Lymphadenopathy
- Hepatosplenomegaly
- Rash, joint pain
- Respiratory symptoms

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| CBC, ESR/CRP | Inflammation |
| Blood culture | Bacteraemia |
| Malaria/dengue/typhoid | Endemic infections |
| TB (CBNAAT/CXR) | TB |
| HIV | Screening |
| Urine | UTI |
| Imaging | Abscess/lymphoma |

## 4. Treatment

| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| TB | NTEP regimen |
| Malaria | ACT |
| Enteric fever | Azithromycin 500 mg |
| Brucellosis | Doxycycline 100 mg BD x 6 weeks |
| Unknown | Avoid blind antibiotics; refer |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Azee; Doxt; Monocef.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hydration
- Nutrition
- Antipyretics (paracetamol 500 mg)
- Psychosocial support

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Empirical antibiotics:** obscure diagnosis/resistance.
- **Steroids:** may mask lymphoma/TB.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in dengue.
- **Antimalarials:** confirm before use.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Weight loss with lymphadenopathy
- Pancytopenia
- Persistent hypotension
- New murmur
- Focal neurology

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess weekly.
- Repeat cultures/imaging.
- Refer if undiagnosed.
