# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

> **Source:** ICMR Treatment Guidelines for Antimicrobial Use in Common Syndromes; NCDC
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common in women; men and children with UTI need evaluation for structural causes.
- Pathogens: *E. coli* (most common), *Klebsiella*, *Proteus*, *Enterococcus*, *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*.
- Risk: female sex, pregnancy, diabetes, stones, obstruction, catheter, poor hygiene.

## 2. Clinical features

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Cystitis | Burning urination, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain, cloudy urine |
| Pyelonephritis | Fever, chills, flank pain, vomiting, loin tenderness |
| Urethritis | Urethral discharge, dysuria (consider STI) |
| Asymptomatic bacteriuria | No symptoms; treat in pregnancy only |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Urine dipstick | Nitrite, leucocyte esterase |
| Urine microscopy | Pus cells, RBCs |
| Urine culture + sensitivity | Recurrent, complicated, pregnancy, pyelonephritis |
| Ultrasound KUB | Stones, obstruction, recurrent UTI |
| Pregnancy test | All women of reproductive age |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Uncomplicated cystitis (adult)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrofurantoin | Martifur | 100 mg twice daily | 5 days |
| Fosfomycin | Monuril | 3 g single dose | Once |
| Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole | Septran | 160/800 mg twice daily | 3 days (only if local resistance < 20%) |
| Pivmecillinam | Generic | 400 mg three times daily | 3–5 days |

### 4.2 Pyelonephritis (adult)

| Drug | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Ciprofloxacin | 500 mg twice daily | 7 days |
| Ceftriaxone (severe) | 1–2 g IV once daily | 10–14 days |
| Gentamicin (if indicated) | Weight-based | Monitor renal function |

**Refer** if vomiting, sepsis, obstruction, pregnancy, or no improvement in 48–72 h.

### 4.3 Special situations

| Situation | Note |
|---|---|
| Pregnancy | Treat asymptomatic bacteriuria; avoid nitrofurantoin after 36 weeks; avoid fluoroquinolones |
| Children | Urine culture before antibiotics; evaluate for vesicoureteric reflux |
| Men | Consider prostatitis/obstruction; longer course |
| Catheter-associated | Remove/replace catheter; culture-directed therapy |

## 5. Supportive care

- Increase oral fluids
- Urinate frequently; complete emptying
- Perineal hygiene
- Analgesia (paracetamol) for dysuria
- Avoid bladder irritants (caffeine, alcohol)

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Nitrofurantoin:** avoid in G6PD deficiency, renal impairment (eGFR < 30), and at term pregnancy; may cause pulmonary toxicity with long use; interacts with antacids (reduce absorption).
- **Fosfomycin:** well tolerated; avoid with metoclopramide (reduces levels).
- **TMP-SMX:** sulfa allergy, G6PD deficiency, pregnancy (3rd trimester), interacts with warfarin, methotrexate, phenytoin; hyperkalaemia with ACEi/ARB.
- **Ciprofloxacin:** QT prolongation, tendon rupture, avoid in children/pregnancy unless needed; antacids/iron reduce absorption.
- **Ceftriaxone:** see pneumonia/typhoid cautions.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Fever with flank pain and vomiting (pyelonephritis)
- Sepsis / hypotension
- Suspected obstruction (anuria, severe pain)
- Pregnancy with UTI
- Male UTI with systemic symptoms
- Renal abscess / emphysematous pyelonephritis (diabetes)
- No improvement after 48–72 h
- Recurrent UTI with stones or structural abnormality

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess in 48–72 hours.
- Urine culture if recurrent or not improving.
- Treat causes (diabetes, stones, hygiene).
- Avoid unnecessary antibiotics to limit resistance.
