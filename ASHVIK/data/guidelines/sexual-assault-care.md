# Sexual Assault Care

> **Source:** WHO; MoHFW medico-legal guidelines; NACO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Sexual assault is a medical emergency and a crime.
- Provide **confidential, non-judgmental, survivor-centred care**.
- Offer STI prophylaxis, emergency contraception, and psychological support.
- Forensic examination requires informed consent.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment and immediate priorities

| Priority | Action |
|---|---|
| Safety | Ensure privacy and safety |
| Consent | Explain each step; obtain informed consent |
| Injuries | Treat life-threatening injuries first |
| Forensic | Collect evidence if consented |
| Prophylaxis | HIV, STI, pregnancy, tetanus |
| Support | Counselling, police/legal, social support |

## 3. Treatment: medical care

### 3.1 STI prophylaxis

| Infection | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Gonorrhoea/chlamydia | Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g oral |
| Trichomonas/bacterial vaginosis | Metronidazole 2 g oral |
| Syphilis | Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM (or test-based) |
| Hepatitis B | Vaccine ± immunoglobulin |
| HIV | PEP within 72 h (refer/start as per NACO) |

### 3.2 Emergency contraception

- Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg within 72 h (or copper IUCD within 5 days).

### 3.3 Tetanus

- Tetanus toxoid if indicated.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Azithromycin — Azee; Metronidazole — Flagyl; Levonorgestrel — i-pill.

## 4. Forensic examination

- With consent, examine and document injuries.
- Collect swabs/samples as per protocol.
- Maintain chain of custody.
- Provide medical report; do not insist on police complaint before treatment.
- Respect confidentiality.

## 5. Psychological support

- Reassure; avoid victim-blaming.
- Screen for depression, anxiety, PTSD.
- Refer to counsellor/mental health services.
- Follow-up support.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **HIV PEP:** adherence, side effects (nausea, hepatotoxicity); refer.
- **Ceftriaxone/azithromycin:** allergy; QT.
- **Metronidazole:** alcohol reaction.
- **Emergency contraception:** nausea; not for regular use.
- **Benzathine penicillin:** anaphylaxis.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe injuries/bleeding
- Shock
- Suspected internal injury
- Pregnancy
- HIV exposure
- Suicidal ideation
- Child sexual abuse
- Trafficking/coercion

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 2 weeks and 3 months (HIV/syphilis).
- Pregnancy test if needed.
- Mental health follow-up.
- Legal/social support.
