# TB Preventive Therapy (TPT)

> **Source:** NTEP; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- TPT prevents progression from latent TB infection to active TB.
- Offer to household contacts of TB patients and high-risk groups.
- **Rule out active TB before starting TPT.**
- Adherence and follow-up are essential.

## 2. Indications

- Household contacts of pulmonary TB patients
- Children < 5 years (contact)
- People with HIV
- Immunosuppressed (steroids, biologics, transplant)
- Silicosis
- Healthcare workers (as per policy)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Screen for active TB (cough, fever, weight loss, night sweats)
- Chest X-ray/CBNAAT if symptomatic
- HIV test
- Contraindications: active hepatitis, pregnancy (relative)

## 4. Treatment

| Regimen | Duration |
|---|---|
| Isoniazid 6H | 6 months |
| Isoniazid + rifapentine weekly (3HP) | 3 months (if available) |
| Rifampicin 4R | 4 months |

**Isoniazid dose:** 5 mg/kg/day (max 300 mg); children 10 mg/kg/day.
**Rifampicin:** 10 mg/kg/day (max 600 mg).

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Isoniazid; Rifampicin — R-Cin.

## 5. Supportive care

- Adherence support
- Pyridoxine 10–25 mg daily with isoniazid
- Monitor for hepatitis
- Notify NTEP

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Isoniazid:** peripheral neuropathy (prevent with pyridoxine), hepatotoxicity.
- **Rifampicin:** enzyme inducer — reduces OCP, warfarin, ART, steroids; hepatotoxicity.
- **Alcohol:** avoid.
- **Pregnancy:** specialist advice; 6H may be used.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Symptoms of active TB
- Jaundice/hepatitis
- Severe rash
- Pregnancy
- HIV with low CD4

## 8. Follow-up

- Monthly review.
- Adherence check.
- Screen for active TB if symptoms.

## 9. Sources

## 10. Disclaimer

Decision-support summary only. Rule out active TB before TPT. Brand names are illustrative, not promotional.
