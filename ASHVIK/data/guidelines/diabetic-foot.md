# Diabetic Foot

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NPCDCS; IWGDF
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Diabetic foot = infection, ulceration, or destruction of deep tissues associated with neuropathy and/or peripheral arterial disease.
- Leading cause of non-traumatic lower limb amputation.
- **Offloading** and infection control are essential.
- Screen feet at every visit.

## 2. Clinical features

- Callus, fissures, dry skin
- Ulcer (plantar, toe, heel)
- Erythema, warmth, swelling
- Pus, foul smell, crepitus
- Reduced sensation (monofilament)
- Absent pulses, cold foot, gangrene
- Deformity (Charcot foot)

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Foot exam | Ulcer, infection, pulses, sensation |
| Probe-to-bone | Osteomyelitis suspicion |
| X-ray | Osteomyelitis, gas |
| CBC, CRP, sugar | Infection control |
| Wound culture | Deep infection |
| Doppler/ABI | Peripheral arterial disease |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Offloading

- Total contact cast or removable cast walker.
- Bed rest with heel protection.
- Avoid weight-bearing on the ulcer.

### 4.2 Wound care

- Debridement of callus/necrosis.
- Saline dressing; avoid antiseptics that impair healing.
- Moist wound healing.
- Treat fungal infection between toes.

### 4.3 Infection

| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild (superficial) | Oral amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg TDS x 7–10 days |
| Moderate/severe | IV antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam/ceftriaxone + metronidazole) + refer |
| Osteomyelitis | Prolonged IV/oral antibiotics; surgical referral |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Amoxicillin-clavulanate — Augmentin; Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Metronidazole — Flagyl.

### 4.4 Vascular assessment

- Absent pulses/gangrene → urgent vascular referral.
- Revascularisation may be needed.

## 5. Supportive care

- Glycaemic control (insulin if needed).
- Nutrition and hydration.
- Tetanus prophylaxis.
- Pressure relief.
- Education on foot care.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Antibiotics:** renal dose adjustment; *C. difficile* risk.
- **Metformin:** hold in severe infection/AKI.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid (kidney injury, mask infection).
- **Steroids:** worsen glycaemia; avoid unless indicated.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Gangrene
- Spreading cellulitis/sepsis
- Osteomyelitis
- Absent pulses/critical ischemia
- Deep abscess/crepitus
- Charcot foot with deformity
- Uncontrolled diabetes

## 8. Follow-up

- Review wound every 2–7 days.
- Repeat debridement as needed.
- Footwear modification.
- Annual foot screening; daily self-inspection.
