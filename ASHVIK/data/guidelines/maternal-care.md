# Maternal Care (ANC, Anaemia, Pre-eclampsia)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); RMNCH+A / NHM; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Early antenatal registration and at least 4 ANC visits improve outcomes.
- Anaemia and hypertensive disorders are the leading preventable causes of maternal death in India.
- Danger signs must trigger immediate referral.
- Link every pregnancy to the ASHA/ANM and JSY/PMSMA scheme.

## 2. Antenatal schedule

| Visit | Timing | Key actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before 12 weeks | Register, history, BP, weight, Hb, urine, blood group, HIV/HBsAg/VDRL, TT/Td, IFA + calcium, folic acid |
| 2 | 14–20 weeks | BP, weight, Hb, fetal heart, anomaly scan (18–20 weeks) |
| 3 | 28–32 weeks | BP, Hb, urine, growth scan, repeat Hb |
| 4 | 36–40 weeks | BP, presentation, birth plan, danger-sign counselling |

- IFA: 1 tablet daily (60 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg folic acid) from 2nd trimester for 180 days.
- Calcium: 500 mg twice daily from 14 weeks.
- TT/Td: 2 doses or 1 booster as per schedule.

## 4. Treatment: anaemia in pregnancy

| Hb | Category | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 11 g/dL | Normal | Prophylactic IFA |
| 10–10.9 | Mild | Oral iron + folic acid; recheck 4 weeks |
| 7–9.9 | Moderate | Oral iron; consider IV iron; recheck 2–4 weeks |
| < 7 | Severe | Refer; IV iron/transfusion; hospital delivery |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Autrin, Orofer; IV ferric carboxymaltose — Ferinject, Encicarb.

## 5. Pre-eclampsia / eclampsia

### Diagnosis
- BP ≥ 140/90 on two occasions + proteinuria (or severe features).
- Severe features: BP ≥ 160/110, headache, blurred vision, epigastric pain, vomiting, reduced urine, breathlessness, low platelets, raised liver enzymes.

### Management
| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Pre-eclampsia without severe features | Refer to CHC/district; monitor BP, urine, fetal wellbeing |
| Severe pre-eclampsia | **Refer immediately**; BP control (labetalol/nifedipine) as per protocol |
| Eclampsia | Magnesium sulfate + emergency referral |

**Magnesium sulfate (Pritchard regimen):**
- Loading: 4 g IV over 20 min + 10 g IM (5 g in each buttock).
- Maintenance: 5 g IM every 4 hours for 24 hours.
- Monitor: respiration, urine output, reflexes; keep calcium gluconate ready.

**Brand examples:** Labetalol 100 mg BD; Nifedipine 10 mg (not sublingual).

## 6. Danger signs — refer immediately

- Vaginal bleeding
- Severe headache, blurred vision, convulsions
- High fever, foul discharge
- Reduced fetal movements
- Leaking fluid before term
- Severe abdominal pain
- Breathlessness
- Swelling of face/hands with high BP
- Prolonged labour / obstructed labour
- Postpartum haemorrhage

## 7. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Iron:** separate from calcium/antacids by 2 hours; constipation.
- **Calcium:** may reduce iron absorption if taken together.
- **Labetalol:** bradycardia, bronchospasm; avoid in asthma.
- **Nifedipine:** hypotension, headache, flushing; avoid sublingual route.
- **Magnesium sulfate:** respiratory depression, loss of reflexes, oliguria — monitor and keep calcium gluconate ready.
- **ACEi/ARB:** contraindicated in pregnancy.

## 8. Postpartum care

- Monitor BP, bleeding, infection.
- Breastfeeding support; contraception counselling.
- Postpartum iron for 6 months.
- Screen for postpartum depression.
- Newborn care: breastfeeding, warmth, immunisation.

## 9. Follow-up

- ANC visits as scheduled; more frequent if high risk.
- Birth preparedness plan and referral transport.
- Postnatal visits: day 1, 3, 7, 14, 28.
- Link to government schemes (JSY, PMSMA, Nikshay if TB).
