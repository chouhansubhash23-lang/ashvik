# Fractures — General Approach

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Assess ABCDE first; then the limb.
- Immobilise before transport.
- Check distal pulses, sensation, and movement before/after splinting.
- Open fractures need antibiotics and urgent referral.

## 2. Clinical features

- Pain, swelling, deformity
- Inability to bear weight/use limb
- Crepitus
- Abnormal mobility
- Open wound with bone exposure
- Neurovascular deficit (pulse, sensation, capillary refill)
- Compartment syndrome: pain out of proportion, tense compartment

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical exam | Deformity, neurovascular status |
| X-ray (2 views) | Confirm fracture |
| CT | Complex/joint fractures (referral) |
| CBC, coagulation | Pre-op |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 First aid

- Analgesia: paracetamol 500 mg/or opioid
- Immobilise with splint in the position found
- Cover open wounds with sterile dressing
- Tetanus prophylaxis
- IV fluids if shocked

### 4.2 Open fracture

- Antibiotics: ceftriaxone 1 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV
- Debridement and surgical referral
- Do not push bone back in

### 4.3 Definitive care

- Closed reduction and cast (if trained)
- Surgical fixation for unstable/open/joint fractures
- Refer if neurovascular deficit

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Metronidazole — Flagyl; Paracetamol — Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Elevate limb (after ruling out compartment syndrome)
- Ice for swelling
- Pain control
- DVT prophylaxis as indicated
- Physiotherapy after immobilisation

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **NSAIDs:** may impair bone healing; avoid in kidney injury.
- **Opioids:** sedation, constipation.
- **Antibiotics:** renal adjustment.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Open fracture
- Neurovascular deficit
- Compartment syndrome
- Joint dislocation
- Pathological fracture
- Polytrauma/shock
- Spine fracture with deficit

## 8. Follow-up

- X-ray after reduction.
- Cast/clinic review.
- Physiotherapy.
- Remove hardware as advised.
