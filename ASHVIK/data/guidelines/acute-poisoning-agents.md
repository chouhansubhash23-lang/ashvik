# Acute Poisoning — Common Agents

> **Source:** NCDC poisoning guidance; ICMR; WHO; National Poison Information Centre
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common agents: organophosphates, aluminium phosphide, kerosene, rat poison, paracetamol, iron, alcohol.
- **Airway, breathing, circulation first**; identify the agent.
- Do not induce vomiting.
- Call the poison helpline/refer early.

## 2. Diagnosis and features by agent

### 2.1 Organophosphate (OP) insecticide

- SLUDGE syndrome: salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhoea, GI cramps, emesis; bradycardia, miosis, bronchorrhoea, fasciculations.
- **Atropine** 2 mg IV, repeat every 5–10 min until secretions dry (may need large doses).
- **Pralidoxime** 1–2 g IV over 30 min, then infusion.
- Decontaminate: remove clothes, wash skin; protect staff.
- Refer for respiratory support.

### 2.2 Aluminium phosphide

- Garlic odour, shock, arrhythmia, metabolic acidosis; very high mortality.
- No specific antidote; supportive care; refer immediately.
- Avoid gastric lavage (risk of phosphine release).

### 2.3 Kerosene / hydrocarbon

- Cough, breathlessness, aspiration pneumonia.
- **No gastric lavage, no induced vomiting.**
- Supportive oxygen; refer if respiratory distress.

### 2.4 Rat poison (warfarin/superwarfarin)

- Bleeding, prolonged PT/INR.
- Vitamin K1 10 mg IV/IM; FFP if severe bleeding; refer.

### 2.5 Paracetamol overdose

- Hepatotoxicity; nausea/vomiting; RUQ pain.
- **N-acetylcysteine** 150 mg/kg IV over 1 h, then 50 mg/kg over 4 h, then 100 mg/kg over 16 h (or oral protocol).
- Refer early; monitor LFT/INR.

### 2.6 Iron overdose

- Vomiting, GI bleed, shock, hepatic failure.
- **Desferrioxamine** 15 mg/kg/h IV (specialist); refer.

### 2.7 Alcohol intoxication / withdrawal

- Intoxication: airway protection, glucose, thiamine 100 mg IV, hydration.
- Withdrawal: benzodiazepines (chlordiazepoxide/lorazepam), thiamine, monitor seizures/delirium tremens; refer if severe.

## 3. Treatment by agent and general management

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| ABC | Airway, oxygen, IV access, monitor |
| Decontamination | Remove clothes, wash skin; activated charcoal only if appropriate |
| Glucose | Check and treat hypoglycaemia |
| Investigations | ECG, electrolytes, LFT, renal, PT/INR |
| Referral | Poison centre/ICU for severe cases |

**Activated charcoal 1 g/kg** if within 1 hour and airway protected — not for corrosives/hydrocarbons.

## 4. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Atropine:** tachycardia, urinary retention, confusion with overdose; titrate to secretions.
- **Pralidoxime:** caution in renal impairment.
- **NAC:** nausea, bronchospasm; monitor.
- **Vitamin K:** requires repeated doses for superwarfarin.
- **Benzodiazepines:** respiratory depression; monitor.

## 5. Red flags — refer immediately

- Respiratory distress/failure
- Shock/hypotension
- Seizures/coma
- Arrhythmia
- Severe acidosis
- GI bleeding
- Aluminium phosphide/iron/paracetamol overdose
- Intentional self-harm (psychiatric assessment)

## 6. Follow-up

- Psychiatric assessment for intentional overdose.
- Counselling on safe storage of pesticides/medicines.
- Liver/kidney function follow-up after paracetamol/iron.
