# Croup (Laryngotracheobronchitis)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Viral inflammation of larynx/trachea; barking cough and stridor.
- Common in children 6 months–3 years.
- **Dexamethasone is first-line.**
- Severe stridor at rest needs nebulised adrenaline and referral.

## 2. Clinical features

- Barking/seal-like cough
- Hoarse voice
- Inspiratory stridor
- Low-grade fever
- Worse at night
- Retractions in severe cases

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Severity | Features |
|---|---|
| Mild | Stridor only on crying |
| Moderate | Stridor at rest, retractions |
| Severe | Agitation, hypoxia, fatigue |
| Life-threatening | Cyanosis, decreased consciousness |

## 4. Treatment

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Dexamethasone | 0.15–0.6 mg/kg single dose (max 10 mg) |
| Nebulised adrenaline | 0.5 mL/kg of 1:1000 (max 5 mL) for severe |
| Paracetamol | 15 mg/kg/dose for fever |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dexamethasone — Decadron; Adrenaline — Adrenaline; Calpol.

- Keep child calm; avoid throat examination.
- Oxygen if hypoxic.

## 5. Supportive care

- Upright position
- Cool humidified air (optional)
- Hydration
- Observe for 2–4 h after adrenaline

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dexamethasone:** hyperglycaemia; short course safe.
- **Adrenaline:** tachycardia; monitor.
- **Sedatives:** avoid (mask hypoxia).
- **Antibiotics:** not routine.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Stridor at rest with hypoxia
- Cyanosis
- Altered consciousness
- Drooling/unable to swallow (epiglottitis)
- Fatigue
- Age < 6 months

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24 h.
- Return if stridor at rest.
