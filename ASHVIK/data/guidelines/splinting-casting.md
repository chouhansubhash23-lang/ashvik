# Splinting and Casting

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Splints immobilise fractures/dislocations for transport or healing.
- Check neurovascular status before and after application.
- Pad bony prominences.
- Refer displaced/unstable fractures.

## 2. Indications

- Fractures (temporary or definitive)
- Dislocations after reduction
- Sprains
- Soft tissue injuries
- Transport immobilisation

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Fracture site and stability
- Neurovascular status (pulse, sensation, capillary refill)
- Skin integrity
- Swelling

## 4. Treatment: application

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Analgesia (paracetamol 500 mg/or opioid) |
| 2 | Pad and position |
| 3 | Apply splint/cast |
| 4 | Check alignment |
| 5 | Recheck pulses/sensation |
| 6 | Elevate and ice |

### Common splints

| Injury | Splint |
|---|---|
| Forearm/wrist | Volar slab |
| Ankle | Posterior slab |
| Knee | Knee immobiliser |
| Shoulder | Sling/immobiliser |
| Finger | Buddy taping |

## 5. Supportive care

- Elevation
- Ice for 24–48 h
- Analgesia
- Cast care instructions

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Compression:** avoid tight casts (compartment syndrome).
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding/bruising.
- **NSAIDs:** may impair healing.
- **Skin:** pad bony areas.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Neurovascular deficit
- Compartment syndrome (pain out of proportion)
- Open fracture
- Unstable/displaced fracture
- Skin breakdown under cast

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24–48 h for swelling.
- X-ray after application.
- Cast removal as per protocol.
