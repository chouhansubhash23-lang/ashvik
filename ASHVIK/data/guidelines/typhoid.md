# Typhoid (Enteric Fever)

> **Source:** ICMR Treatment Guidelines for Antimicrobial Use in Common Syndromes; NCDC
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Cause: *Salmonella enterica* serotype Typhi / Paratyphi A, B, C.
- Spread: faecal-oral — contaminated water and food.
- Incubation: 7–14 days (range 3–30).
- Risk: poor sanitation, unsafe drinking water, street food.

## 2. Clinical features

- Step-ladder fever (rising over the first week)
- Headache, malaise, body ache
- Abdominal pain, constipation (adults) or diarrhoea (children)
- Loss of appetite, nausea
- Relative bradycardia
- Rose spots (faint, transient) — often missed
- Splenomegaly, hepatomegaly
- Complications: intestinal perforation, bleeding, encephalopathy, cholecystitis

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Notes |
|---|---|
| Blood culture | Gold standard; best in first week |
| CBC | Leucopenia, thrombocytopenia may occur |
| Widal test | Poor specificity; interpret with caution |
| Stool culture | Second/third week |
| Bone marrow culture | Most sensitive, reserved for difficult cases |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Uncomplicated typhoid (adult)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Azithromycin | Azee, Azithral | 500 mg once daily | 7 days |
| Cefixime | Taxim-O, Zifi | 200 mg twice daily | 7–10 days |
| Ceftriaxone (if severe/IV needed) | Monocef, Intacef | 2 g IV once daily | 10–14 days |

**Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, ofloxacin) are not recommended for empirical therapy** in India due to high resistance. Use only if culture confirms susceptibility.

### 4.2 Children

| Drug | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Azithromycin | 10–20 mg/kg once daily (max 500 mg) | 7 days |
| Ceftriaxone | 75–100 mg/kg/day IV | 10–14 days |
| Cefixime | 10–15 mg/kg/day divided BD | 7–10 days |

### 4.3 Severe / complicated typhoid

- IV ceftriaxone 2 g once daily (adult)
- Add dexamethasone only for severe typhoid with shock/altered consciousness, under specialist advice
- Surgical referral for perforation or major bleeding

## 5. Supportive care

- Oral fluids; IV fluids if vomiting or poor intake
- Soft, easily digestible diet
- Paracetamol for fever
- Monitor for abdominal pain, distension, bleeding

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Azithromycin:** QT prolongation with amiodarone, fluoroquinolones, ondansetron; antacids may reduce absorption; caution with warfarin.
- **Ceftriaxone:** do not mix with calcium-containing IV fluids in neonates; avoid Ringer's lactate as diluent; may potentiate warfarin.
- **Cefixime:** may increase INR with warfarin; antacids reduce absorption.
- **Fluoroquinolones:** tendinopathy, QT prolongation, cartilage damage in children; avoid in pregnancy.
- **Dexamethasone:** hyperglycaemia, infection masking; use only in severe typhoid with specialist input.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe abdominal pain, rigidity, distension (perforation)
- Blood in stool / melaena
- Persistent vomiting, dehydration
- Altered consciousness, seizures
- Shock / low BP
- Jaundice
- Pregnant woman with typhoid
- No improvement after 5–7 days of appropriate therapy

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 48–72 hours; expect fever to settle in 3–5 days.
- Repeat blood culture if not improving.
- Test and treat household contacts if symptomatic.
- Counsel on safe water, hand hygiene, food safety.
