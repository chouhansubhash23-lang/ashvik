# Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI)

> **Source:** NACO/NCDC syndromic management guidelines; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common STIs: syphilis, gonorrhoea, chlamydia, trichomoniasis, genital herpes, HPV, HIV.
- Use **syndromic management** where lab tests are unavailable.
- Treat partners; counsel on condoms and consent.
- Screen all STI patients for HIV and syphilis.

## 2. Syndromes and features

| Syndrome | Features |
|---|---|
| Urethral discharge | Discharge, dysuria (men) |
| Vaginal discharge | Abnormal discharge, itching |
| Genital ulcer | Painful/blistering (herpes) or painless (syphilis) |
| Pelvic inflammatory disease | Lower abdominal pain, fever, discharge |
| Genital warts | HPV |
| Inguinal bubo | Swelling, pain |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical syndrome | First-line |
| HIV test | All patients (with consent) |
| VDRL/RPR | Syphilis |
| Gram stain/culture | Gonorrhoea (if available) |
| PCR | Chlamydia/gonorrhoea (if available) |
| Urine pregnancy test | Before treatment |

## 4. Treatment (syndromic)

### 4.1 Urethral discharge (men)

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Ceftriaxone | 250 mg IM single dose |
| Azithromycin | 1 g orally single dose (or doxycycline 100 mg BD x 7 days) |

### 4.2 Vaginal discharge

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Ceftriaxone | 250 mg IM single dose |
| Azithromycin | 1 g orally single dose |
| Metronidazole | 2 g orally single dose |

### 4.3 Genital ulcer

| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Syphilis | Benzathine penicillin 2.4 million IU IM single dose (or doxycycline 100 mg BD x 14 days) |
| Herpes | Acyclovir 400 mg three times daily x 7–10 days |
| Chancroid | Azithromycin 1 g single dose |

### 4.4 Pelvic inflammatory disease

| Drug | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Ceftriaxone | 250 mg IM single dose | Once |
| Doxycycline | 100 mg twice daily | 14 days |
| Metronidazole | 500 mg twice daily | 14 days |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Azithromycin — Azee; Doxycycline — Doxt; Metronidazole — Flagyl; Acyclovir — Zovirax; Benzathine penicillin — Penidure LA.

## 5. Supportive care

- Confidential counselling; partner notification
- Condom promotion and demonstration
- HIV testing and linkage to ART
- Syphilis screening in pregnancy
- Avoid self-medication

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Ceftriaxone:** see other chapters; IM injection painful — use lidocaine if available.
- **Azithromycin:** QT prolongation; antacids reduce absorption.
- **Doxycycline:** avoid in pregnancy/lactation and children < 8 years; photosensitivity; antacids/iron reduce absorption.
- **Metronidazole:** disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol; warfarin interaction.
- **Acyclovir:** renal dose adjustment; hydration.
- **Benzathine penicillin:** anaphylaxis — keep adrenaline ready; test dose not reliable.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- PID with fever and peritoneal signs
- Tubo-ovarian abscess
- Severe genital ulcer with systemic symptoms
- Neonatal conjunctivitis
- Syphilis in pregnancy
- Suspected HIV with opportunistic infection
- Sexual assault — forensic and psychological support

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 7 days.
- Confirm partner treatment.
- Repeat HIV/syphilis testing as indicated.
- Counsel on prevention and contraception.
