# Neonatal Jaundice

> **Source:** WHO; IAP; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Most jaundice is physiological.
- **Jaundice in first 24 h is pathological.**
- Measure bilirubin if available.
- Treat with phototherapy; exchange transfusion if severe.

## 2. Clinical features

- Yellow skin/sclera
- Poor feeding, lethargy
- Dark urine/pale stools
- Kernicterus (late): hypotonia, seizures

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Serum bilirubin | Severity |
| Blood group/Rh | Hemolysis |
| CBC/reticulocytes | Hemolysis |
| Direct bilirubin | Cholestasis |
| Sepsis screen | Infection |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Mild | Continue feeding, monitor |
| Moderate | Phototherapy |
| Severe | Exchange transfusion/refer |
| Hemolysis | Refer |

**Phototherapy** according to charts; protect eyes.
**Paracetamol:** not routine.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Phototherapy unit; IVIG (specialist).

## 5. Supportive care

- Frequent feeding
- Paracetamol 500 mg for maternal pain (not for the neonate)
- Monitor weight/urine
- Keep warm

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Phototherapy:** dehydration, eye protection.
- **IVIG:** specialist.
- **Drugs displacing bilirubin:** avoid.
- **Herbal remedies:** unsafe.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Jaundice < 24 h
- Palms/soles yellow
- Lethargy/poor feeding
- High bilirubin
- Seizures
- Preterm/LBW

## 8. Follow-up

- Recheck bilirubin.
- Feeding/growth.
- Hearing screening if severe.
