# Migraine and Tension-Type Headache

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); International Headache Society criteria; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Migraine: recurrent moderate-severe headache, often unilateral, pulsating, with nausea/photophobia.
- Tension-type headache: bilateral, pressing, mild-moderate, no vomiting.
- Red flags must be excluded before treating as primary headache.
- Analgesic overuse headache is common — limit acute painkillers to < 10–15 days/month.

## 2. Clinical features

| Feature | Migraine | Tension-type |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Unilateral (often) | Bilateral |
| Quality | Pulsating | Pressing/tightening |
| Intensity | Moderate-severe | Mild-moderate |
| Activity | Worse with activity | Not worsened |
| Associated | Nausea, vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia | None |
| Aura | Visual/sensory in some | None |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical criteria (IHS) | Diagnosis of primary headache |
| Neuro examination | Exclude secondary causes |
| BP, fundus | Hypertension, papilloedema |
| Imaging (CT/MRI) | Only if red flags or abnormal neuro exam |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Acute attack

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paracetamol | Dolo 650, Crocin | 1 g | Max 4 g/day |
| Ibuprofen | Brufen | 400 mg | With food |
| Naproxen | Naprosyn | 500 mg | — |
| Sumatriptan | Suminat, Imigran | 50–100 mg | Avoid in CAD, uncontrolled HTN, pregnancy |
| Metoclopramide | Perinorm | 10 mg | For nausea/vomiting |
| Domperidone | Domstal | 10 mg | For nausea |

Take acute treatment early in the attack. Avoid opioids and frequent NSAIDs.

### 4.2 Prophylaxis (≥ 4 attacks/month or disabling)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Propranolol | Ciplar | 40–80 mg twice daily | Avoid in asthma |
| Amitriptyline | Tryptomer | 10–25 mg at night | Sedation, anticholinergic |
| Topiramate | Topamac | 25–50 mg twice daily | Kidney stones, weight loss |
| Flunarizine | Vertin | 5–10 mg at night | Weight gain, parkinsonism |

Review prophylaxis after 8–12 weeks.

### 4.3 Tension-type headache

- Paracetamol or ibuprofen as needed (limit use)
- Amitriptyline 10–25 mg at night for chronic cases
- Stress management, sleep hygiene, exercise

## 5. Supportive care

- Headache diary: triggers, frequency, medication use
- Regular meals, hydration, sleep
- Avoid known triggers (bright light, loud noise, skipped meals, alcohol)
- Relaxation techniques
- Limit analgesic use

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Triptans:** serotonin syndrome with SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs; avoid with ergots within 24 h; contraindicated in CAD, stroke, uncontrolled hypertension, pregnancy.
- **Propranolol:** bradycardia, bronchospasm; interacts with verapamil, digoxin, insulin (masks hypoglycaemia).
- **Amitriptyline:** anticholinergic effects, arrhythmia, serotonin syndrome; caution in elderly, glaucoma, prostate enlargement.
- **Topiramate:** kidney stones, metabolic acidosis, cognitive slowing; reduces OCP efficacy at higher doses; avoid in pregnancy.
- **Flunarizine:** sedation, weight gain, extrapyramidal symptoms; avoid in depression.
- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury; avoid in dengue.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Sudden thunderclap headache (SAH)
- Fever + neck stiffness (meningitis)
- New focal neurological deficit
- Papilloedema / visual loss
- Headache after head injury
- New headache at age > 50
- Headache in pregnancy with hypertension (pre-eclampsia)
- Cancer/HIV with new headache
- Worsening pattern / no response

## 8. Follow-up

- Review after 4–8 weeks.
- Assess headache frequency and disability.
- Check medication overuse.
- Refer if diagnosis uncertain or refractory.
