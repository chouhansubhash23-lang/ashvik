# Fever in Children

> **Source:** IMNCI / RBSK; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Fever is a common presentation; most causes are viral.
- Use IMNCI danger signs to triage.
- Do not use aspirin in children (Reye syndrome).
- Look for malaria/dengue/typhoid in endemic areas.

## 2. Clinical features

- Temperature ≥ 100.4 °F (38 °C)
- Poor feeding, irritability, lethargy
- Rash, cough, diarrhoea
- Febrile seizures
- Dehydration

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Sign | Action |
|---|---|
| Unable to drink/breastfeed | Urgent referral |
| Convulsions | Urgent referral |
| Lethargy/unconscious | Urgent referral |
| Stiff neck | Meningitis? refer |
| Chest indrawing | Pneumonia? refer |
| Severe dehydration | IV fluids/refer |
| Malnutrition | Higher risk |

- Malaria RDT/dengue test if endemic.
- Urine dipstick for UTI.

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Paracetamol | 15 mg/kg/dose every 6 h (max 4 doses/day) |
| Ibuprofen | 10 mg/kg/dose every 8 h (≥ 6 months) |
| Fluids | Oral/ORS; encourage feeding |
| Tepid sponging | If very hot |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Calpol, Crocin; Ibuprofen — Brufen.

**Avoid aspirin** in children.

## 5. Supportive care

- Light clothing
- Hydration
- Monitor temperature and danger signs
- Treat specific cause

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Paracetamol:** hepatotoxicity with overdose; max 4 doses/day.
- **Ibuprofen:** avoid in dehydration/kidney injury; GI upset.
- **Aspirin:** contraindicated (Reye syndrome).
- **Antibiotics:** only for bacterial infection.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Age < 3 months with fever
- Unable to feed/drink
- Convulsions
- Lethargy/unconscious
- Stiff neck
- Chest indrawing
- Severe dehydration
- Rash with bleeding
- Fever > 5 days

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24–48 h.
- Return if danger signs.
- Complete treatment.
