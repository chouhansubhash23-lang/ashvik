# Acute Otitis Media in Children

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common in children < 5 years; often follows a viral URI.
- Most cases resolve without antibiotics; severe cases need treatment.
- **Amoxicillin is first-line.**
- Refer if mastoiditis or complications.

## 2. Clinical features

- Ear pain, tugging at ear
- Fever
- Irritability, poor feeding
- Ear discharge (perforation)
- Bulging/red tympanic membrane on otoscopy
- Hearing difficulty

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Mild (pain < 48 h, no fever) | Analgesia, observe |
| Severe (fever, pain > 48 h) | Antibiotics |
| < 6 months | Antibiotics |
| Otorrhea | Antibiotics |
| Mastoid swelling | Refer |

## 4. Treatment

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 40–45 mg/kg/day divided BD (max 1 g/day) |
| Amoxicillin-clavulanate | If recent amoxicillin failure |
| Paracetamol | 15 mg/kg/dose every 6 h |
| Ibuprofen | 10 mg/kg/dose every 8 h (≥ 6 months) |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Mox; Augmentin; Calpol; Brufen.

Duration: 5–7 days (10 days if < 2 years/severe).

## 5. Supportive care

- Analgesia
- Warm compress
- Hydration
- Avoid ear drops if perforation suspected

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Amoxicillin:** rash; GI upset.
- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate:** diarrhoea, cholestatic jaundice.
- **Ibuprofen:** avoid in dehydration.
- **Aspirin:** contraindicated.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Mastoid swelling/tenderness
- Facial palsy
- Meningism
- Severe headache
- Persistent fever > 48–72 h
- Infants < 3 months

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 48–72 h.
- Hearing assessment if recurrent.
- Prevent recurrence (avoid smoke, vaccinate).
