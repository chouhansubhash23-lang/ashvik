# Childhood Malnutrition (SAM / MAM)

> **Source:** National Nutrition Mission / POSHAN Abhiyaan; WHO/UNICEF; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition):** weight-for-height < −3 SD, MUAC < 11.5 cm, or bilateral pitting oedema.
- **MAM (Moderate Acute Malnutrition):** MUAC 11.5–12.5 cm or weight-for-height −2 to −3 SD.
- SAM with medical complications needs **NRC admission**.
- Early identification and follow-up prevent death and stunting.

## 2. Clinical features

- Visible severe wasting (ribs, shoulders)
- Bilateral pitting oedema (kwashiorkor)
- MUAC < 11.5 cm (SAM)
- Poor appetite, lethargy
- Recurrent infections, diarrhoea
- Hair/skin changes, angular stomatitis
- Developmental delay

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Weight, height/length | Plot on WHO growth chart |
| MUAC | Left arm, midpoint |
| Oedema | Press both feet for 3 seconds |
| Appetite test | Ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) / F-75 |
| Medical complications | Fever, diarrhoea, pneumonia, TB, HIV |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 SAM without complications — community management

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| RUTF | 8–12 sachets/week (energy-dense paste) |
| Antibiotics | Amoxicillin 15 mg/kg TDS x 5 days (as per protocol) |
| Vitamin A | 200,000 IU (age ≥ 12 months); 100,000 IU (6–11 months) |
| Zinc | 20 mg daily x 14 days |
| Deworming | Albendazole as per age |
| Follow-up | Weekly MUAC/weight; refer if not improving |

### 4.2 SAM with complications — NRC

- Admit to Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre.
- Treat hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, dehydration, infection.
- F-75 stabilisation → F-100/RUTF rehabilitation.
- Antibiotics, vitamin A, zinc, potassium, magnesium.
- Monitor for refeeding syndrome.

### 4.3 MAM

- Supplementary nutrition (Take-Home Ration / hot cooked meal).
- Counselling on diet diversity and feeding.
- Monthly growth monitoring.
- Deworming and micronutrients.

## 5. Supportive care

- Promote breastfeeding (0–6 months exclusive; continue to 2 years).
- Complementary feeding from 6 months.
- Hand hygiene, safe water, sanitation.
- Immunisation up to date.
- Family counselling; link to anganwadi/ASHA.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Amoxicillin:** rash; complete course.
- **Vitamin A:** avoid repeated high doses (toxicity); not in pregnancy (high dose).
- **Zinc:** may cause vomiting; separate from iron/calcium.
- **Albendazole:** avoid in pregnancy (first trimester); GI upset.
- **Refeeding:** watch for electrolyte shifts, heart failure.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Bilateral pitting oedema
- MUAC < 11.5 cm with poor appetite
- Severe dehydration / diarrhoea
- Pneumonia, sepsis, meningitis
- Hypoglycaemia, hypothermia
- Severe anaemia
- TB/HIV co-infection
- No weight gain after 2 weeks

## 8. Follow-up

- Weekly for SAM; monthly for MAM.
- Discharge criteria: MUAC ≥ 12.5 cm, weight gain, oedema resolved.
- Continue supplementary feeding after discharge.
- Developmental assessment at follow-up.
