# Paediatric Dehydration

> **Source:** WHO/IMNCI; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Most dehydration is from diarrhoea/vomiting.
- Assess dehydration (Plan A/B/C).
- **ORS is life-saving.**
- Severe dehydration needs IV fluids and referral.

## 2. Clinical features

| Sign | Some dehydration | Severe dehydration |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | Restless | Lethargic |
| Eyes | Sunken | Sunken |
| Thirst | Drinks eagerly | Drinks poorly |
| Skin pinch | Slow | Very slow |
| Urine | Reduced | Minimal/anuria |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Weight loss
- Vital signs
- Urine output
- Electrolytes if severe

## 4. Treatment

### Plan A (no dehydration)

- ORS after each stool: < 2 years 50–100 mL; 2–10 years 100–200 mL; older 200–250 mL.
- Continue feeding.
- Zinc 10–20 mg daily x 14 days.

### Plan B (some dehydration)

- ORS 75 mL/kg over 4 h.
- Reassess.
- Breastfeed between ORS.

### Plan C (severe dehydration)

- IV Ringer's lactate 100 mL/kg:
  - Infants: 30 mL/kg over 1 h, then 70 mL/kg over 5 h.
  - Older: 30 mL/kg over 30 min, then 70 mL/kg over 2.5 h.
- Refer.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Electral; Zinconia.

## 5. Supportive care

- Continue breastfeeding
- Monitor urine output
- Reassess frequently

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **ORS:** do not add extra sugar.
- **Zinc:** may cause vomiting.
- **IV fluids:** avoid overload.
- **Loperamide:** avoid in children.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe dehydration
- Shock
- Altered consciousness
- Blood in stool
- No urine
- Persistent vomiting

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 24 h.
- Teach ORS preparation.
- Return if danger signs.
