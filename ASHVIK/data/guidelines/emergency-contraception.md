# Emergency Contraception

> **Source:** National Family Planning Programme; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Emergency contraception (EC) prevents pregnancy after unprotected sex.
- **Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg within 72 hours** (best < 24 h).
- Copper IUCD within 5 days is more effective.
- EC is not a regular contraceptive method.

## 2. Indications

- Unprotected intercourse
- Contraceptive failure (condom break, missed pill)
- Sexual assault
- Incorrect fertile-window calculation

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Time since intercourse
- Last menstrual period
- Pregnancy test if indicated
- Contraindications (IUCD)
- Consent and confidentiality

## 4. Treatment

| Method | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Levonorgestrel | 1.5 mg single dose | Within 72 h |
| Ulipristal acetate | 30 mg single dose | Within 120 h (if available) |
| Copper IUCD | Insertion | Within 5 days |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** i-pill, Unwanted-72; CuT 380A.

- Repeat dose if vomiting within 3 h.
- Offer antiemetic if needed.

## 5. Supportive care

- Counselling on regular contraception
- STI/HIV testing if indicated
- Pregnancy test if period delayed
- Confidential support

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Enzyme inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin):** reduce EC efficacy — consider IUCD.
- **Levonorgestrel:** nausea, vomiting, irregular bleeding.
- **IUCD:** cramping, expulsion; avoid in active PID.
- **Obesity:** reduced efficacy of oral EC — consider IUCD.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Suspected pregnancy
- Sexual assault
- Severe abdominal pain
- Heavy bleeding
- IUCD complications

## 8. Follow-up

- Pregnancy test if period missed.
- Contraception counselling.
- STI screening.

## 10. Disclaimer

Decision-support summary only. EC is for emergencies; promote ongoing contraception. Brand names are illustrative, not promotional.
