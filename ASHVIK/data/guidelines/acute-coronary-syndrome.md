# Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) — Referral Focus

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NPCDCS; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- ACS = unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI.
- **Time is muscle** — every minute of delay increases damage.
- Rural PHC role: recognise, give first-line drugs, stabilise, refer immediately (108 ambulance).
- Do not wait for ECG/troponin if transfer is delayed.

## 2. Clinical features

- Central chest pain/pressure > 20 min, radiating to arm/jaw/back
- Sweating, nausea, breathlessness
- Palpitations, syncope
- Atypical: epigastric pain, fatigue, breathlessness (elderly, women, diabetics)
- Silent MI in diabetics

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| ECG (12-lead) | STEMI: ST elevation; NSTEMI: ST depression/T inversion |
| Troponin (if available) | Confirms myocardial injury |
| BP, pulse, SpO2 | Stabilisation |
| Glucose | Hypo/hyperglycaemia |

## 4. Treatment (immediate — load and go)

| Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Aspirin | 300 mg chewed (or 150–300 mg) | Unless allergy/active bleeding |
| Clopidogrel | 300–600 mg (if PCI planned) or 75 mg | Discuss with receiving centre |
| Nitroglycerin (SL) | 0.4 mg, repeat up to 3 doses | Avoid if SBP < 90, RV infarct, PDE5 inhibitor use |
| Oxygen | Only if SpO2 < 90% or breathlessness | — |
| Morphine | 2–4 mg IV if severe pain | Monitor breathing |
| Atorvastatin | 80 mg | High-intensity statin |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Aspirin — Ecosprin; Clopidogrel — Clopilet; Nitroglycerin — Sorbitrate; Atorvastatin — Atorva.

### STEMI — reperfusion

- Primary PCI if available within 120 min.
- Thrombolysis (streptokinase/tenecteplase) if PCI unavailable and no contraindication, within 12 h — only where protocol permits.
- **Refer immediately**; do not manage STEMI at OPD.

## 5. Supportive care

- Rest, reassurance, semi-recumbent position
- IV access, monitoring
- Treat arrhythmia/hypotension
- Arrange 108 ambulance with ECG and note of drugs given
- Notify receiving hospital

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Aspirin:** GI bleed, bronchospasm; avoid in active bleeding/allergy.
- **Clopidogrel:** bleeding; omeprazole may reduce activation (prefer pantoprazole); stop before surgery as advised.
- **Nitrates:** severe hypotension with PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil/tadalafil) — contraindicated within 24–48 h; avoid in RV infarct.
- **Morphine:** respiratory depression, hypotension; avoid in COPD.
- **Thrombolytics:** bleeding, stroke risk; strict contraindication checklist.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Chest pain > 20 min with ST elevation
- Hypotension/shock
- Pulmonary oedema
- Arrhythmia (VT/VF)
- Syncope
- Pain with neuro deficit (aortic dissection)
- Pregnancy with chest pain

## 8. Follow-up

- After discharge: dual antiplatelet, statin, beta-blocker, ACEi/ARB, cardiac rehab.
- Control BP, sugar, lipids; stop smoking.
- Regular cardiology follow-up.
