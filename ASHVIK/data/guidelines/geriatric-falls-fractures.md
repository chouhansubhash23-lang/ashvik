# Geriatric Falls and Fractures

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NPHCE; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Falls are a leading cause of injury, disability, and death in older adults.
- Most falls are multifactorial and preventable.
- **Hip fracture is a surgical emergency** — refer early.
- Always look for a medical cause (syncope, arrhythmia, stroke, hypoglycaemia).

## 2. Clinical features

- Fall with or without injury
- Hip pain, inability to bear weight, shortened/externally rotated leg
- Wrist/vertebral fractures
- Head injury (especially on anticoagulants)
- Syncope, palpitations, dizziness
- Fear of falling, reduced mobility

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Injury exam | Fracture/dislocation |
| X-ray | Hip, wrist, spine |
| ECG | Arrhythmia, syncope |
| BP (lying/standing) | Orthostatic hypotension |
| Glucose | Hypoglycaemia |
| Medication review | Sedatives, antihypertensives |
| Cognitive screen | Dementia/delirium |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Fracture

| Injury | Action |
|---|---|
| Hip fracture | Analgesia, immobilise, **refer for surgery** |
| Wrist fracture | Splint, analgesia, refer |
| Vertebral fracture | Analgesia, brace, assess osteoporosis |
| Head injury | Observe, CT referral if indicated |

**Analgesia:** paracetamol 500–650 mg TDS; avoid NSAIDs in elderly/renal impairment.

### 4.2 After the fall

- Treat the cause (arrhythmia, hypoglycaemia, infection, dehydration).
- Review and stop sedatives/anticholinergics.
- Physiotherapy and gait/balance training.
- Home safety: lighting, rails, remove rugs.
- Vision/hearing correction.

### 4.3 Osteoporosis

- Calcium 500 mg twice daily + vitamin D 800 IU daily.
- Bisphosphonate if indicated (specialist).
- Exercise and fall prevention.

## 5. Supportive care

- Pain control
- Pressure ulcer prevention if immobile
- DVT prophylaxis as indicated
- Nutrition and hydration
- Caregiver education

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Sedatives/benzodiazepines:** increase fall risk — avoid.
- **Antihypertensives:** orthostatic hypotension.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk after head injury.
- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury.
- **Opioids:** sedation, falls, constipation.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hip fracture / inability to bear weight
- Head injury on anticoagulants
- Loss of consciousness/arrhythmia
- Suspected stroke
- Severe pain/spinal tenderness with neuro deficit
- Open fracture
- Recurrent falls

## 8. Follow-up

- Falls clinic/physiotherapy.
- Medication review at every visit.
- Home hazard assessment.
- Bone health review.
