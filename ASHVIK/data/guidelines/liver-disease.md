# Liver Disease (Hepatitis, Cirrhosis)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NCDC; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Hepatitis A/E are waterborne and self-limiting; B/C are bloodborne and chronic.
- Cirrhosis complications: ascites, varices, encephalopathy, SBP.
- Avoid hepatotoxic drugs and alcohol.
- Vaccinate against hepatitis A/B where indicated.

## 2. Clinical features

| Condition | Features |
|---|---|
| Acute hepatitis | Jaundice, dark urine, nausea, fatigue, RUQ pain |
| Hepatitis E in pregnancy | High mortality; severe hepatitis |
| Chronic hepatitis B/C | Often asymptomatic; fatigue; cirrhosis later |
| Cirrhosis | Ascites, jaundice, spider naevi, palmar erythema, gynecomastia |
| Hepatic encephalopathy | Confusion, asterixis, drowsiness |
| SBP | Fever, abdominal pain, ascites |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| LFT (bilirubin, AST/ALT, ALP, albumin) | Liver injury |
| PT/INR | Synthetic function |
| Viral markers (HAV IgM, HBsAg, anti-HCV, HEV IgM) | Cause |
| Ultrasound abdomen | Cirrhosis, ascites, portal vein |
| Ascitic fluid analysis | SBP (neutrophils > 250/mm³) |
| Fibroscan | Fibrosis (if available) |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Acute hepatitis A/E

- Supportive: rest, hydration, nutrition.
- Avoid alcohol and hepatotoxic drugs (paracetamol caution).
- Monitor for acute liver failure: encephalopathy, coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia → refer.

### 4.2 Chronic hepatitis B/C

- Refer for antiviral assessment.
- Screen family, vaccinate contacts.
- Avoid alcohol.

### 4.3 Cirrhosis / ascites

| Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Ascites | Salt restriction, spironolactone 25–100 mg + furosemide 20–40 mg daily |
| SBP | Ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily x 5–7 days + albumin |
| Variceal bleed | Resuscitate, octreotide, antibiotics, urgent endoscopy referral |
| Encephalopathy | Lactulose, rifaximin; treat precipitant |
| Pruritus | Antihistamine, ursodeoxycholic acid |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Spironolactone — Aldactone; Furosemide — Lasix; Lactulose — Duphalac; Rifaximin — Rifagut.

## 5. Supportive care

- Alcohol abstinence
- Nutrition (avoid protein restriction in encephalopathy)
- Vaccination (hepatitis A/B, influenza, pneumococcal)
- Avoid NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, sedatives
- Screen for varices and HCC

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Paracetamol:** limit to 2 g/day in cirrhosis.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid (kidney injury, bleeding, ascites).
- **Sedatives:** precipitate encephalopathy.
- **Aminoglycosides:** nephrotoxic.
- **Spironolactone:** hyperkalaemia; with ACEi/ARB caution.
- **Rifampicin/isoniazid:** hepatotoxic.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Hepatic encephalopathy
- GI bleeding (haematemesis/melaena)
- SBP
- Acute liver failure (coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia)
- Hepatorenal syndrome (oliguria)
- Pregnancy with hepatitis E
- Severe jaundice with coagulopathy

## 8. Follow-up

- LFT/INR every 3–6 months.
- Ultrasound + AFP every 6 months for cirrhosis.
- Endoscopy surveillance for varices.
- Alcohol counselling and vaccination.
