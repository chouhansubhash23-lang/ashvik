# Malaria

> **Source:** NVBDCP National Drug Policy on Malaria; WHO Guidelines for Malaria (S11)
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Vector: female *Anopheles* mosquito (dusk to dawn biting).
- Types: *Plasmodium vivax*, *P. falciparum* (severe), *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*.
- Incubation: 7–30 days.
- Endemic across India; highest in Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, NE states.

## 2. Clinical features

- Cyclical fever with chills and sweating
- Headache, body ache, nausea, vomiting
- Anaemia, jaundice (falciparum)
- Splenomegaly
- Severe malaria: altered consciousness, seizures, severe anaemia, dark urine, breathlessness, bleeding, low sugar, kidney injury

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Rapid diagnostic test (RDT) | First-line at PHC; detects *P. falciparum* (some detect vivax) |
| Peripheral blood smear | Confirm species and parasite density |
| CBC, platelet count | Baseline; anaemia/thrombocytopenia |
| Blood sugar, creatinine, bilirubin, urine output | Severe malaria assessment |

**Rule:** every fever with chills should be tested for malaria before empirical treatment.

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 *P. vivax* (uncomplicated)

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Adult dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chloroquine | Lariago | 10 mg/kg on day 1, 10 mg/kg day 2, 5 mg/kg day 3 | 3 days |
| Primaquine | Generic | 0.25 mg/kg once daily | 14 days |

**Primaquine is contraindicated in:** pregnancy, infants < 6 months, G6PD deficiency (risk of haemolysis). Test G6PD where available.

### 4.2 *P. falciparum* (uncomplicated)

**Option A — ACT-SP (national programme)**

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Adult dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artesunate | Falcigo | 4 mg/kg once daily | 3 days |
| Sulfadoxine + Pyrimethamine (SP) | Generic | Sulfadoxine 25 mg/kg + pyrimethamine 1.25 mg/kg single dose | Day 1 only |
| Primaquine (gametocidal) | Generic | 0.75 mg/kg single dose | Day 2 |

**Option B — Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL)**

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Adult dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artemether 20 mg + Lumefantrine 120 mg | Coartem | 4 tablets twice daily | 3 days |

Take AL with fatty food/milk to improve absorption.

### 4.3 Severe malaria (emergency)

| Drug | Adult dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| IV Artesunate | 2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12, 24 h, then once daily | Preferred; switch to oral ACT when tolerating |
| IM Artemether (if artesunate unavailable) | 3.2 mg/kg loading, then 1.6 mg/kg daily | — |
| IV Quinine (alternative) | 20 mg/kg loading over 4 h, then 10 mg/kg 8-hourly | Cardiac monitoring; hypoglycaemia risk |

**Refer immediately.** Do not manage severe malaria at OPD.

## 5. Supportive care

- Paracetamol for fever (avoid aspirin/NSAIDs if dengue not excluded).
- Oral fluids; IV fluids if vomiting/severe disease.
- Treat anaemia and hypoglycaemia.
- Monitor urine output, consciousness, breathing.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Chloroquine:** may prolong QT; caution with azithromycin, fluoroquinolones, ondansetron; antacids reduce absorption (separate by 2 h); may worsen psoriasis.
- **Primaquine:** haemolysis in G6PD deficiency; avoid in pregnancy and infants < 6 months; caution with other haemolytic drugs.
- **Artesunate/artemether:** QT prolongation with other QT-prolonging drugs; avoid in first trimester unless severe malaria (WHO allows in 2nd/3rd trimester; severe disease → treat).
- **SP:** sulfa allergy, G6PD deficiency, pregnancy (3rd trimester), neonates (kernicterus); interacts with warfarin, methotrexate, phenytoin.
- **Quinine:** hypoglycaemia, QT prolongation, cinchonism; interacts with warfarin, digoxin.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Altered consciousness, seizures, confusion
- Severe anaemia, jaundice, dark urine
- Breathlessness / ARDS
- Bleeding, low BP, shock
- Low urine output / kidney injury
- Hypoglycaemia
- Pregnancy with falciparum malaria
- Infant < 6 months with fever

## 8. Follow-up

- Repeat smear/RDT if fever persists after 3 days.
- Check Hb after 1–2 weeks in severe anaemia.
- Counsel on bed nets, repellents, and eliminating stagnant water.
