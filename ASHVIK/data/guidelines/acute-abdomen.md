# Acute Abdomen — Recognition and Referral

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Acute abdomen = sudden severe abdominal pain, often needing surgery.
- Causes: appendicitis, perforation, obstruction, pancreatitis, cholecystitis, PID, ectopic pregnancy, renal colic.
- **Do not delay referral** for peritonitis or shock.
- Analgesia does not mask diagnosis — give it.

## 2. Clinical features

- Severe abdominal pain, guarding, rigidity
- Fever, vomiting, distension
- Absent bowel sounds (ileus/peritonitis)
- Rebound tenderness
- Shock (tachycardia, hypotension)
- Pregnancy — consider ectopic

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical exam | Site, guarding, rebound |
| CBC, CRP | Infection |
| Urine dipstick/pregnancy test | UTI, ectopic |
| X-ray erect abdomen | Free gas, obstruction |
| Ultrasound | Gallstones, appendicitis, PID, ectopic |
| Amylase/lipase | Pancreatitis |

## 4. Treatment (stabilise and refer)

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| IV fluids | 0.9% saline/Ringer's lactate |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol 500 mg IV/oral; opioid if severe |
| NG tube | If vomiting/distension |
| Antibiotics | Ceftriaxone + metronidazole if perforation suspected |
| Urinary catheter | Monitor output |
| Referral | Surgical emergency |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Metronidazole — Flagyl.

## 5. Supportive care

- Nil by mouth
- Monitor vitals and urine output
- Keep warm
- Arrange transport

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Opioids:** respiratory depression; monitor.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid in dehydration/kidney injury.
- **Antibiotics:** renal dose adjustment.
- **Antiemetics:** may mask progression; use cautiously.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Peritonitis (rigidity, rebound)
- Shock
- Free gas on X-ray
- Suspected ectopic pregnancy
- Obstructed labour
- GI bleeding
- Severe pancreatitis
- Postoperative abdomen

## 8. Follow-up

- Surgical review.
- Post-op care and wound review.
- Investigate cause.
