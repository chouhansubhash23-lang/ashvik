# Rabies Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

> **Source:** National Rabies Control Programme; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- PrEP protects high-risk people before exposure.
- Recommended for veterinarians, animal handlers, lab staff, children in endemic areas.
- Three-dose schedule.
- Booster if exposed.

## 2. Indications

- Veterinary/animal handlers
- Lab workers
- Children in high-risk areas
- Travelers with animal contact

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Risk assessment
- Previous vaccination
- Immunosuppression
- Tetanus status

## 4. Treatment

| Schedule | Doses |
|---|---|
| IM | Days 0, 7, 21 or 28 |
| ID (if used) | 0.1 mL per site |

**Vaccine dose:** 0.5 mL or 1 mL IM as per product.
**Paracetamol 500 mg** for fever/aches.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Verorab; Rabipur.

## 5. Supportive care

- Education on wound care
- Booster advice
- Documentation

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Immunosuppression:** may need additional doses.
- **Pregnancy:** safe.
- **Vaccine:** local pain/fever.
- **Animal bite:** still needs PEP.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Animal bite despite PrEP
- Immunocompromised
- Severe reaction

## 8. Follow-up

- Complete schedule.
- Booster after exposure.
