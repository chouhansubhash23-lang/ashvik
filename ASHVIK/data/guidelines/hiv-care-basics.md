# HIV Care Basics

> **Source:** NACO; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- HIV is treatable; early ART improves outcomes.
- Test with consent and confidentiality.
- Link to ART centre; screen for TB and STIs.
- Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) within 72 hours.

## 2. Clinical features

- Fever, weight loss, night sweats
- Chronic diarrhoea
- Oral thrush, skin infections
- Lymphadenopathy
- Recurrent infections
- Neurological symptoms

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| HIV rapid test | Screening (with consent) |
| Confirmatory test | Diagnosis |
| CD4 count | Immune status |
| Viral load | Treatment monitoring |
| TB screening | All patients |
| STI screening | All patients |

## 4. Treatment

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| New diagnosis | Link to ART centre; start ART |
| Opportunistic infection | Treat and refer |
| PEP | Start within 72 h (28 days) |
| Pregnancy | Prevention of mother-to-child transmission |

**ART regimens** are provided free under NACO; do not start ad-hoc regimens without training.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** TDF/3TC/DTG (NACO regimen); Cotrimoxazole 960 mg prophylaxis.

## 5. Supportive care

- Nutritional support
- Adherence counselling
- Mental health support
- Partner notification (confidential)
- Condom promotion

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Rifampicin:** reduces some ART levels — specialist adjustment.
- **Cotrimoxazole:** rash, hyperkalaemia.
- **ART:** hepatotoxicity, renal toxicity, drug interactions.
- **PEP:** adherence and side effects.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Severe opportunistic infection
- Respiratory distress (PCP/TB)
- Altered consciousness
- Severe diarrhoea/dehydration
- Suicidal ideation
- Pregnancy with untreated HIV

## 8. Follow-up

- ART adherence and viral load.
- TB screening.
- STI screening.
- Psychosocial support.
