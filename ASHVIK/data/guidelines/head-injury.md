# Head Injury

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Head injury ranges from minor concussion to severe brain injury.
- **GCS, pupils, and focal deficits** guide urgency.
- Assume cervical spine injury until cleared.
- Avoid hypotension and hypoxia — they worsen outcomes.

## 2. Clinical features

- Loss of consciousness, confusion
- Headache, vomiting
- Seizures
- Focal deficit, unequal pupils
- CSF leak (nose/ear)
- Scalp hematoma, skull fracture
- Post-traumatic amnesia

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| GCS | Severity (mild 13–15, moderate 9–12, severe ≤ 8) |
| Pupils | Raised ICP |
| CT head | Fracture, bleed (referral) |
| Cervical spine imaging | If neck pain/tenderness |
| CBC, coagulation | Baseline |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| ABCDE | Airway, breathing, circulation |
| Cervical spine | Immobilise |
| Oxygen | SpO2 ≥ 94% |
| IV fluids | Maintain BP; avoid hypotension |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol 500 mg IV/oral |
| Antiemetic | Ondansetron |
| Referral | GCS ≤ 13, focal deficit, seizure, skull fracture |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Dolo 650; Ondansetron — Emeset.

**Do not give sedatives** unless controlling seizures/agitation.

## 5. Supportive care

- Head elevation 30°
- Monitor GCS, pupils, vitals
- Avoid hyperthermia
- Seizure precautions
- Keep family informed

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Sedatives/opioids:** may mask GCS; avoid unless needed.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid (bleeding).
- **Mannitol/hypertonic saline:** specialist.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- GCS ≤ 13 or falling
- Unequal pupils
- Focal deficit
- Seizure
- Open/depressed skull fracture
- CSF leak
- Anticoagulant use
- Suspected cervical spine injury

## 8. Follow-up

- Observe 24 h for mild injury.
- CT/neurosurgical review.
- Post-concussion symptoms.
- Rehabilitation.
