# Quality Improvement and Audit

> **Source:** NHP; NHM quality assurance; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Quality improvement (QI) uses data to improve care.
- Audit compares practice with standards.
- Start with a small, measurable project.
- Involve the whole team.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Identify a problem (e.g., antibiotic misuse, missed follow-ups).
- Choose a standard/indicator.
- Collect baseline data.
- Analyze causes.

## 3. Treatment: QI cycle (PDSA)

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| Plan | Define change and measure |
| Do | Implement on small scale |
| Study | Analyze results |
| Act | Adopt/adapt/abandon |

### Example indicators

- % patients with BP recorded
- % viral fever given antibiotics (target low)
- % follow-ups completed
- Hand hygiene compliance

- Use **paracetamol 500 mg** only as an example in prescription audits.

## 4. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dose:** not applicable (non-drug topic).
- **Paracetamol 500 mg** example of audit criterion.
- **Bias:** ensure complete sampling.
- **Confidentiality:** anonymize patient data.

## 5. Supportive care

- Train staff.
- Display results.
- Celebrate improvements.
- Document changes.

## 6. Red flags

- Unsafe practice identified
- Repeated errors
- Poor documentation
- No follow-up on actions

## 7. Follow-up

- Re-audit after 3 months.
- Share learning.
- Sustain changes.
