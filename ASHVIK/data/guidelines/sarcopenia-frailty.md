# Sarcopenia and Frailty

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NPHCE
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Sarcopenia = loss of muscle mass and strength.
- Frailty = reduced reserve and increased vulnerability.
- Both increase falls, disability, and mortality.
- Exercise and nutrition are core.

## 2. Clinical features

- Weakness, slow gait
- Weight loss
- Exhaustion
- Low activity
- Frequent falls
- Poor recovery from illness

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Grip strength | Weakness |
| Gait speed | Slow |
| Chair stand | Lower limb strength |
| SARC-F | Screening |
| BMI/calf circumference | Muscle mass |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Resistance exercise | 2–3 times/week |
| Protein | 1.0–1.5 g/kg/day |
| Vitamin D | 800 IU daily |
| Calcium | 500 mg twice daily |
| Treat depression | Counselling/SSRI |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Shelcal; Uprise D3; Protinex.

## 5. Supportive care

- Falls prevention
- Physiotherapy
- Nutrition
- Social engagement

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Calcium/vitamin D:** as above.
- **Sedatives:** worsen frailty/falls.
- **Steroids:** muscle loss.
- **Statins:** myopathy (rare).

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Rapid weight loss
- New weakness
- Recurrent falls
- Suspected malignancy
- Acute illness

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess strength/function.
- Exercise programme.
- Nutrition review.
