# Hypothermia

> **Source:** WHO; ICMR; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Hypothermia = core temp < 35 °C.
- Elderly, homeless, and neonates are at risk.
- **Rewarm gently**; avoid rough handling.
- Severe hypothermia needs hospital rewarming.

## 2. Clinical features

- Shivering (mild), confusion
- Cold skin, bradycardia
- Slurred speech, drowsiness
- Coma, arrhythmia (severe)
- Frostbite (local)

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Core temperature | Severity |
| ECG | Osborn waves/arrhythmia |
| Glucose | Hypoglycaemia |
| Electrolytes | Derangement |

## 4. Treatment

| Severity | Action |
|---|---|
| Mild (32–35) | Warm clothes, shelter, warm drinks |
| Moderate (28–32) | Active external rewarming, monitor |
| Severe (< 28) | Hospital rewarming; avoid movement |
| Frostbite | Warm water 37–40 °C; do not rub |

**Paracetamol 500 mg** for associated pain (avoid if liver disease).

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Remove wet clothes
- Warm blankets
- Monitor vitals
- Treat hypoglycaemia

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Sedatives:** worsen hypothermia.
- **Alcohol:** increases heat loss.
- **Rapid rewarming:** arrhythmia risk.
- **Frostbite:** no rubbing/snow.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Core temp < 28 °C
- Altered consciousness
- Arrhythmia
- Severe bradycardia
- Frostbite with necrosis

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess temperature.
- Prevention advice.
- Social support.
