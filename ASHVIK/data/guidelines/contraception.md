# Contraception Counselling

> **Source:** National Family Planning Programme / MoHFW; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Offer a **method mix** and let the couple choose.
- Condoms protect against STI/HIV; hormonal methods do not.
- Screen for contraindications before starting hormonal methods.
- Emergency contraception is not a regular method.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

Assess age, parity, breastfeeding, smoking, BP, migraine history, VTE history, and drug interactions before choosing a method.

## 3. Treatment: Methods

| Method | Effectiveness | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Male condom | High with correct use | STI protection; no prescription |
| Combined OCP | High | Daily pill; not for smokers > 35 |
| Progestin-only pill | High | Breastfeeding-friendly |
| DMPA injection | High | Every 3 months; delayed return of fertility |
| IUCD (CuT 380A) | Very high | 10 years; inserted by trained provider |
| Implant | Very high | 3–5 years; provider-dependent |
| Sterilisation | Permanent | Informed consent; male/female |
| Emergency pill | Emergency only | Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg within 72 h |

## 4. Starting combined OCPs

- Take one tablet daily at the same time.
- Start on day 1–5 of menses (or anytime if not pregnant).
- Use condoms for the first 7 days.
- Missed pill: take as soon as remembered; use backup if > 24 h late.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Mala-N, Femilon.

## 5. Contraindications to combined OCPs

- Smoker > 35 years
- Migraine with aura
- History of VTE/DVT
- Breast cancer
- Severe liver disease
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Multiple cardiovascular risk factors

## 6. Emergency contraception

| Method | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Levonorgestrel | 1.5 mg single dose | Within 72 h (best < 24 h) |
| Copper IUCD | Insertion | Within 5 days |

**Brand example (illustrative):** i-pill.

## 7. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, topiramate:** reduce OCP efficacy — use backup or alternative method.
- **OCPs:** nausea, breast tenderness, breakthrough bleeding; small VTE risk; may reduce efficacy of lamotrigine.
- **DMPA:** weight gain, delayed fertility, bone density (reversible); not for unexplained vaginal bleeding.
- **IUCD:** cramping, heavier periods; check for expulsion; avoid in active PID.
- **Emergency pill:** nausea; not for regular use.

## 8. Red flags / refer

- Suspected pregnancy
- Abnormal uterine bleeding
- Severe pelvic pain after IUCD insertion
- DVT symptoms (leg swelling/pain)
- Breast lump
- Severe hypertension
- Sexual assault — emergency contraception + STI prophylaxis + counselling

## 9. Follow-up

- Review after 3 months for OCP/DMPA.
- Check IUCD threads after menses.
- Counsel on dual protection.
- Offer postpartum and post-abortion contraception.
