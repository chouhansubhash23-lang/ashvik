# Dengue

> **Source:** NVBDCP dengue guidelines; WHO Dengue Guidelines (S12)
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Vector: *Aedes aegypti* (day-biting, breeds in clean stagnant water).
- Peak: monsoon and post-monsoon (Jul–Nov).
- Four serotypes; second infection with a different serotype increases severe-dengue risk.
- No specific antiviral — management is supportive and early recognition of warning signs.

## 2. Clinical features

**Febrile phase (day 1–5)**
- High fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain
- Myalgia, arthralgia, rash
- Nausea, vomiting
- Mild bleeding (gum/nose) may occur

**Critical phase (day 4–7, around defervescence)**
- Plasma leakage → rising haematocrit, pleural effusion, ascites
- Warning signs appear

**Recovery phase (day 7–10)**
- Reabsorption of fluid, itching, bradycardia

## 3. Warning signs

- Abdominal pain or tenderness
- Persistent vomiting
- Mucosal bleeding (gums, nose, vomit, stool)
- Lethargy / restlessness
- Liver enlargement > 2 cm
- Rising haematocrit with falling platelets
- Fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion)

## 4. Diagnosis

| Test | When |
|---|---|
| NS1 antigen | Day 1–5 of fever |
| IgM ELISA | Day 5 onward |
| CBC + haematocrit | Daily monitoring in warning signs |
| Dengue RDT | Where ELISA unavailable (interpret cautiously) |

**Do not use platelet count alone to decide admission or transfusion.**

## 5. Treatment (supportive)

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fever | Paracetamol 500–650 mg (adult) every 6–8 h; max 4 g/day. **Avoid aspirin, ibuprofen, diclofenac, naproxen.** |
| Hydration | Oral fluids/ORS; IV fluids (0.9% saline or Ringer's lactate) if vomiting or warning signs |
| Monitoring | Haematocrit, platelets, urine output, BP, warning signs |
| Platelet transfusion | Only for significant bleeding, not for a low count alone |
| Avoid | IM injections, NSAIDs, steroids (unless indicated), unnecessary antibiotics |

**Brand example (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Dolo 650, Crocin, Calpol.

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Paracetamol:** hepatotoxic with alcohol or overdose; may increase INR with warfarin; max 4 g/day.
- **NSAIDs/aspirin:** increase bleeding risk — contraindicated in suspected dengue.
- **IV fluids:** overhydration can cause pulmonary oedema in the critical phase — titrate carefully.
- **Steroids:** not routine; may worsen outcome.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Shock (cold extremities, weak pulse, narrow pulse pressure, low BP)
- Respiratory distress / pulmonary oedema
- Severe bleeding
- Altered consciousness, seizures
- Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting
- Liver/kidney failure
- Pregnancy with dengue
- Infant < 1 year

## 8. Follow-up

- Daily review during critical phase (day 4–7).
- Repeat CBC/haematocrit as advised.
- Advise return immediately if warning signs appear.
