# Peptic Ulcer Disease and GERD

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); NHP; national H. pylori guidance
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- **GERD:** reflux of stomach contents causing heartburn/regurgitation.
- **Peptic ulcer:** gastric or duodenal ulcer; *H. pylori* and NSAIDs are major causes.
- **Alarm features** require endoscopy/referral.
- PPI is the mainstay; lifestyle changes are additive.

## 2. Clinical features

| Condition | Features |
|---|---|
| GERD | Heartburn, acid regurgitation, water brash, chronic cough, hoarseness |
| Gastric ulcer | Epigastric pain, worse with food, nausea, weight loss |
| Duodenal ulcer | Epigastric pain, relieved by food, night pain |
| H. pylori | Dyspepsia, ulcer, sometimes asymptomatic |
| Complications | Haematemesis, melaena, perforation, obstruction |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical assessment | Typical symptoms in young patients |
| H. pylori stool antigen / urea breath test | Confirm infection |
| Upper GI endoscopy | Alarm features, age > 45 with new dyspepsia, non-response |
| CBC | Anaemia from chronic bleed |
| Stool occult blood | GI bleeding |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 GERD / dyspepsia

| Drug | Brand example (illustrative) | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Omeprazole | Omez | 20 mg once daily | 4–8 weeks |
| Pantoprazole | Pan 40, Pantocid | 40 mg once daily | 4–8 weeks |
| Rabeprazole | Razo | 20 mg once daily | 4–8 weeks |
| Antacid (alginate) | Gelusil, Digene | As needed | Short term |

Take PPI 30–60 minutes before breakfast.

### 4.2 H. pylori eradication (14 days)

| Regimen | Drugs |
|---|---|
| Triple therapy | PPI (standard dose BD) + amoxicillin 1 g BD + clarithromycin 500 mg BD |
| Quadruple therapy (resistance/high prevalence) | PPI + bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline |

**Brand examples:** Amoxicillin — Mox; Clarithromycin — Claribid; Metronidazole — Flagyl, Metrogyl.

Confirm eradication with stool antigen/breath test 4 weeks after completing therapy (off PPI for 2 weeks).

### 4.3 NSAID-induced ulcer

- Stop NSAID if possible.
- PPI for 8 weeks.
- Consider misoprostol or PPI prophylaxis in high-risk patients.

## 5. Supportive care

- Small frequent meals; avoid late meals
- Avoid alcohol, smoking, spicy/fatty food, caffeine
- Weight loss if obese
- Raise head of bed for night reflux
- Avoid NSAIDs, aspirin (unless cardiac indication)

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **PPIs:** may reduce clopidogrel activation (especially omeprazole) — prefer pantoprazole with clopidogrel; long-term use → B12 deficiency, hypomagnesaemia, fracture risk.
- **Clarithromycin:** QT prolongation; interacts with statins (rhabdomyolysis), warfarin, carbamazepine.
- **Amoxicillin:** rash in infectious mononucleosis.
- **Metronidazole:** disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol; warfarin interaction.
- **Bismuth:** black stools/tongue (harmless); avoid in renal failure.
- **Antacids:** reduce absorption of tetracyclines, quinolones, iron, levothyroxine — separate by 2 hours.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Haematemesis / melaena
- Severe abdominal pain with rigidity (perforation)
- Persistent vomiting / obstruction
- Dysphagia, odynophagia
- Unexplained weight loss, anaemia
- New dyspepsia at age > 45
- Family history of gastric cancer
- No response to PPI after 4 weeks

## 8. Follow-up

- Review in 4 weeks.
- Confirm H. pylori eradication if treated.
- Endoscopy referral if alarm features or non-response.
- Reinforce lifestyle and medication adherence.
