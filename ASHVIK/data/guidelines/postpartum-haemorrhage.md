# Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH)

> **Source:** RMNCH+A / NHM; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- PPH = ≥ 500 mL blood loss after vaginal delivery or ≥ 1000 mL after caesarean.
- Leading cause of maternal death.
- **Uterine atony is the commonest cause.**
- Act fast: massage, oxytocin, fluids, refer.

## 2. Clinical features

- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Tachycardia, hypotension
- Pallor
- Uterus soft/atonic
- Retained placenta/trauma

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Blood loss | Visual/weighing |
| BP/pulse | Shock |
| Hb | Baseline |
| Coagulation | If suspected |
| Ultrasound | Retained products |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Call for help; ABC |
| 2 | Uterine massage |
| 3 | Oxytocin 10 IU IM/IV |
| 4 | IV fluids |
| 5 | Misoprostol 800 mcg sublingual |
| 6 | Tranexamic acid 1 g IV |
| 7 | Refer for surgical management |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Oxytocin — Pitocin; Misoprostol — Cytotec; Texid.

## 5. Supportive care

- Warm
- Monitor urine output
- Blood transfusion if needed
- Treat cause (retained placenta/trauma)

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Oxytocin:** hypotension with rapid IV.
- **Misoprostol:** fever, shivering.
- **Tranexamic acid:** thrombosis risk.
- **Ergometrine:** contraindicated in hypertension.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Shock
- Continued bleeding
- Retained placenta
- Coagulopathy
- Uterine rupture

## 8. Follow-up

- Hb check.
- Iron therapy.
- Contraception counselling.
