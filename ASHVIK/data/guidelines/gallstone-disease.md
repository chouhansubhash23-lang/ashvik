# Gallstone Disease (Biliary Colic, Cholecystitis, Cholangitis)

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); Tokyo Guidelines; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Gallstones are common, especially in women, obesity, age > 40.
- Biliary colic = intermittent RUQ pain after fatty meals.
- Cholecystitis = persistent pain + fever + Murphy's sign.
- Cholangitis = fever + jaundice + RUQ pain (Charcot's triad) — emergency.

## 2. Clinical features

| Condition | Features |
|---|---|
| Biliary colic | RUQ/epigastric pain, radiating to back, nausea, triggered by fatty food |
| Acute cholecystitis | Persistent pain, fever, Murphy's sign, leucocytosis |
| Cholangitis | Fever, jaundice, RUQ pain, confusion, hypotension |
| Pancreatitis | Epigastric pain radiating to back, vomiting |

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Ultrasound abdomen | Gallstones, wall thickening, duct dilatation |
| CBC, CRP | Infection |
| LFT, bilirubin, ALP | Cholestasis |
| Amylase/lipase | Pancreatitis |
| MRCP/ERCP | Duct stones (referral) |

## 4. Treatment

### 4.1 Biliary colic

- Analgesia: NSAID (diclofenac) or antispasmodic.
- Avoid fatty meals.
- Elective cholecystectomy referral.

### 4.2 Acute cholecystitis

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nil by mouth | Yes |
| IV fluids | Yes |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol/opioid |
| Antibiotics | Ceftriaxone 1 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV |
| Surgery | Refer for cholecystectomy |

### 4.3 Cholangitis

- **Emergency referral** for ERCP/drainage.
- IV fluids, antibiotics, monitor.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Metronidazole — Flagyl; Diclofenac — Voveran.

## 5. Supportive care

- Hydration
- Antiemetics
- Monitor vitals
- Low-fat diet after recovery

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **NSAIDs:** GI bleed, kidney injury; avoid in dehydration.
- **Opioids:** may cause sphincter spasm; use judiciously.
- **Antibiotics:** renal adjustment; *C. difficile*.
- **Antispasmodics:** anticholinergic effects.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Cholangitis (fever, jaundice, confusion, hypotension)
- Severe pain with peritonitis
- Gallstone pancreatitis
- Empyema/perforation
- Pregnancy with cholecystitis
- Obstructive jaundice

## 8. Follow-up

- Surgical review after acute episode.
- Low-fat diet.
- Recurrence counselling.
