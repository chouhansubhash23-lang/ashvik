# Telemedicine Basics

> **Source:** Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (India); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Telemedicine extends care to remote areas.
- Follow the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines and consent requirements.
- Know when teleconsultation is unsafe — refer in person.
- Maintain records and confidentiality.

## 2. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Confirm patient identity.
- Assess symptoms and vitals (if available).
- Determine if teleconsultation is appropriate.
- Identify red flags requiring in-person care.

## 3. Treatment: teleconsultation

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce yourself and confirm identity |
| 2 | Obtain consent |
| 3 | Take history |
| 4 | Assess red flags |
| 5 | Advise/refer |
| 6 | Document |

- Prescribe only when safe; follow e-prescription rules.
- Example: paracetamol 500 mg for mild fever.
- Advise follow-up.

## 4. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Dose:** not applicable (non-drug topic).
- **Paracetamol 500 mg** example of a safe tele-prescription.
- **Opioids/sedatives:** avoid tele-prescribing.
- **Emergency:** advise immediate in-person care.

## 5. Supportive care

- Use secure platform.
- Ensure privacy.
- Provide written advice.
- Link to local ASHA/PHC.

## 6. Red flags

- Chest pain/breathlessness
- Altered consciousness
- Severe bleeding
- Pregnancy emergencies
- Suicidal ideation
- Inability to assess

## 7. Follow-up

- Document consultation.
- Follow-up call/visit.
- Audit prescriptions.
