# Risk Communication

> **Source:** WHO; NCDC; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Communicate clearly, honestly, and early.
- Use local language and trusted messengers.
- Address uncertainty and rumours.
- Coordinate with health authorities.

## 2. Principles

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| Trust | Honest, consistent messages |
| Timing | Early and regular updates |
| Audience | Tailored to community |
| Channels | Local media, ASHA, panchayat |
| Feedback | Listen and respond |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Identify audience
- Assess risk perception
- Map trusted channels
- Monitor rumours

## 4. Treatment / response

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| Key messages | Prevention, symptoms, where to seek care |
| Spokesperson | Trained, credible |
| Materials | Posters, audio, social media |
| Paracetamol 500 mg | Example of self-care message |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Dolo 650; ORS.

## 5. Supportive care

- Community engagement
- Stigma reduction
- Mental health support

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Misinformation:** correct quickly.
- **Fear:** avoid alarmist language.
- **Consent:** protect privacy.
- **Cultural sensitivity:** essential.

## 7. Red flags — escalate

- Rumours causing harm
- Vaccine hesitancy
- Stigma/violence
- Media crisis

## 8. Follow-up

- Evaluate reach/understanding.
- Update messages.
- Document.
