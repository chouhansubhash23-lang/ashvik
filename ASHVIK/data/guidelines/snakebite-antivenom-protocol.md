# Snakebite Antivenom Protocol

> **Source:** National Snakebite Protocol; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Antivenom is life-saving for systemic envenomation.
- Give only when indicated — not for dry bites.
- Watch for anaphylaxis.
- Do not use tourniquets, cuts, or suction.

## 2. Indications for antivenom

- Neurotoxicity (ptosis, dysphagia, respiratory weakness)
- Coagulopathy/bleeding (viper)
- Severe local swelling
- Shock
- Rising creatinine

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| 20-min whole blood clotting test | Viper envenomation |
| Neuro exam | Cobra/krait |
| CBC, creatinine, electrolytes | Systemic effects |
| ECG | Arrhythmia |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Immobilise limb; pressure bandage for neurotoxic |
| 2 | IV access; monitor |
| 3 | Antivenom as per protocol (polyvalent) |
| 4 | Treat anaphylaxis (adrenaline 0.5 mg IM) |
| 5 | Tetanus prophylaxis |
| 6 | Refer for severe cases |

**Antivenom dose:** as per national protocol (often 10 vials initial; repeat based on response).
**Adrenaline:** 0.5 mg IM for anaphylaxis.
**Paracetamol 500 mg** for pain.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Polyvalent ASV; Adrenaline; Tetanus toxoid.

## 5. Supportive care

- Airway/breathing support
- IV fluids
- Monitor urine output
- Dialysis for AKI

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **ASV:** anaphylaxis, serum sickness; keep adrenaline ready.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid if coagulopathy.
- **Sedatives:** avoid respiratory depression.
- **Tourniquet:** contraindicated.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Respiratory weakness
- Bleeding/coagulopathy
- Shock
- AKI
- Anaphylaxis to ASV
- Children/elderly

## 8. Follow-up

- Observe 24 h.
- Repeat clotting test.
- Physiotherapy for weakness.
