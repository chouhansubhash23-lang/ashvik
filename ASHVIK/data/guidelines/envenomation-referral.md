# Envenomation Referral

> **Source:** National Snakebite Protocol; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Early referral to an antivenom centre saves lives.
- Stabilise airway/breathing/circulation first.
- Communicate bite details and treatment given.
- Do not delay transport for tests.

## 2. Clinical features

- Neurotoxicity (ptosis, weakness)
- Coagulopathy/bleeding
- Local swelling
- Shock
- Scorpion autonomic symptoms

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| 20-min clotting test | Viper |
| Neuro exam | Cobra/krait |
| Vitals/SpO2 | Severity |
| ECG | Scorpion |

## 4. Treatment

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Immobilise limb |
| 2 | IV access |
| 3 | Antivenom if available |
| 4 | Treat anaphylaxis (adrenaline 0.5 mg IM) |
| 5 | Refer with note |

**Paracetamol 500 mg** for pain.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Polyvalent ASV; Adrenaline; Dolo 650.

## 5. Supportive care

- Oxygen
- IV fluids
- Monitor urine output
- Keep warm

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **ASV:** anaphylaxis; keep adrenaline ready.
- **NSAIDs:** bleeding.
- **Sedatives:** respiratory depression.
- **Tourniquet:** avoid.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Breathing difficulty
- Bleeding
- Shock
- AKI
- Altered consciousness
- Paediatric/elderly

## 8. Follow-up

- Observe 24 h.
- Repeat clotting test.
- Rehabilitation.
