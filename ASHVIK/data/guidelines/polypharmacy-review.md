# Polypharmacy Review

> **Source:** NPHCE; ICMR; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Polypharmacy = ≥ 5 medicines, or unnecessary medicines.
- Older adults are at high risk of adverse drug reactions.
- Review every medicine for indication, dose, and interactions.
- Deprescribe cautiously with monitoring.

## 2. Clinical features

- Falls, confusion
- Dizziness, postural hypotension
- GI bleeding
- Renal impairment
- Fatigue, anorexia

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| Medication list | All drugs incl. OTC/herbal |
| Beers criteria | Potentially inappropriate drugs |
| Renal function | Dose adjustment |
| Adherence | Missed/duplicate doses |
| Drug interactions | Checker |

## 4. Treatment

| Action | Example |
|---|---|
| Stop unnecessary | Vitamins without deficiency |
| Simplify dosing | Once-daily formulations |
| Switch safer | Avoid benzodiazepines |
| Monitor | BP, sugar, renal |

- **Paracetamol 500 mg** preferred over NSAIDs for pain.
- **Vitamin D 800 IU** if deficient.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Paracetamol — Dolo 650; Uprise D3.

## 5. Supportive care

- Pill box
- Caregiver education
- Pharmacy review
- Written medication list

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **NSAIDs + ACEi/ARB + diuretic:** kidney injury.
- **Warfarin + antibiotics:** bleeding.
- **Benzodiazepines:** falls/confusion.
- **Anticholinergics:** confusion.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Suspected adverse drug reaction
- GI bleeding
- Severe hypoglycaemia
- Renal failure
- Delirium

## 8. Follow-up

- Review after any change.
- Monitor labs.
- Reassess adherence.
