# Blood Transfusion — Basics

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); National Blood Transfusion Council; WHO
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Transfusion is life-saving but has risks; use only when indicated.
- **Crossmatch and consent** are mandatory.
- Monitor the patient during and after transfusion.
- Report adverse reactions immediately.

## 2. Indications

| Component | Indication |
|---|---|
| Packed red cells | Symptomatic anaemia (Hb < 7 g/dL), active bleeding |
| Platelets | Severe thrombocytopenia with bleeding |
| FFP | Coagulopathy with bleeding |
| Whole blood | Massive haemorrhage (where components unavailable) |

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

- Hb/haematocrit
- Blood group and crossmatch
- Coagulation profile
- Consent
- Vital signs baseline

## 4. Treatment: transfusion

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Verify patient ID, blood group, unit number |
| 2 | Baseline vitals |
| 3 | Start slowly (first 15 min) |
| 4 | Monitor vitals every 15 min |
| 5 | Complete within 4 h |
| 6 | Document |

**Premedication** (paracetamol 500 mg) only if prior febrile reaction, as advised.

## 5. Supportive care

- Keep IV access patent
- Monitor for reactions
- Hydration
- Iron/folate after transfusion if deficient

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **No drugs in the blood bag.**
- **Calcium:** separate from blood (citrate binding) if massive transfusion.
- **Diuretics:** caution in heart failure.
- **Anticoagulants:** bleeding risk.

## 7. Red flags — stop transfusion

- Fever/chills
- Rash/urticaria
- Breathlessness/wheeze
- Hypotension
- Dark urine
- Back pain
- Circulatory overload (crackles, raised JVP)

## 8. Follow-up

- Recheck Hb after 24 h.
- Investigate cause of anaemia.
- Document reaction if any.
