# Acute Appendicitis

> **Source:** ICMR STW Vol. 3 (2022); WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-27
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Common surgical emergency; peak in adolescents/young adults.
- Pain migrates from umbilicus to right iliac fossa.
- **Refer early** — perforation increases morbidity.
- Avoid laxatives and enemas.

## 2. Clinical features

- Periumbilical pain → RIF
- Anorexia, nausea, vomiting
- Low-grade fever
- Tenderness at McBurney's point
- Rebound tenderness, guarding
- Rovsing's/psoas/obturator signs
- Atypical in pregnancy/elderly

## 3. Diagnosis

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Clinical (Alvarado score) | Risk stratification |
| CBC | Leucocytosis |
| Urine dipstick | Exclude UTI |
| Ultrasound | Appendix, abscess |
| CT | If equivocal (if available) |
| Pregnancy test | All women of reproductive age |

## 4. Treatment

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nil by mouth | Yes |
| IV fluids | 0.9% saline |
| Analgesia | Paracetamol/opioid |
| Antibiotics | Ceftriaxone 1 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV (pre-op) |
| Surgery | Appendectomy — refer |

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Ceftriaxone — Monocef; Metronidazole — Flagyl.

**Do not give laxatives/enemas.**

## 5. Supportive care

- Monitor vitals
- Urine output
- Keep patient comfortable
- Arrange urgent transport

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Opioids:** may mask pain but do not delay surgery; monitor breathing.
- **NSAIDs:** avoid if dehydration/kidney injury.
- **Antibiotics:** allergy; renal adjustment.
- **Laxatives:** contraindicated.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Generalised peritonitis
- Shock/sepsis
- Suspected perforation (free gas)
- Appendix mass/abscess
- Pregnancy with appendicitis
- Children/elderly with atypical presentation

## 8. Follow-up

- Post-appendectomy wound review.
- Histopathology.
- Return to activity as advised.
