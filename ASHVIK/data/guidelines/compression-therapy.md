# Compression Therapy

> **Source:** ICMR; WHO; NHP
> **Evidence:** `../../evidence/sources.md`
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-28
> **Status:** Draft — clinician review required
> **Setting:** Rural OPD / PHC (India)

## 1. Quick facts

- Indicated for venous ulcers, lymphoedema, and DVT prophylaxis.
- **Do not use in arterial disease** without assessment.
- Measure and fit correctly.
- Check skin and circulation.

## 2. Clinical features

- Leg swelling
- Venous ulcers
- Varicose veins
- Post-thrombotic syndrome
- Lymphoedema

## 3. Diagnosis / Assessment

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| ABPI | Exclude arterial disease |
| Ultrasound | DVT/venous reflux |
| Skin check | Infection/ulcer |
| Limb circumference | Fitting |

## 4. Treatment

| Condition | Compression |
|---|---|
| Venous ulcer | Multi-layer bandage (30–40 mmHg) |
| DVT prophylaxis | Graduated compression stockings |
| Lymphoedema | Bandaging/garments |
| Varicose veins | Stockings |

- **Paracetamol 500 mg** for associated pain.
- Elevate leg when possible.

**Brand examples (illustrative):** Compression stockings; Multilayer bandage.

## 5. Supportive care

- Leg elevation
- Exercise
- Skin care
- Wound care

## 6. Drug interactions and cautions

- **Arterial disease:** avoid strong compression.
- **Heart failure:** caution with fluids.
- **Infection:** treat before compression.
- **Diabetes:** check skin.

## 7. Red flags — refer immediately

- Rest pain/gangrene
- Sudden severe swelling (DVT)
- Spreading infection
- Compartment syndrome
- Ulcer with exposed tendon/bone

## 8. Follow-up

- Reassess ABPI.
- Check fitting.
- Wound review.
